# Serum Packaging, Size, and Supply-Chain Benchmark

- **Research ticket:** [#253 — Benchmark serum sizes, packaging, and supply chains](https://github.com/brandon-y-lee/helix/issues/253)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-31
- **Decision served:** choose defensible Mini and Full Size bands for the helix Core serum and define a sourceable frosted-glass dropper system without treating the supplied Haramoon reference as a transferable design.
- **Market:** United States direct-to-consumer. Benchmark prices are current U.S. list prices where the brand publishes them; non-U.S. facts are labeled.

> **Research recommendation, not a purchase specification.** Public Product pages establish fill, price, directions, and sometimes broad packaging materials. They rarely disclose dropper output, bottle weight, annealing, brimful capacity, elastomer grade, coating chemistry, neck finish, pipette geometry, closure displacement, evacuation, breakage, master-case configuration, filler, or packaging manufacturer. No relationship, material, performance, cost, or manufacturing route is inferred from visual resemblance. Final selection requires the exact formula, signed component drawings and bills of material, samples from the production tool, measured dosing/evacuation, compatibility and transit testing, declarations, and comparable quotations at helix quantities.

## Executive decision

Carry four capacity scenarios into formula-and-package testing, but center sourcing on this launch pair:

- **Mini design target: 10 mL / approximately 0.34 U.S. fl oz.** At two uses per day it spans 14–33 nominal days across a 0.15–0.35 mL per-use sensitivity range. It best expresses a true 14–30-day trial, limits glass and formula cash, and is well below TSA's 3.4 oz-per-container carry-on limit for liquids, gels, creams, and pastes. It is not yet proven in the desired classic frosted-glass/silver-collar/black-bulb stock architecture. [TSA 3-1-1 rule](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/factsheets/tsa-travel-tips)
- **Mini stock fallback: 15 mL / approximately 0.50 U.S. fl oz.** This capacity is present in current serum commerce and in public dropper catalogues. It reaches 14–30 days only when measured use is about 0.25–0.54 mL twice daily; at a low 0.15 mL dose it lasts about 50 days. Choose it if 10 mL creates poor label area, pipette clearance, fill efficiency, MOQ, or excessive component cost.
- **Full Size design target: 30 mL / approximately 1.0 U.S. fl oz.** Thirty milliliters is the strongest cross-segment norm in the 17-Product panel and the best-supported stock glass/dropper platform. It reaches 60–90 nominal days at a controlled 0.17–0.25 mL per-use dose twice daily, before residual. That makes measured delivery—not capacity alone—the central specification.
- **Full Size high-dose fallback: 50 mL / approximately 1.69 U.S. fl oz.** This matches the supplied 50 mL Haramoon reference and supports 60–90 days when actual use is roughly 0.28–0.42 mL twice daily. It adds formula, glass mass, freight, cube, residual, and breakage exposure. It should advance only if a home-use/dose study shows 30 mL cannot honestly serve 60–90 days.

For the primary package, start with a **differentiated stock round bottle** in two tested finish tracks—frosted/translucent clear and dark amber—with a silver metal-look collar, black bulb, compatible gasket/wiper, and glass pipette. Quote a softened-square or rectangular stock bottle as a visibly differentiated option, not as the default. The supplied Haramoon composition—broad rounded-square amber/frosted body, low shoulder, oversized silver collar, black bulb, and dense white graphics—is an inspiration reference; copying the combination closely carries design-rights risk. The companion design screen recommends stock round as the lower-risk first concept and requires meaningful changes to silhouette, proportions, shoulders, base, closure, and graphics if a square route advances. [Design-rights screen](./reference-packaging-design-rights-screen.md)

Do not select amber because it looks clinical or clear frost because it looks premium. The final formula and its light-stability data decide whether amber glass, an opaque coating, another barrier, or no special light barrier is necessary. Decorative frosting is not evidence of UV protection.

Three exact, first-party supplier relationships were found and are useful precisely because most brands do not publish theirs:

- **Aptar Beauty supplies Dermalogica FutureCode Booster's NeoDropper Autoload system.** [Aptar](https://www.aptar.com/en-us/news-events/aptar-beauty-launches-next-generation-auto-loading-dropper-technology-for-easier-more-accurate-skincare-dosing)
- **Quadpack supplied Chantecaille's 30 mL 24K Gold Serum Intense Skin-Up bottle/dropper.** [Quadpack](https://www.quadpack.com/about-us/news/14154183/the-gold-standard-in-beauty-packaging/)
- **ICONS/ICS identifies its 30 mL molded-glass bottle, metalized PP collar, and dropper applicator for Womaness Fountain of Glow.** This is a relevant adjacent serum relationship, not evidence that ICONS supplies any helix benchmark brand. [ICONS America](https://icons-america.com/ics-featured-in-global-cosmetic-industry-sustainable-packaging-solutions-story-september-2021-issue/)

Boox names Rhode at brand level but does not identify the Peptide Glazing Fluid, a particular shipper, dates, or scope. No first-party relationship was found for Anua, Aesop, Haramoon, or the other benchmark packages. Toly Korea remains an unverified lead. Supplier silence is an explicit diligence gap, not permission to infer.

## Scope and evidence rules

The panel covers **17 current facial serums from 17 distinct brands**: four indie, four K-beauty, four masstige, four luxury, plus the required supplied Haramoon reference. It intentionally includes traditional bulb/pipette droppers, auto-loading droppers, pumps, and airless alternatives because the business decision is a package system, not merely a bottle silhouette.

Evidence is classified as:

- **Verified Product fact:** the brand or named supplier publishes the fact for the exact Product or relationship.
- **Verified brand-level fact:** a first party names the brand but not the exact Product, component, service, or period.
- **Supported inference:** a calculation or procurement implication from verified facts, with assumptions shown.
- **Unverified lead:** a third-party record, visual resemblance, or proposed relationship that needs direct confirmation.
- **Unknown:** the reviewed public evidence does not establish the fact. Unknown does not mean absent.

Directions such as “drops,” “pipette,” “pump,” “appropriate amount,” and “one dropper” are recorded verbatim as use units, not converted to milliliters unless the dispenser output is published for that exact package. Retail price per milliliter is customer-value context, not component-cost evidence. Product imagery may establish a visible format or color treatment; it does not establish glass, resin, coating, metal, supplier, or performance.

## What the benchmark distribution says

### Fill architecture

Thirty milliliters appears in **12 of the 16 non-Haramoon benchmark brands' current ranges** when all published size variants are counted: Dieux, Glossier, Anua, Beauty of Joseon, SKIN1004, The Ordinary, e.l.f., La Roche-Posay, Dermalogica, SkinCeuticals, Chantecaille, and Estée Lauder. It is the only capacity repeated across indie, K-beauty, masstige, and luxury.

The smaller commercial fills are 15 mL at Rhode and 20 mL at Estée Lauder. The panel does not prove a current 10 mL classic-dropper serum, so 10 mL is a **service-life-derived design target**, not a market-mode claim. Fifteen milliliters is the better-evidenced stock/commercial fallback.

Larger fills cluster at 45–60 mL, with value or luxury extensions at 70, 100, and 115 mL. The supplied Haramoon is 50 mL; that places it in an established larger-serum range but does not prove that its physical package, dose, or service life is optimal.

TSA's 3-1-1 rule limits ordinary carry-on liquids, gels, creams, and pastes to **3.4 oz or less per container** in a one-quart clear bag. All four helix capacity scenarios—10, 15, 30, and 50 mL—sit below that container threshold. “Travel” therefore does not distinguish them by aviation eligibility; it distinguishes trial duration, glass mass, cube, label usability, and customer value. The final screening decision remains with TSA. [TSA travel tips](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/factsheets/tsa-travel-tips)

### Retail price per mL cannot reveal package cost

Representative list-price arithmetic spans about **$0.43/mL** for e.l.f. 30 mL to **$15/mL** for Chantecaille 30 mL. Anua is about $0.73/mL; Rhode Big about $0.64/mL; Glossier about $1.13/mL; Dermalogica about $2.97/mL; SkinCeuticals about $6.17/mL. These calculations include formula, brand, channel, margin, acquisition, distribution, geography, and volume. They cannot isolate the bottle/dropper, decoration, carton, filler, or freight.

Small formats predictably command a higher retail price per milliliter. Rhode's Little is about $1.33/mL versus about $0.64/mL for Big. This does not mean the Mini package costs twice as much; it means a second component set, fill/setup, quality release, artwork, inventory position, pick, and fulfillment touch are spread across much less formula.

## Dose and service-life model

### Why competitor directions are not enough

The panel includes:

- **2–3 drops:** Anua and Beauty of Joseon;
- **3–4 drops:** Glossier and La Roche-Posay;
- **3–5 or 4–5 drops:** e.l.f. and SkinCeuticals;
- **one dropper or one to two pipettes:** Estée Lauder and Aesop;
- **one auto-loaded dose:** Dermalogica;
- **one to three pumps:** the pump comparators; and
- **appropriate amount:** Torriden, SKIN1004, and Haramoon.

None publishes a directly comparable milliliter-per-use value for the exact retail package. Drop size varies with pipette tip, formula density/viscosity/surface tension, squeeze/release, aspiration depth, hold angle, and user technique. A “full dropper” may not mean a pipette physically filled to the top. Pump and auto-load counts are likewise meaningless volumetrically without measured output.

Virospack's public standard-range sheet is useful only as a hardware sensitivity anchor: different standard droppers list nominal aspirated volumes around **0.35, 0.40, and 0.65 mL**, depending on assembly. Those values are not Product use doses and must not be assigned to Aesop, Anua, Haramoon, or any other brand. [Virospack standard range](https://virospack.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/VIROSPACK_STANDARD_RANGE_DROPPER_3.pdf)

### Nominal days before residual

The transparent formula is:

`nominal days = declared fill ÷ (measured mL per use × uses per day)`

The following twice-daily sensitivity table deliberately spans a small drop-count dose through a substantial aspiration. It excludes residual, priming loss, spills, overfill, underfill tolerance, and skipped uses.

| Nominal fill | Approx. U.S. fluid ounces | 0.15 mL/use BID | 0.20 mL/use BID | 0.25 mL/use BID | 0.35 mL/use BID | 0.50 mL/use BID |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **10 mL** | 0.34 | 33 days | **25 days** | **20 days** | **14 days** | 10 days |
| **15 mL** | 0.51 | 50 days | 38 days | **30 days** | **21 days** | **15 days** |
| **30 mL** | 1.01 | 100 days | **75 days** | **60 days** | 43 days | 30 days |
| **50 mL** | 1.69 | 167 days | 125 days | 100 days | **71 days** | **50 days** |
| **60 mL** | 2.03 | 200 days | 150 days | 120 days | **86 days** | **60 days** |

Bold values fall within the relevant 14–30-day Mini or 60–90-day Full brief. Fluid-ounce equivalents are engineering conversions; final U.S./SI declaration must be reviewed on the actual label. NIST gives approximately 29.57 mL per U.S. fluid ounce. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/metric-si/unit-conversion/approximate-conversions-us-customary-measures-metric)

For once-daily Haramoon-like instructions, the denominator halves. A 50 mL fill at 0.35–0.65 mL per evening would last about **77–143 nominal days** before residual. That supported calculation shows why a 50 mL reference cannot establish a 60–90-day helix fill without its actual use dose.

### Design-dose implication

To support a truthful twice-daily claim before residual:

- a 30 mL Full needs an average delivered/used dose of about **0.17 mL per use for 90 days** or **0.25 mL per use for 60 days**;
- a 50 mL Full needs about **0.28–0.42 mL per use** for 90–60 days;
- a 10 mL Mini needs about **0.17–0.36 mL per use** for 30–14 days; and
- a 15 mL Mini needs about **0.25–0.54 mL per use** for 30–14 days.

This makes **0.20–0.25 mL per use** the initial 30 mL Full design-dose window if twice-daily use is expected. It is a target to validate with formula efficacy, sensory, coverage, and user behavior—not a package engineer's authority to reduce the dermatologically appropriate dose.

### Required dose study

Before locking fill or making a duration claim:

1. Define intended treated area: face only or face/neck; state once- versus twice-daily directions.
2. Establish formula mass per clinically/sensorially appropriate use, then convert with measured density.
3. Test at least three component lots and multiple filled units at early-, middle-, and late-fill levels. Include expected upright/storage orientations.
4. Measure delivered mass for a standardized full bulb actuation, partial consumer-like actuation, stated drop count, and ordinary home use. Record mean, standard deviation, coefficient of variation, P10/P50/P90, misloads, bubbles, drips, and exterior mess.
5. Repeat after compatibility aging and thermal/transit conditioning; elastomer set, bulb recovery, viscosity drift, frosting/coating damage, or wiper drag can change output.
6. Run a blinded home-use study long enough to capture dose learning, one-handed handling, drop counting, bathroom use, spills, remaining-Product perception, and days to consumer-declared empty.
7. Base any 14–30 or 60–90-day statement on **usable** fill after residual, not nominal capacity.

## Primary package architecture

### A dropper is an assembly, not one component

A classic system can include bottle, neck/finish, bulb/teat, collar/shell, inner cap, gasket or liner, optional wiper, pipette/rod, tip geometry, decoration, and tamper feature. Quadpack's published Skin-Up traditional dropper, for example, specifies perfumery-quality glass, a silicone teat, plastic or aluminum collar, glass pipette, optional LDPE wiper, and alternative NBR/TPR materials. Its push-button range spans **15, 30, and 50 mL** with glass and PP/aluminum closure options. [Traditional Skin-Up](https://catalogue.quadpack.com/product/14019234/skin-up-bottle-with-dropper) · [Push-button Skin-Up](https://catalogue.quadpack.com/product/14294448/skin-up-push-button-dropper)

Virospack publicly offers molded glass at **15, 30, and 50 mL**, tubular glass at smaller capacities, amber/tinted options for sensitive formulas, and classic/push-button/eco droppers. Its standard range includes shiny silver shells and multiple bulb/pipette configurations. This verifies capability, not availability at helix's 1,000–3,000 primary-container target. [Virospack](https://virospack.com/personal-care-packaging/) · [Standard range](https://virospack.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/VIROSPACK_STANDARD_RANGE_DROPPER_3.pdf)

ICONS America publishes molded and tubular glass capability in round, rounded-square, square, and other profiles with droppers and closure decoration. Its public materials show 15 and 30 mL platforms. Its Womaness case demonstrates a silver-looking **metalized PP collar**, which is visually relevant and materially different from an aluminum shell. [ICONS glass](https://icons-america.com/portfolio/glass/) · [ICONS catalogue](https://icons-america.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/ICONS-America-Glass-Packaging-0825.pdf.pdf)

For every offer, require one system-level drawing and BOM. “Glass bottle + silver dropper” is not comparable when one quote uses aluminum shell/silicone bulb/glass pipette/wiper and another uses metalized PP/NBR/plastic rod/no wiper.

### Round versus square or rectangular

**Round stock is the launch default** because it is broadly represented across public 15/30/50 mL platforms, typically uses less glass and parcel cube than a broad slab at equal capacity, is less dependent on label-panel registration, and creates more visible distance from the Haramoon reference. These are procurement hypotheses until actual drawings, weights, and case packs are compared.

Square and softened-rectangular stock forms do exist. ICONS publishes rounded-square and square glass capability; Quadpack's Charismo materials document a square dropper architecture. Their existence does not establish a 10/30 or 15/30 mL pair with the exact finish, neck, collar, pipette, MOQ, origin, or rights helix needs. [ICONS glass](https://icons-america.com/portfolio/glass/) · [Quadpack Charismo](https://www.quadpack.com/packbase/11590059/13599613-JHNMVOJZ/f/QP%20Charismo%20Dropper%20PR.pdf)

If a square option advances, require a design-rights checkpoint and meaningfully change the total impression: narrower/taller proportion, different corner radius, shoulder and base, smaller collar, altered bulb silhouette, and helix's sparse graphic system. Do not recreate the Haramoon broad slab and graphic density with only a new logo.

### Frosted clear versus dark amber

Run two evidence-based finish tracks:

| Track | Potential value | Decision-critical risks and evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Frosted/translucent clear glass** | Shows fill level, supports the supplied milky/frosted cream-family language, and can look clean/editorial. | Formula may need light protection; exterior acid etch, organic spray, powder coat, or other treatment has different rub, chemical, recovery, and supplier implications. Require process, substances/coating mass, transmission, adhesion, rub, shower/humidity, formula-contact boundary, and recyclability evidence. |
| **Dark amber glass or amber treatment** | May provide light attenuation and directly references apothecary/clinical cues. | “Amber-looking” can be furnace-colored glass, coating, or decoration. Require wavelength-resolved transmission on the finished pack and formula stability in the actual system. Dark glass obscures residual/fill level and can complicate color inspection. Amber is not automatically needed or sustainable. |

Virospack describes a satin soft-touch decoration route for tubular and molded glass, but its aesthetic claim is not performance proof. Require exact coating chemistry and tests. [Virospack soft-touch](https://virospack.com/soft-touch-beyond-sight/)

### Silver collar and black bulb

“Silver metal base” is an appearance brief, not a material specification. Quote at least:

- metalized PP collar;
- aluminum shell over an inner closure; and
- unmetalized or lower-decoration alternatives for cost/sustainability comparison.

Require the complete mass and material split, decoration process, recycled-content evidence, torque path, gasket/liner, disassembly, and recovery claim. A metal shell over plastic can add weight and separation complexity without functional benefit.

Black bulb options may include silicone, NBR, TPR/TPE, or another elastomer. Color alone does not establish compatibility. Require grade, colorant, extractables/odor/taint information where appropriate, formula-contact assessment, compression set, rebound after aging, aspiration output, leak performance, and supplier change control.

### Dose repeatability

The desired classic bulb/pipette system provides recognizable ritual but is user-variable. Key controls include:

- bulb geometry and recovery;
- aspiration volume and time;
- pipette inner diameter, length, straightness, tip, and end clearance;
- formula viscosity, density, surface tension, particulates, and air entrainment;
- neck/gasket seal and wiper interference;
- fill height and bottle orientation; and
- whether directions say drops, partial squeeze, or full actuation.

Automatic-load systems provide useful comparators. Aptar's NeoDropper Autoload claims a consistent auto-loaded dose with sealed bottle/short applicator; Quadpack's Chantecaille system auto-loads a controlled single dose. Neither public case publishes a milliliter output or helix-compatible MOQ, so both require samples and direct specification. [Aptar NeoDropper Autoload](https://www.aptar.com/en-us/products/neodropper-autoload-auto-loading-dropper-packaging) · [Quadpack relationship](https://www.quadpack.com/about-us/news/14154183/the-gold-standard-in-beauty-packaging/)

Aptar Serumony demonstrates another alternative: 10/15 mL airless “dropper” packaging with a published **70 µL dose**, and a 10 mL refill variant with claimed 90% restitution. It does not match the classic Haramoon aesthetic, but it proves a small-format controlled-dose fallback exists if repeatability, protection, or evacuation outranks ritual. Supplier claims require independent validation. [Serumony](https://www.aptar.com/en-us/products/serumony-airless-dropper-packaging) · [Serumony refill](https://www.aptar.com/en-us/products/serumony-refill-refillable-airless-dropper-packaging)

### Residual product

No benchmark brand publishes an independently comparable residual percentage for its exact retail serum. Traditional pipettes can leave Product below the tip, along the base, around corners, on the wiper, in the pipette, and on bottle walls. A broad square bottle can create unreachable corner volume; a very short bottle can constrain pipette length; a long pipette can contact the base or bend. Thick formulas can coat glass or bridge a narrow tip.

Measure residual as a package-system outcome:

1. Record filled package mass and net contents with formula density.
2. Follow the labeled regimen and an ordinary-consumer handling protocol through declared empty.
3. Record last successful dose, dose drift, drips/mess, and consumer perception of Product remaining.
4. Weigh package after ordinary use, after a defined recovery maneuver, and after destructive laboratory recovery.
5. Report ordinary residual in grams and percent of net contents, usable evacuation, P10/P50/P90, and stranded retail value.
6. Repeat across formula age, temperatures, orientations, lots, and both sizes.

An optional wiper can reduce neck/pipette mess but changes closure displacement, drag, dose, component count, and retained Product. Quote and test with and without it. Aptar's internal-dip-tube/short-applicator architecture and Quadpack Dropify's viscosity-dependent inner options are useful comparative mechanisms, not evidence that either will outperform on the helix formula. [Aptar Dermalogica case](https://www.aptar.com/en-us/news-events/aptar-beauty-launches-next-generation-auto-loading-dropper-technology-for-easier-more-accurate-skincare-dosing) · [Quadpack Dropify](https://catalogue.quadpack.com/product/14287932/dropify-bottle)

### Breakage and DTC logistics

Glass is not qualified by an empty-bottle drop test. Test the exact filled, closed, decorated, cartoned system through filling, component delivery, assembly, parcel fulfillment, customer use, and disposal. At minimum:

- bottle dimensions, empty mass, brimful and nominal capacity, fill/closure displacement, glass distribution, base stability, annealing, and thermal shock;
- filled-product drop in relevant bathroom and warehouse orientations;
- closure torque, gasket compression, bulb/collar damage, pipette fracture/contact, leak and pressure differential;
- vibration, repetitive shock, thermal cycling, humidity, abrasion, and e-commerce parcel tests;
- Mini and Full in their final unit carton/dunnage and realistic mixed-order parcels;
- master-case count, dimensions, gross mass, dividers, top-load, pallet pattern, cube utilization, and breakage rate; and
- post-test dose, seal, frosting/coating, legibility, and residual—not merely bottle survival.

Haramoon Vietnam's published 204 g packed weight and 56 × 105 × 35 mm dimensions are a useful request for an exact comparator sample, not a component-weight benchmark. Formula density, net fill tolerance, carton/dunnage, and whether the measurements describe the bottle or sale unit are unresolved. Do not subtract an assumed 50 g formula and call the balance “glass.”

The 50 mL broad slab is expected to use more formula, space, and protective material than a 30 mL compact round, but the cost model must use quoted item mass, case pack, breakage allowance, freight class/mode, and Southern California endpoint. The project's landed-cost model—not retail price or FOB unit price—governs comparison. [Landed-cost evidence model](./packaging-landed-cost-evidence-model.md)

### Unit carton

## Bottom line

The market supports **30 mL as the default Full Size**, but not because it is conventional: at a deliberately controlled 0.20–0.25 mL twice-daily dose it yields about **75–60 nominal days**, aligns with the broadest stock ecosystem, and limits glass/formula/cube. The honest Mini target is **10 mL** for 14–30 days across a broad plausible dose range, with **15 mL** as the better-evidenced stock and commercial fallback. A **50 mL Full** is justified only when the measured formula dose requires it; Haramoon's 50 mL appearance is not service-life proof.

Source the visual intent as a system: frost/amber, glass form, silver collar, black bulb, gasket/wiper, pipette, dose, residual, carton, and DTC protection. Begin with a differentiated stock round and keep square/rectangular as a gated option. The cheapest high-quality supplier cannot be named from public pages. It is the offer that passes identity, compatibility, dose, residual, breakage, regulatory, design-rights, and continuity gates and then wins on **risk-adjusted Southern California landed cost** at the 1,000–3,000 component objective—not the lowest visible bottle price.
