# White crinkle-retaining cleanser-tube supplier scan

- **Research date:** 2026-08-31
- **Decision scope:** primary containers for the helix cleanser, Track A (a white tube intended to remain collapsed or visibly folded as it empties)
- **Demand and MOQ basis:** model 1,000, 2,000, and 3,000 saleable finished units per Product split between Mini and Full Product Variants; separately target a 1,000–3,000 MOQ for each primary-component configuration
- **Provisional sizes:** 30 mL Mini and 120 mL Full; 40, 100, and 150 mL are sensitivity cases
- **Decision status:** public-evidence shortlist for a common RFQ and physical qualification; not a production award

## Decision in one page

The public evidence does **not** establish a production-qualified cheapest supplier. It establishes a useful global manufacturing set, the only public low-volume price references found, and the questions that must be answered through the common RFQ before suppliers can be ranked.

The scan found 16 credible direct-manufacturer candidates with a traceable factory or manufacturing identity across Europe, North America, Japan, Taiwan, and China, plus one supplier-asserted manufacturing route—Gamex—that remains conditional until its legal entity and actual production site are disclosed. It also found one unusually relevant low-MOQ commercial route—Pack-Tubes Studio in Texas—that must remain conditional until the legal seller, actual impact-extrusion site, certifications, and change-control responsibility are disclosed. No Korean supplier passed the direct-factory and fold-retaining construction screen on public evidence. That is a real evidence gap, not evidence that no such Korean factory exists.

The best documented **technical RFQ pair for both provisional sizes** is ALLTUB and Tubettificio Perfektüp: each publishes aluminum-tube diameter/capacity ranges that encompass 30 and 120 mL. Their public pages do not disclose launch-compatible MOQ, price, exact wall gauge, or lead time, so neither can yet be called commercially viable for helix. The most efficient next step is therefore a **two-lane RFQ**, not an award:

1. **Launch-MOQ lane:** Pack-Tubes Studio, Montebello, ALLTUB, Perfektüp, Scandolara, and TUBEX. Ask every bidder for 30 and 120 mL; retain 40 mL Mini and 100/150 mL Full as separately priced sensitivity cases. 2. **Scale/benchmark lane:** LINHARDT, Beauty Sky, Xinji, Xinyi, Hsuan Hau, Kansai Tube, Pedercini, Tecnotubetti, DBN, Metallipack, Gamex, and Taisei Kako. Every candidate starts with **fold retention unverified on the finished helix component**. “Collapsible aluminum,” “dead-fold,” “no suck-back,” tube thickness, and a wrinkled marketing photograph are not substitutes for a controlled sample test. Exact construction, internal coating, closure, tail fold, decoration, formula compatibility, and transport can change performance. The final common RFQ should require the same samples, test fill, conditioning, dispense schedule, 1-minute/24-hour profile measurements, evacuation measurement, transit conditioning, and lot/tool identity for every candidate.

## Scope and decision rules

This report answers one constrained question: which publicly identifiable suppliers merit an RFQ for a **white, fold-retaining cleanser tube** resembling the use-state character of a permanently collapsing artist-paint tube? It does not identify Aesop's supplier, reproduce a protected trade dress, award a supplier, select a formulation, or authorize outreach.

The qualified cohort is aluminum-led because that is where reviewed first-party sources disclosed both the actual seamless construction and relevant dead-fold/no-suck-back assertions. This is an **evidence-permits limitation**, not a conclusion that every ABL, PBL, or other laminate must recover its shape. The reviewed Pack-Tubes laminate page discloses a PE/EAA/aluminum/EAA/PE example, 2–250 mL range, and 10,000-piece MOQ but no retained-collapse assertion; it therefore remains an unverified alternative construction rather than a behavior-qualified candidate. The common RFQ may accept any fully disclosed low-springback construction, but it must be sampled and scored separately. [Pack-Tubes laminate construction](https://www.pack-tubes.com/product-page/laminated-tubes)

The working size architecture remains 30 mL Mini and 120 mL Full. Those are provisional research sizes, not final net-content declarations. The [cleanser size and supply-chain benchmark](./cleanser-packaging-size-supply-chain-benchmark.md) explains the usage and competitor logic; 40, 100, and 150 mL are retained here so an apparently attractive supplier is not rejected before the commercial effect of a nearby standard size is known.

### Evidence states

Every behavior or fit claim uses one of the following states:

| Code | Meaning | What it can support |
|---|---|---|
| **C — construction/capability** | A first-party supplier page or document identifies a material, process, size, closure, finish, factory, or certification claim. | RFQ eligibility and questions; not finished-component performance. |
| **S — supplier assertion** | The supplier expressly describes dead-folding, staying squeezed, no suck-back, or similar behavior. | Sample priority only; not verification. |
| **I — supported inference** | A conclusion follows from disclosed geometry or range, but the exact target component configuration is not published. | A conditional RFQ request, clearly labeled. |
| **U — unknown** | No adequate first-party public evidence was found. | Must remain blank or become an RFQ/document request. |

No reviewed source independently verifies the retained-collapse behavior of the exact proposed helix tube. “Aluminum” is a construction fact; retained collapse is a system-level performance requirement. **Wall gauge is unknown for every exact target candidate in this scan**, and no behavior conclusion is inferred from gauge or thickness.

Capacity fit uses a separate notation:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **E** | The supplier publicly lists that exact nominal capacity or a public stock item at that capacity. |
| **R** | The capacity lies inside a supplier-published diameter/capacity range, but an exact stock drawing is not public. |
| **G** | The source publishes grams, not milliliters; fit depends on formula density and tail fold. |
| **P** | Geometry makes the request plausible, but the supplier does not publish a capacity mapping. |
| **—** | Outside the public capacity range. |
| **U** | Public evidence is inadequate to assess fit. |

For commercial comparisons, this report follows the [landed-cost evidence model](./packaging-landed-cost-evidence-model.md). Public catalog and storefront facts are E1 context. A supplier becomes rankable only with an E3 written offer normalized to the same component scope and P3 Southern California landed boundary, after quality gates pass. Sustainability is a weighted objective under the [packaging sustainability method](./packaging-sustainability-scoring.md); it is neither an automatic material veto nor an automatic material win.

### Barrier is a formula-contact system, not just an aluminum body

The cleanser does not contact bare exterior aluminum. It contacts the disclosed internal lacquer or, in Taisei's two-layer offer, a polyethylene inner layer; the formula can also contact shoulder/nozzle material, membrane or puncture feature, tail sealant/latex, and closure liner. Consequently, “epoxy phenolic,” “BPA-NI,” “double coat,” “PE inner,” or “plain lacquer” must be tied to an exact supplier coating code, cure specification, regulatory declaration, and change-control agreement before compatibility is assessed.

The report does not treat an “aluminum tube” as automatically mono-material. ALLTUB's third-party-verified EPD examples for a D28 × 145 mm 65 mL tube with PP cap and a D32 × 161 mm 95 mL tube with PP cap allocate finished-system mass among aluminum, polymeric coatings, and PP cap; the published examples are 72/16/12% and 75/16/9%, respectively. Those example ratios are informative but must not be transferred to a different helix size or supplier. [ALLTUB EPD](https://www.alltub.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/alltub-aluminium-tubes-for-the-cosmetic-market.pdf)

### Gauge cannot be used as a shortcut

None of the reviewed first-party pages publishes the wall gauge for the exact 30 or 120 mL white target component. Diameter, “heavy wall,” annealing temperature, aluminum purity, and the word “collapsible” do not establish fold retention. The common RFQ should request:

- nominal body-wall gauge and tolerance at body, shoulder, and transition;
- alloy/temper or slug specification and anneal controls;
- internal lacquer dry-film target/tolerance and cure test;
- exterior base/ink/overprint coating mass or dry-film target where available;
- tail sealant or latex code and application controls;
- finished empty-component mass by part; and
- whether any construction change is permitted without written approval.

The data should be used to control replication and investigate test results—not to predict a passing fold score before samples exist.

14. all component-level MOQs, including cap, membrane, lacquer, white color, ink, carton, and pallet constraints;
15. filler setup/minimums, outside-component fees, line yield, test/retained units, Formula disposition, rework/scrap ownership, and responsibility allocation where the bidder fills—or the technical inputs a separate domestic filler needs to quote them; and
16. P2 filling/assembly/release and delivery of fulfillment-ready finished units to the Southern California warehouse/3PL for the **P3 boundary**, sourced from the responsible filler/3PL when the tube bidder does not control those stages.

The normalized cost model combines supplier and logistics evidence across P1, P2, and P3. An empty-component quote delivered to the filler is P1 and must never be labeled P3.

Only conforming written offers should enter the weighted decision. After gates, use 45% landed cost, 20% technical/performance, 15% evidence-adjusted sustainability, 10% reliability/lead time, and 10% aesthetic fit. An offer that fails factory identity, formula-contact disclosure, or performance qualification is **not** rescued by a low weighted score.

## Sustainability, compliance, and logistics

### Sustainability is weighted and evidence-adjusted

No candidate receives full sustainability credit for saying “aluminum,” “recyclable,” “green,” “PCR,” “mono-material,” or “zero waste.” The same evidence-adjusted 15-point method applies to all candidates.

Publicly notable evidence includes:

- ALLTUB's third-party-verified EPD for specific D28/D32 cosmetic tubes from the Kolín plant. It is strong process/site evidence but not an EPD for the helix 30/120 mL component configurations. [EPD](https://www.alltub.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/alltub-aluminium-tubes-for-the-cosmetic-market.pdf)
- ALLTUB's Green Tube PCR offer, LINHARDT's PCR tube/closure development, Scandolara's PCR content options, Pedercini's PCR/PIR claims, and Montebello's high-purity recycled-aluminum statement. Each exact claim needs configuration-level recycled-content percentage, pre/post-consumer split, mass balance versus physical content, certifier, plant, cap/coating exclusions, and availability at the quoted MOQ. [ALLTUB Green Tube](https://www.alltub.com/en-gb/sustainability/the-green-tube/) · [LINHARDT handout](https://linhardt.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/LIN_Handout_Mono-Material-Aluminium-Tube_EN.pdf) · [Scandolara](https://scandolara.com/tubetti-alluminio/) · [Pedercini](https://www.tubettificiopedercini.it/en/home.html) · [Montebello](https://www.montebellopkg.com/high-quality-aluminum-tubes/)
- Taisei's water-emulsion PE coating claim is relevant to manufacturing emissions but does not itself establish recyclability, lower life-cycle impact, or target compatibility. [Taisei](https://www.taisei-g.co.jp/english/products/aluminiumtube.html)

The RFQ should collect part masses for body, cap, membrane, coatings, sealant and decorations; exact PCR/PIR evidence; manufacturing energy and water data where available; factory-to-filler route; carton/pallet configuration; scrap rate; recovery assumptions; and a configuration-specific recyclability opinion for the intended U.S./California claim. A candidate with weaker material marketing but strong verified mass, route, defect, and recovery evidence can outscore a candidate with an unsupported “100% recyclable” page.

### U.S. and California controls

The [U.S./California cosmetic-packaging constraints report](./us-california-cosmetic-packaging-constraints.md) governs the final label and documentation. For this component decision:

- request exact toxics-in-packaging certification for every body, cap, liner, coating, ink, membrane and sealant;
- maintain a component/material/mass ledger suitable for California SB 54 analysis;
- do not make a recyclability claim by default;
- verify whether the chosen net quantity is expressed by volume or weight for the final formula and use correct U.S. customary/metric declarations; and
- do not treat a supplier's pharmaceutical/GMP marketing as proof of U.S. cosmetic packaging compliance.

### Logistics evidence needed

For P1 comparison, each supplier must quote the same empty-component delivery boundary to the named Southern California filler and disclose:

- manufacturing, decorating, cap, packing, consolidation and shipping locations;
- master-carton count, dimensions, gross/net weight, pallet pattern and stack limit;
- whether empty tubes ship open and how mouths/tails are protected from contamination or deformation;
- lot segregation and labels across tubes, caps, membranes and cartons;
- normal mode/port and alternate route;
- Incoterm, HS classification used for the quote, country of origin, duty/tariff assumptions, broker fees and insurance;
- transit days plus production queue, proof approval and inspection time;
- minimum economical air and ocean shipment; and
- replacement plan for dent, coating, print, contamination, closure, or transit failures.

No regional advantage should be asserted until carton density and the actual proposed route are known. Empty collapsible tubes can cube out before they weigh out; a closer factory is not automatically the lower landed-carbon or lower landed-cost option if packing efficiency, minimum shipment, defect rate, or required secondary handling differs.

The P3 comparison must then add P2 filling/assembly/release and finished-goods freight to the named Southern California warehouse/3PL. Those downstream values normally come from the domestic filler, carriers, broker, and 3PL—not from an empty-tube seller—and must use the same Product-volume/Mini–Full mix scenarios.

## Sample and retained-collapse qualification

### Sample stages

1. **Empty construction screen:** exact diameter/length, liner, white base, neck, membrane, closure and tail area; verify dimensions, mass, coating continuity, appearance, torque, seal and documentation.
2. **Safe-simulant behavior screen:** same tool/material/site where possible; reject constructions that recover shape, crack coatings, delaminate, split at shoulder/tail, lose seal, or retain excessive content.
3. **Filler engineering run:** verify empty-component feeding/handling, cleanliness, fill temperature, headspace, tail fold/crimp, coding, leak testing, dent controls, carton packout and line speed.
4. **Final-formula compatibility:** accelerated and real-time conditions appropriate to the formula; upright/inverted/on-side; repeated dispense; barrier, corrosion, liner, seal, odor/color, torque, leakage, weight loss and formula quality endpoints.
5. **Distribution/pilot lot:** production-representative decorated components from the nominated site/tool/lot, filled on the intended line, packed in intended secondary/tertiary system, transit-conditioned and released against the same golden sample.

### Controlled fold-retention method

The method should be finalized with the filler and lab, but every candidate must be tested identically:

1. Build a randomized replicate matrix at ambient, defined elevated, and defined low-temperature conditions, with upright, inverted, and on-side storage. Record body profile, mass, dimensions, cap torque and baseline photographs after conditioning.
2. For construction comparisons, hold Formula or safe simulant, net fill, decoration, cap and orifice constant where engineering permits. If final hardware cannot be matched, run a matched control and the proposed actual assembly separately and disclose every difference.
3. Dispense the defined mass twice daily using an ordinary-hand protocol or controlled fixture that represents intended use; cap consistently. Photograph the same marked face at fixed distance, angle, exposure and lighting.
4. Photograph and measure the collapsed profile immediately, at 1 minute, and at 24 hours after each defined use point. Record maximum thickness/profile recovery at fixed datum points rather than relying on a subjective “looks crinkled” judgment.
5. Repeat at approximately 25%, 50%, 75%, and 95% of dispensed contents using the same squeeze/fold protocol; document force escalation and the influence of storage orientation and temperature.
6. Measure ordinary-hand end-of-use residual product gravimetrically, then run a **separate** defined roll/squeeze maximum-evacuation method. Do not use the assisted roll result as the consumer-use or retained-fold score. Record suck-back, air intake, drool, cap fouling, cracking, sharp creases, pinholes, coating flake, tail/shoulder damage and leakage.
7. Repeat the sequence after formula-compatibility conditioning and the intended distribution test. Include a second production lot before final award.

The protocol should report the raw measurements for both 30 and 120 mL. A supplier can pass one size and fail the other because diameter, length, headspace, cap, tail fold and coating system differ.

### Provisional pass/fail framework

The final engineering team should set numeric limits after benchmark sampling, but award logic should include:

- no rebound that defeats the intended visibly collapsed use state at 1 minute or 24 hours;
- no liner/exterior cracking, flaking, pinhole, corrosion, leakage, tail/shoulder split or closure loss;
- controlled dose and acceptable force through the selected orifice throughout use;
- target evacuation efficiency and residual mass with no unsafe sharp edge or user handling problem;
- appearance within approved crinkle/dent boundaries after distribution; and
- repeatability across nominated tool, site, lot and both sizes.

Supplier assertions receive no pass credit. They only decide which samples should be tested first.

## Cost-benefit scenarios and recommended shortlist

### Scenario A — protect the 1,000–3,000-unit Product launch and component-MOQ objective

**First commercial check:** Pack-Tubes Studio 30 mL, with a request for a custom/stock 120 mL geometry; Montebello 40 mL stock check plus custom 30/120 quote. **Parallel direct-factory check:** ALLTUB, Perfektüp, TUBEX and Scandolara at the exact launch tiers. LINHARDT belongs in the scale benchmark unless it provides a written exception to its published 20,000-piece MOQ per product version.

Benefits:

- best chance of preserving launch cash and reducing obsolete packaging;
- public North American price/stock context; and
- enough European technical competition to test whether a direct factory will accept a pilot quantity or aggregate tooling.

Costs/risks:

- the only public tiered route lacks a 120 mL standard and sufficient identity/quality disclosure;
- small orders can carry high decoration/setup allocation and limited customization;
- the Mini and Full may come from different suppliers, creating white/color/cap mismatch and duplicate qualification; and
- public price is not landed cost.

Decision rule: do not raise MOQ merely to reduce unit price. Compare evidence-supported P3 cash/economic cost under identical Product-volume/Mini–Full mix scenarios; apply quality/compatibility gates first and score technical and reliability risk separately so the same risk is not counted again inside cost.

### Scenario B — accept a nearby stock size

Compare 40 mL Mini and 150 mL Full against 30/120 mL only after product/brand owners accept the usage and price-position implications. Pack-Tubes Studio gives a 150 mL price reference with an unresolved 180-versus-197 mm sleeve-length conflict; Montebello gives an aged 40 mL stock reference with unknown stock color; Perfektüp and ALLTUB encompass both sizes in published ranges.

Benefits:

- may unlock stock tooling, lower setup, faster samples and a more competitive MOQ;
- stronger public commercial evidence at 40/150 than at 120; and
- useful benchmark for size-driven landed cost.

Costs/risks:

- more formula and packaging material per sale, possible price/margin repositioning, and different use duration;
- larger Full may increase carton cube and shipping cost;
- Mini may no longer function as a true trial/travel size; and
- changing net content to fit a supplier is a product decision, not a procurement shortcut.

Decision rule: show the full product gross-margin, cash, freight, use-duration and sustainability effect—not just component unit cost.

### Scenario C — qualify a high-MOQ scale source while launching locally

Use a launch-compatible route for initial production while qualifying Beauty Sky, Xinji, Xinyi, Hsuan Hau or another direct scale factory for a forecast-triggered transfer.

Benefits:

- avoids committing 10,000–100,000 pieces before demand is proven;
- creates leverage and a validated scale path; and
- allows quality/factory audits and transit testing before dependency.

Costs/risks:

- duplicated samples, artwork, white/color matching, compatibility and filler validation;
- change in tube feel/fold profile can be consumer-visible;
- inventory bridge and supplier-change disclosure/label impacts; and
- the apparent high-volume saving can disappear after tooling, freight, duty, defects and write-offs.

Decision rule: transfer only after the exact scale item passes the same gates, and preserve the approved construction/site/tool through written change control.

## Unknowns that must remain unknown today

- No exact helix tube has a verified retained-collapse result.
- Exact 30/120 mL wall gauge is not public for any candidate.
- No direct factory has publicly confirmed the 1,000–3,000 MOQ for both exact provisional sizes.
- No comparable current P3 landed quote exists.
- No current public quote includes an unquestionably identical body, liner, sealant, membrane, closure, decoration, tooling, inspection, packout, freight, duties and delivery scope across suppliers.
- The final cleanser formula-contact compatibility is untested.
- The final net-content unit, density, maximum fill, headspace and tail fold are not set.
- The current availability of several PCR or aluminum-closure options at the target size/MOQ is unconfirmed.
- Korean direct-factory availability remains an evidence gap.
- Pack-Tubes Studio's upstream manufacturing/legal chain is unresolved.

Preserving these unknowns is a procurement control. Replacing them with optimistic assumptions would create false precision and bias the common RFQ.

## Final recommendation

Proceed to the later common RFQ with the A1/A2 candidates and use B/C candidates as controlled technical and scale comparators. Require exact 30 and 120 mL drawings first, quote 40/100/150 alternatives separately, and refuse anonymous or undisclosed factories. Buy no production quantity until the nominated site/tool/material/liner/closure system passes the empty-component, retained-collapse, compatibility, filler, transit, pilot-lot and compliance gates and the Product-level Mini/Full scenarios have a complete evidence-supported P3 model.

The cheapest **public number** is not the cheapest qualified packaging system. After hard gates, rank conforming offers using the approved composite: **45% evidence-supported P3 landed cost, 20% technical/performance, 15% evidence-adjusted sustainability, 10% supplier reliability/lead time, and 10% aesthetic fidelity**. Keep systemic supplier/quality risk in the appropriate gate, score, and sensitivity analysis unless a specific ordinary failure cost can be defensibly monetized; do not hide it inside an undefined “risk-adjusted cost” or double-count it.
