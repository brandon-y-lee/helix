# Packaging Quality, Compatibility, and Supplier Gates

**Status:** Public research report  
**Decision scope:** Formula-independent supplier and package prequalification for Helix primary, secondary, tertiary, decoration, filling, warehousing, and logistics partners  
**Research date:** 2026-08-30  
**Decision boundary:** This report defines what can be approved from documents, empty components, or safe mechanical simulants. It deliberately does not approve an exact Formula/package combination for sale.

## Executive decision

Helix should use a **gated release ladder, not a blended supplier score**. Cost, aesthetics, sustainability, service, and lead time can rank suppliers only after every hard gate is passed. A low quote, a familiar brand customer, an ISO certificate, a polished sample, or a passing empty-package test must never compensate for unknown factory provenance, undisclosed product-contact materials, missing traceability, uncontrolled changes, a critical defect, or refusal to complete Formula-specific validation.

The defensible approval sequence is:

1. verify the exact legal entity, manufacturing site, processes, and subcontractors;
2. approve the quality system, evidence packet, bill of materials, drawings, tolerances, and change-control terms;
3. test production-equivalent empty packages and, where useful, safe mechanical simulants;
4. qualify the nominated tooling, cavities, decoration, assembly, filler line, packout, and transport configuration;
5. hold final release until the exact Formula in the exact saleable pack passes compatibility, stability, microbiology, filling-line, and distribution validation; and
6. inspect and release each production lot under the approved specification and sampling plan.

This separation follows the underlying regulatory risk. FDA identifies the container itself, insanitary manufacture, packaging, storage, and shipping as potential routes to adulteration; its cosmetic GMP inspection checklist expects primary-packaging identity, lot/status control, sampling, testing, rejection controls, permanent codes, and records. FDA does not prescribe one universal cosmetic test list: the responsible company remains responsible for using scientifically sound evidence to substantiate safety. ([FDA GMP inspection checklist](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-guidance-documents/good-manufacturing-practice-gmp-guidelinesinspection-checklist-cosmetics), [FDA product testing](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-science-research/product-testing-cosmetics), [FDA authority over cosmetics](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/fda-authority-over-cosmetics-how-cosmetics-are-not-fda-approved-are-fda-regulated))

## What this report can and cannot establish

### Formula-independent approval can establish

- who actually manufactures, decorates, assembles, fills, warehouses, and transports the package;
- whether those named sites have credible quality systems and controlled subcontracting;
- the identity, revision, dimensions, tolerances, and declared materials of every package subcomponent;
- whether samples represent the nominated production site, tool, cavity, material, finish, and process;
- empty-package workmanship, dimensional fit, closure function, leakage, dispensing mechanics, evacuation, decoration durability, glass robustness, case/pallet performance, and route-specific transport resistance;
- whether the supplier can trace lots, control changes, contain defects, investigate root causes, and deliver reliably; and
- whether the filler can receive, quarantine, inspect, reconcile, fill, close, code, reject, retain, and trace the package under controlled conditions.

### Formula-independent approval cannot establish

- chemical compatibility with the final Formula;
- migration, sorption, corrosion, stress cracking, delamination, elastomer swelling, coating attack, or adhesive failure caused by that Formula;
- preservative effectiveness or microbiological protection in use;
- final shelf life, product weight loss, odor/color/pH/viscosity stability, dose, or evacuation across the claimed life;
- equivalence after a Formula, material, site, tool, process, decoration, fill, headspace, or packout change; or
- survival of the exact finished pack through the intended distribution system.

ISO/TR 18811 explicitly provides a framework rather than a universal cosmetic stability protocol: the manufacturer must select and justify the conditions, parameters, and acceptance criteria, including packaging interactions. ISO 11930 provides a reference approach for interpreting preservation-efficacy testing and microbiological risk assessment when evaluating a cosmetic product's overall antimicrobial protection. These remain final-product work, not paperwork that a package supplier can pre-answer. ([ISO/TR 18811:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/63465.html), [ISO 11930:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/75058.html))

## The release ladder

| Stage | Minimum evidence | Decision | Expiry or reset trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Identity screen | Legal entity, site address, process map, named subcontractors, ownership, sanctions/adverse-event screen as applicable | Reject, investigate, or admit to document review | Any identity, ownership, site, or subcontractor change |
| 1. Document prequalification | Site-scoped QMS evidence, complete component dossier, drawings, specifications, traceability, change control, defect/CAPA process, capacity and continuity evidence | Reject, conditional, or approve for sampling | Expired certificate, overdue audit, unresolved major finding, or material change |
| 2. Empty-package qualification | Production-equivalent samples; dimensional, functional, leakage, dose, evacuation, decoration, glass, secondary, and transport tests selected by risk | Reject, revise, or approve the package platform for Formula trials | Drawing, tool, cavity, material, process, decoration, site, or test-method change |
| 3. Filler and pilot qualification | Nominated line, settings, controls, coding, rejects, reconciliation, yield, line trial, production-equivalent packout | Reject, remediate, or admit to finished-product validation | Line, setup, equipment, fill condition, or component change |
| 4. Formula-specific validation | Exact Formula, every saleable size, exact full BOM and decoration, stability/compatibility/microbiology, line trial, finished-pack transit | Approve or reject the exact commercial configuration | Any relevant Formula, package, process, fill, claim, shelf-life, or route change |
| 5. Lot release and surveillance | Approved sample plan, inspection/test record, CoA/CoC where specified, traceability, deviations, retain samples, supplier scorecard | Release, quarantine, concession, rework, or reject lot | Every lot; tightened control after adverse trend or change |

Approval is configuration-specific. A pass for the mini bottle does not approve the full-size bottle; a clear bottle does not approve a coated or frosted bottle; an undecorated sample does not approve the decorated pack; and a manually assembled sample does not approve a production assembly line.

## Universal hard gates

### 1. Factory identity and provenance

Before an RFQ finalist is treated as a manufacturer, Helix should require:

- exact corporate legal name, registration number, ownership, manufacturing address, and quality contact;
- each activity performed at that site: molding, glass forming, tube extrusion, lacquering, coating, frosting, printing, labeling, closure assembly, bulb/pipette assembly, cartoning, filling, warehousing, or testing;
- all brokers, distributors, factories, decorators, assemblers, laboratories, fillers, warehouses, and logistics subcontractors in the chain;
- a written approved-site list and a ban on substitution or undisclosed subcontracting;
- site-scoped certificates and audit reports whose legal entity, address, scope, dates, and issuing body match the claimed work;
- proof that quoted samples and future production come from the nominated factory, tool, cavity set, materials, and processes; and
- tooling ownership, location, unique ID, capacity, preventive-maintenance plan, spare strategy, and access rights.

A distributor may remain commercially useful, but it must disclose the actual factory and preserve the same lot traceability, approved-site controls, and change notices as a direct manufacturer. A trading-company certificate cannot be used as evidence for an unnamed factory.

ISO itself does not certify organizations. Where certification is used, Helix should verify the issuing certification body, accreditation, certificate status, scope, site, and expiration rather than accepting a logo or PDF at face value. ISO 9001 is useful evidence of a general quality-management system; it does not prove that a particular package is conforming or compatible. ([ISO on certification](https://www.iso.org/certification.html), [ISO 9001:2015](https://www.iso.org/standard/62085.html))

For a cosmetic filler or contract manufacturer, ISO 22716 is relevant because it covers cosmetic production, control, storage, and shipment. It is not a substitute for component-level evidence and should not be demanded of a packaging-only factory without first establishing that the scope is applicable. Likewise, applicable FDA facility registration under MoCRA should be verified for cosmetic manufacturing or processing facilities, but registration is neither FDA approval nor proof of quality. ([ISO 22716:2007](https://www.iso.org/standard/36437.html), [FDA Cosmetics Direct](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/registration-listing-cosmetic-product-facilities-and-products/cosmetics-direct), [FDA MoCRA](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/modernization-cosmetics-regulation-act-2022-mocra))

### 2. Complete package dossier

The controlled dossier must cover every functional and product-contact part, not merely the visible container:

| Evidence | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Component map | Body, shoulder/head, neck/finish, cap, overcap, liner, seal, membrane, gasket, wiper, bulb, collar, pipette, dip tube, pump orifice, coating/frost, lacquer, ink, label, adhesive, carton, insert, shipper, divider, pallet and containment |
| Material declaration | Material family and grade where available, virgin/recycled content, color masterbatch, additives, coatings, inks, adhesives, elastomers, glass color/treatment, and whether each surface is direct, indirect, or non-product-contact |
| Controlled drawings | Revision, dimensions, tolerances, capacity, fill/finish geometry, weight, closure interface, pipette/end clearance, seal land, decoration zone, critical-to-quality characteristics, cavity identification, and approved measurement method |
| Specification | Workmanship limits, performance tests, conditioning, sample size, acceptance criteria, defect classes, packaging/handling, shelf/storage controls, and required records |
| Lot evidence | Raw-material lot, production date/shift/line/tool/cavity, assembly/decorator lots, inspection status, CoA/CoC fields, release signature, and forward/backward traceability |
| Production equivalence | Nominated site/tool/cavity/material/finish/decoration/process; signed golden sample; color and finish master; first-article approval; no undisclosed hand finishing |

An SDS, generic resin brochure, food-contact letter, or country-of-origin statement alone does not establish material identity, cosmetic suitability, or compatibility. Any legal or restricted-substance declarations required for a market belong in the compliance workstream, but the quality dossier must identify the exact part and material to which each declaration applies.

### 3. Change control

The quality agreement should require written notice and Helix approval **before** changing any:

- legal entity, factory, subcontractor, source, material grade, recycled-content source or percentage, colorant, coating, lacquer, ink, adhesive, elastomer, or formulation of a package material;
- drawing, tolerance, mold, cavity, tooling repair, manufacturing process, sterilization/cleaning practice, decoration, assembly, inspection, test method, sampling plan, or release site;
- filler line, machine part, settings window, cleaning method, torque/crimp/seal parameters, coding, packout, shipper, pallet pattern, carrier, warehouse, or route; or
- certificate, regulatory status, discontinuation timing, capacity, ownership, or business-continuity condition that could affect supply or conformity.

Notice alone does not authorize a change. The agreement must state which changes require document review, new first articles, partial requalification, or full Formula-specific revalidation, plus how obsolete stock and mixed lots are segregated.

### 4. Traceability, nonconformance, and CAPA

Every supplier must demonstrate one-step-forward/one-step-back traceability through all named sites, components, assembly lots, and finished lots. A mock trace should recover the affected inputs, cavities, production window, inspections, customers/shipments, and retained samples within an agreed response time.

The nonconformance system must provide quarantine, status labeling, deviation/concession approval, containment, rework instructions, reinspection rules, root-cause analysis, corrective and preventive action, effectiveness checks, replacement or credit, and escalation for complaints or field failures. Suppliers must not ship on verbal deviation. Critical defects, suspected falsification, loss of traceability, or uncontrolled substitution trigger immediate stop-ship and investigation.

FDA's inspection checklist specifically expects controlled identity and status for primary packaging, disposition of rejected materials, batch/filling records, code marks, distribution records, and laboratory records. Those expectations make traceability and hold/release controls commercial gates even before a final Formula is selected. ([FDA GMP inspection checklist](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-guidance-documents/good-manufacturing-practice-gmp-guidelinesinspection-checklist-cosmetics))

### 5. Test evidence and laboratory competence

Every consequential report should identify:

- sample description, drawing revision, site, lot, tool/cavity, decoration and assembly status;
- quantity, conditioning, method and revision, fixtures, equipment, calibration status, and test laboratory;
- pre-agreed acceptance criteria and defect classification;
- raw observations and measurements, not only a certificate that says “pass”;
- deviations, excluded results, photographs where useful, statistics, conclusion, reviewer, and date; and
- the exact configuration to which the result applies.

When third-party laboratory evidence is warranted, verify ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation for the **applicable method and scope**, not merely a general laboratory badge. ISO/IEC 17025 addresses competence, impartiality, and consistent laboratory operation, but accreditation still does not choose the right test or acceptance limit for Helix. ([ISO/IEC 17025:2017](https://www.iso.org/standard/66912.html))

### 6. Reliability and continuity

Commercial qualification should include site-level evidence for:

- quoted versus demonstrated capacity by line, mold/tool, cavity and shift;
- promised versus actual lead time, on-time-in-full delivery, defect/complaint rate, and expedited-shipment history over a meaningful period;
- sample, pilot and production-lot equivalence;
- preventive maintenance, spare tooling/cavities, critical raw-material sources, constrained operations, shutdown calendars, and recovery time;
- backup electricity, disaster, cyber, labor, port, freight and geographic-concentration plans; and
- financial and ownership conditions material to continuity.

Where a new supplier has no reliable history, use conditional qualification, smaller staged orders, intensified inspection, and an expiry date. Treat claims about large-brand customers as leads to verify, never as a release criterion. Quote price should enter the cost-benefit model only after these gates.

## Formula-independent test matrix

The methods below are a **risk-based menu**, not a demand to run every published standard on every part. Helix and the qualified laboratory must select current, applicable methods; define conditioning, sample size, orientation, cycles, and acceptance limits in advance; and retain raw evidence.

### All primary packages

| Attribute | Empty-package or safe-simulant evidence | Required interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and workmanship | Material/BOM check; weight; appearance; contamination, particulates, flash, sharp edges, chips, cracks, sinks, warpage and molding/glass defects | Critical safety defects are not averaged into an aesthetic score |
| Dimensions and capacity | Drawing-based dimensional study across lots and cavities; brimful/nominal capacity; finish/closure gauges; component interchangeability | Report distribution and outliers, not only averages |
| Closure function | Application/removal torque, torque retention, thread engagement, opening force, tamper feature, liner/seal seating and repeated cycles | Use production closure equipment or correlate bench fixtures to it |
| Integrity | Upright, side and inverted gross-leak testing; pressure/vacuum or altitude exposure where route-relevant; seal inspection | State sensitivity and limitations; “no visible leak” is not barrier or compatibility proof |
| Dispensing | Delivered mass/volume, repeatability, drip/stringing, priming, recovery, dose at specified orientation and cycle | Safe inert media may compare mechanics only; Formula viscosity and surface tension remain future tests |
| Evacuation | Residual amount, usable dose count, closure/wiper access and end-of-life behavior | Safe simulant is directional, not final product performance |
| Durability | Handling/drop/impact, repeated opening, cap/hinge/closure cycling, component retention | Define realistic bathroom and fulfillment hazards |
| Decoration | Color difference, gloss, registration, print completeness, adhesion, rub/scuff, chemical resistance to agreed non-Formula exposures, code legibility | A flat coupon result does not automatically approve a shaped decorated pack |

ASTM publishes applicable methods for torque retention of continuous-thread closures, qualitative gross leak integrity of threaded rigid packages, vacuum leakage of empty rigid containers, and gross leak detection for flexible packages. The flexible-package method warns that small leaks may escape detection and that viscous contents can plug a leak; method sensitivity must therefore be stated. ([ASTM D2063/D2063M-24](https://store.astm.org/d2063_d2063m-24.html), [ASTM D5094/D5094M-24](https://store.astm.org/d5094_d5094m-24.html), [ASTM D4991-25](https://store.astm.org/d4991-25.html), [ASTM D3078-02(2021)e1](https://store.astm.org/d3078-02.html))

For decoration, methods exist for chemical resistance of printed inks/coatings, abrasion of printed matter, tape adhesion, and instrumental color difference. These methods support controlled comparison, but the purchaser and producer still need to agree the relevant chemicals, substrate, preparation, procedure, and pass/fail limits. ASTM D3359 is principally scoped to coatings on metallic substrates and yields a coarse adhesion rating, so it should not be applied indiscriminately to plastic or glass decoration. ([ASTM F2250-13(2025)](https://store.astm.org/f2250-13r25.html), [ASTM D5264-98(2019)](https://store.astm.org/d5264-98r04.html), [ASTM D3359-23](https://store.astm.org/d3359-23.html), [ASTM D2244-25](https://store.astm.org/d2244-25.html))

### Cleanser tubes

For both the planned crinkle-retaining metal-style tube and the flexible non-crinkling plastic/laminate tube, qualify:

- tube material/layer construction, wall gauge and distribution, diameter/length, head and orifice geometry, cap fit and orientation;
- side seam, head/shoulder weld, crimp or end-seal dimensions and strength;
- closure airtightness, membrane/foil presence and burst/removal behavior, deformation, burst/gross leakage, drop, repeated flexing, and evacuation;
- for aluminum or internally lacquered structures, lacquer identity, thickness, porosity/continuity and adhesion; and
- decoration registration, adhesion, rub, cracking and scuff after flexing, filling, sealing and transport.

The current EN 12377 method addresses closure airtightness for flexible tubes. The European Tube Manufacturers Association also maps tube-specific EN methods for wall thickness, dimensions/tolerances, lacquer thickness and porosity, head weld, print and coating adhesion, side-seam strength, membrane strength, deformation, and closure orientation. Use the applicable subset with Helix-defined limits; listing a method is not a pass. ([UNE-EN 12377:2023](https://www.une.org/encuentra-tu-norma/busca-tu-norma/norma?c=N0071580), [ETMA tube types and standards](https://www.etma-online.org/tubetypes.html))

Chemical attack on metal/lacquer, stress cracking or delamination of plastics/laminates, Formula-driven gas or weight loss, and final crinkle/evacuation behavior are held for Formula-specific validation.

### Serum dropper packs

Qualify the glass bottle, closure, bulb, collar, gasket/liner, wiper and pipette as one assembly:

- bottle capacity, weight, wall/base/finish dimensions, verticality, top sealing geometry, annealing/stress evidence where applicable, thermal shock, impact/drop, base stability, chips and sharp edges;
- pipette material, length, straightness, tip quality, end clearance, retention and alignment across bottle/closure tolerances;
- collar/thread fit, closure torque and retention, gasket/liner/wiper dimensions and retention, upright/side/inverted leakage and cycling;
- bulb recovery, compression force and mechanical set under empty/safe-simulant conditions;
- delivered mass/volume, repeatability and coefficient of variation across units, lots, orientations and dose cycles; and
- frost/coating, metallization, print and color durability after closure cycling, handling and transit.

ISO glass-container standards provide applicable methods for thermal-shock resistance and dimensional tolerances, while the ISO/TC 63 catalogue also identifies methods for capacity, verticality, height/finish geometry, vertical load and wide-mouth sealing-surface flatness. The exact subset must match the selected flaconnage geometry and capacity; a method whose scope starts above a mini size cannot be assumed to cover it. ([ISO 7459:2004](https://www.iso.org/standard/35615.html), [ISO 9058:2008](https://www.iso.org/standard/41965.html), [ISO/TC 63 glass-container catalogue](https://www.iso.org/committee/49492/x/catalogue/), [ASTM C149-14(2020)](https://store.astm.org/c0149-14r20.html))

Formula-driven bulb/gasket swelling or embrittlement, pipette/coating attack, extractables or migration as risk indicates, dose at final viscosity, and long-term seal integrity remain Formula-specific.

### Moisturizer jars

Qualify the glass jar, cap, liner/seal and any inner disk or spatula together:

- capacity, finish, thread, sealing-land flatness, cap/liner fit, application/removal torque and torque retention;
- upright/side/inverted leakage where the closure claim requires it, induction or pressure-sensitive seal evidence if selected, opening and repeated-use cycling;
- glass thermal shock, vertical load/stack response, impact/drop, base stability, chips and sharp edges;
- access, nominal fill/headspace geometry, usable evacuation and product trapping around shoulder/threads using safe mechanics only; and
- frost/coating, print, color and cap-finish durability after cycling and transit.

FDA identifies consumer finger contact with jar products as a contamination route. That makes in-use microbiological protection a future finished-product gate; an attractive wide mouth or an empty-jar test cannot answer it. ([FDA microbiological safety and cosmetics](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/potential-contaminants-cosmetics/microbiological-safety-and-cosmetics))

### Secondary packaging and decoration

For cartons, inserts, labels, seals and assembly operations, approve:

- dieline and assembled dimensions, board/caliper or other agreed material property, cut/crease/fold/glue quality, closure and product fit;
- protection against bottle/tube/jar movement, abrasion and glass-to-glass contact;
- print content/version control, color and registration, legibility, barcode/data-code verification, code permanence and mix-up prevention;
- label location, adhesion, edge lift, tunneling, ooze, wrinkle and performance after humidity, cold/heat and handling conditioning;
- tamper-evident feature presence and evidence for the intended claim; and
- scuff, rub, abrasion, compression and appearance after the complete distribution sequence.

Secondary-pack tests should be performed on the finished primary pack at representative weight and dimensions. A carton that passes empty or around a dummy block may support development, but it is not the final packout release.

### Tertiary packaging, warehousing and logistics

Qualify the exact saleable-unit count, divider, void fill, shipper, tape/seal, master case, pallet pattern, stretch-wrap/containment and route:

- case dimensions, board construction and agreed edge-crush/box-compression performance;
- packout count/orientation, dividers, overhang, load stability, compression/creep and warehouse stacking duration;
- climatic conditioning, vibration, manual and mechanical handling, drop/impact, compression and altitude/pressure when route-relevant;
- DTC parcel and master-case/unit-load protocols selected for the actual carrier and distribution system; and
- post-test leakage, glass breakage, closure movement, dose function, carton crushing, label/decoration damage and saleable appearance.

ASTM D4169 and D7386 provide distribution-cycle frameworks; ASTM D4332 addresses conditioning; ASTM D642 addresses compression; and ASTM D6179 addresses rough handling of unitized loads. ISTA 3A is a parcel-delivery simulation, but ISTA cautions that a test result does not necessarily satisfy every carrier rule. A pass applies only to the tested configuration, weight, conditioning and sequence. ([ASTM packaging standards catalogue](https://store.astm.org/products-services/standards-and-publications/standards/paper-standards-and-packaging-standards.html), [ASTM D7386-25](https://store.astm.org/d7386-25.html), [ASTM D4332](https://store.astm.org/standards/d4332), [ASTM D642-25](https://store.astm.org/d0642-25.html), [ASTM D6179-20(2025)](https://store.astm.org/d6179-20r25.html), [ISTA 3A overview](https://ista.org/docs/3Aoverview.pdf), [ISTA procedure-selection guidelines](https://ista.org/docs/2020_ISTA_Guidelines.pdf))

The warehouse/logistics gate additionally requires approved locations and carriers, environmental limits and monitoring, lot/status segregation, inventory accuracy, handling-damage controls, excursion/incident response, claims performance and recall cooperation.

### Cosmetic filler

The nominated domestic or ODM filler should demonstrate:

- an appropriate cosmetic GMP system, applicable facility registration, incoming quarantine and release, supplier controls, sanitation, cleaning and cross-contamination controls;
- line capability and controlled settings for the exact tube, dropper and jar configurations, including fill accuracy, headspace, crimp/end seal, torque, liner/seal application, wiper/pipette assembly, coding and reconciliation;
- line clearance, component/version verification, reject detection, yield and breakage accounting, in-process inspection, deviations/CAPA, retain samples and full lot traceability;
- an engineering trial using production-equivalent components and final decoration, followed by inspection and test of filled units; and
- separate setup, yield and acceptance evidence for mini and full sizes even when both use the same bulk Formula.

FDA's current inspection information notes that cosmetic GMP regulations are not yet codified in the same way as drug GMP, while MoCRA requires FDA GMP rulemaking. The filler qualification must therefore use the current law and FDA status at the time of manufacture, plus the quality agreement and scientifically justified controls above; it should not claim “FDA-approved” status. ([FDA inspection of cosmetics](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-compliance-enforcement/inspection-cosmetics), [FDA MoCRA](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/modernization-cosmetics-regulation-act-2022-mocra))

## Acceptance sampling, defects, and lot disposition

Helix should not publish a universal numeric AQL before drawings, risks, processes and filler controls are known. Instead, each approved specification must include:

1. a photographic defect standard and unambiguous **critical, major and minor** definitions for that component;
2. inspection levels, AQLs where appropriate, sample sizes, accept/reject numbers, normal/tightened/reduced switching rules and skip-lot restrictions;
3. separately identified destructive and functional tests;
4. lot formation rules that prevent mixing sites, tools, materials, revisions or untraceable production windows;
5. rules for quarantine, reinspection, sorting, rework, concession and destruction/return; and
6. trend limits that trigger tightened inspection, CAPA, requalification or stop-ship even if an individual lot passes.

ISO 2859-1:2026 is the current lot-by-lot, AQL-indexed sampling framework and contains switching rules between normal, tightened and reduced inspection. AQL is a sampling and economic tool, not permission to ship safety hazards. Critical defects such as glass chips/sharp edges, wrong materials or labels, loss of traceability, gross leakage, or package contamination should be governed by a separately justified zero-acceptance or equivalent hard-stop rule rather than diluted into a weighted score. ([ISO 2859-1:2026](https://www.iso.org/standard/85464.html))

Lot disposition belongs to Helix or its authorized filler/quality representative, not the supplier alone. Any concession must identify the exact lot, defect, risk assessment, quantity, expiry, segregation and authorized approver; it must not silently rewrite the standing specification.

## Supplier decision rules

| Status | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Reject / stop** | Factory or subcontractor cannot be verified; product-contact part or material is undisclosed; samples are not production-equivalent; traceability/change control is refused; certificate is false or materially mismatched; critical defect or contamination is found; data are manipulated; or supplier refuses final Formula-specific validation |
| **Conditional** | No hard-stop exists, but a bounded noncritical evidence gap, limited operating history, correctable finding or unproven capacity remains; record owner, corrective action, expiry, order cap and intensified inspection |
| **Prequalified for RFQ/sampling** | Provenance and document gates pass, complete dossier is available, quality agreement is viable, and supplier may create production-equivalent samples; this is not package approval |
| **Formula-trial approved** | Empty/simulant tests and production-equivalence gates pass for the named configuration; exact Formula validation may begin |
| **Commercial configuration approved** | Exact Formula, size, full BOM, decoration, filler line, packout and route pass the final validation protocol; approved specification and change controls are active |
| **Lot released** | The particular production lot passes identity, records, inspection/test and deviation review under the approved configuration |

Supplier comparison may begin only among candidates at the same gate status. A scorecard should display hard-gate status first, then weighted objectives such as landed cost, quality capability, sustainability, aesthetics, service, capacity and lead time. Missing evidence should be “unknown/not qualified,” not converted into an average score.

## Minimum RFQ and quality-agreement schedule

The supplier package should require, at minimum:

- approved legal entities, sites, processes, subcontractors and audit rights;
- complete component/BOM and product-contact map, controlled drawings, specifications and revision hierarchy;
- nominated tooling/cavities, ownership, maintenance, capacity and production-equivalent sample rules;
- certificates and declarations tied to exact sites and components, with verification rights;
- incoming, in-process and final controls; defect taxonomy, sampling plan, test methods and records;
- lot/cavity traceability, code format, retain samples and record-retention period;
- prior written change notification/approval and requalification triggers;
- nonconformance, containment, deviation, CAPA, complaint, stop-ship and recall cooperation;
- agreed test responsibility, laboratories, sample ownership, costs, acceptance criteria and raw-data access;
- order quantity, over/under-run limit, lead-time definition, capacity reservation, delivery terms and service metrics;
- handling, storage, shelf/storage limits, packout, pallet and transport requirements;
- warranty, replacement/credit, consequential investigation costs, confidentiality and intellectual-property/tooling terms; and
- business continuity, discontinuation notice and exit/transfer assistance.

Commercial counsel and the applicable compliance workstream should review legal, market-access and chemical-restriction clauses. This quality report should not be treated as legal advice or a substitute for market-specific compliance.

## Final Formula-specific hold

No package reaches commercial approval until a written, risk-based protocol tests the **exact Formula in the exact saleable configuration** for every size and relevant orientation. At minimum, that future protocol should address:

- accelerated and real-time stability with justified conditions and timepoints;
- appearance, odor, color, pH, viscosity/rheology, weight loss, leakage and closure integrity;
- package interaction: corrosion, stress cracking, delamination, sorption, migration/extractables where risk indicates, coating/ink/adhesive attack, elastomer swelling or embrittlement and glass/package surface effects;
- preservative effectiveness and microbiological quality, including in-use exposure appropriate to dropper, tube and wide-mouth jar behavior;
- delivered dose, repeatability, priming, evacuation, opening/closing and consumer-use cycles across temperature and age;
- filler engineering and production trials, fill/seal/crimp/torque settings, yield, component damage, coding and line-cleaning evidence;
- final decorated-unit, carton, case/pallet and DTC/master-case transit validation, followed by full functional and appearance inspection; and
- shelf-life conclusion, approved specification, retains, ongoing stability and change-control links.

Package suppliers themselves recognize this boundary. Aptar describes compatibility testing as material resistance to a customer's bulk over time and states that the customer/end user remains responsible for compatibility; its product literature conditions compatibility claims on conclusive laboratory testing. Quadpack's laboratory services similarly include Formula aging, leakage, torque, decoration and tube sealing while leaving compatibility responsibility with the brand. ([Aptar laboratory testing](https://aptar.com/en-us/beauty/expertise-services/laboratory-testing), [Aptar regulatory scope statement](https://aptar.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/aptar_fb_bh_europe_regulatory_scope_02.04.pdf), [Aptar dropper compatibility statement](https://aptar.com/en-us/news-events/aptar-beauty-reinvents-dropper-packaging-experience), [Quadpack laboratory services](https://www.quadpack.com/assets/13652916/quadpack-sets-up-laboratory-services-in-the-americas/))

## Bottom line

The cheapest high-quality package is not the lowest quoted component. It is the lowest **risk-adjusted landed cost among suppliers that pass the same hard gates** and can reproduce the approved configuration. Helix should use this report as the evidence schema for supplier research and RFQs: expose provenance and unknowns early, qualify components and factories before investing in full trials, and reserve final release for the exact Formula, fill, package, decoration and distribution system.
