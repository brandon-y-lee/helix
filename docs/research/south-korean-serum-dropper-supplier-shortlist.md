# South Korean serum dropper-system supplier shortlist

**Research date:** 2026-09-03

**Decision status:** Public-evidence shortlist for a common RFI/RFQ and gated sample program; no supplier or configuration is qualified

**Scope:** Primary packaging only for the Helix serum Mini and Full, delivered to a South Korean ODM

**Public-source boundary:** Current supplier-owned pages/catalogs, Korean public-authority directories, Korean exchange/regulatory filings, and official corporate reports. KOTRA's BuyKOREA is used only as a host for a clearly labeled seller-authored assertion, never as independent verification. No supplier outreach, private quotations, samples, audits, or unverified third-party marketplace claims were used as proof.

## Executive answer

Helix has an evidence-supported **six-supplier South Korea-focused RFQ shortlist**, plus three explicitly conditional Korean routes. It does not yet have six interchangeable Korean-made systems. Only **Pilko Cospharm** publicly bridges all three target capacities (10, 15, and 30 mL), clear/brown glass, a broad dropper-component menu, and disclosed Korean glass-forming and dropper-assembly equipment. Even Pilko is only the first RFQ lead: its public evidence does not prove one matched, production-ready Helix assembly, current stock, exact neck tolerances, target frost, measured dose, formula compatibility, launch MOQ, or delivered cost.

The non-padded core shortlist is:

1. **Pilko Cospharm** — strongest technical starting point across 10/15/30 mL bottle and dropper-component evidence.
2. **Morae Korea / Morae Packaging** — strongest independently corroborated direct Korean glass/plastic manufacturer with public 10 and 30 mL dropper items, but not one proven family.
3. **MIWOO Corporation** — strong dropper specialist with a 15–30 mL stock-mold program and a Korean-origin 30 mL round glass item; Korean factory scope and complete seal specification need proof.
4. **LuxePackKorea** — best public launch-quantity aesthetic lead: a 30 mL glass listing with silver-matte cap, black bulb, silicone/NBR choice, PE gasket, Ø18 neck, and 1,000-unit MOQ; upstream factories, glass pipette, Mini, and internal catalog consistency remain open.
5. **SMCG / Somang Glass** — direct Korean glass former and turnkey integrator; official filing says caps, pumps, and droppers are sourced from outside manufacturers, so the complete system and component countries must be mapped.
6. **KUGIL GLASS** — Korean commercial entity with a KOTRA-hosted, seller-authored export listing for a complete 7–50 mL system, 5,000-unit MOQ, and 30–35-day EXW lead; its supplier-issued equipment profile is for KUGIL VINA, so it is not a Korea-made route until item-level origin is proved.

Three controlled alternatives remain useful but do not meet the same public product-fit threshold: **PUM-TECH Korea** has named 15/30 mL metal-dropper listings but does not disclose bottle material or a coordinated glass Mini; **Yonwoo** has an exact 30 mL glass dropper but its coordinated Mini/Full families are plastic/heavy-blow; and **Verescence Pacific** has Korean-made 15/30 mL glass families but a 20,000-unit made-to-order MOQ and no publicly matched complete dropper. They belong in contingency, clarification, or scale work—not inside the six-supplier core.

This is a potential-supplier list, not an award list. Once the [confidential RFQ and sample-handling workspace](https://github.com/brandon-y-lee/helix/issues/263) exists and the common RFQ is approved, the first RFI/RFQ should go to Pilko, Morae, MIWOO, LuxePackKorea, SMCG, and KUGIL. Production-equivalent samples should initially be ordered only from Pilko, Morae, and MIWOO. KUGIL must pass its country-of-origin RFI before sample spend; SMCG must disclose its sourced dropper chain. PUM-TECH, Yonwoo, and Verescence remain controlled alternatives.

No public evidence supports a lowest-landed-cost conclusion. LuxePackKorea's 1,000-unit MOQ is a promising launch hypothesis, and a Korean factory-to-Korean-ODM route could remove a component import leg, but those advantages can be overwhelmed by unit price, decoration minimums, imported closures, tooling, surplus inventory, domestic freight, ODM outside-pack fees, defects, and finished-goods freight to Southern California. A cost decision requires normalized, configuration-identical **P1 delivered-to-ODM quotes** and a common **P3 delivered-Southern-California model**.

## 1. Scope, method, and evidence accounting

### 1.1 Decision question

The screen asks:

> Which 5–10 suppliers with a meaningful South Korean operating presence are credible RFQ candidates for coordinated 10 or 15 mL Mini and 30 mL Full serum dropper systems delivered to a South Korean ODM?

The normalized target is:

| Element | Mini target | Mini fallback | Full target | Non-negotiable interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Nominal fill | 10 mL | 15 mL | 30 mL | Same design language and preferably the same documented stock family |
| Bottle | Frosted clear/translucent glass and true amber glass alternatives | Same | Same | Brown spray, lacquer, or coating is not “true amber glass”; require glass-composition and spectral evidence |
| Dropper | Black bulb, silver metal-look collar, glass pipette | Same | Same | “Silver” must resolve to aluminum, metallized plastic, coating, or another named construction |
| Seal | Named gasket/liner/top-seal interface; formula-justified wiper/reducer optional | Same | Same | Neck shorthand alone does not establish system fit or leak resistance |
| Dose | Formula- and directions-led | Same | Same | Catalog aspiration or nominal dose is screening evidence, not filled-product performance |
| Decoration | Sparse Helix-owned art; abrasion- and compatibility-tested | Same | Same | Prefer lower-complexity stock round/soft-shouldered forms |
| Launch quantity | Quote 1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000 per SKU | Same | Same | Also quote 5,000 / 10,000 for scale comparison and disclose the true first-run minimum |

The size architecture is inherited from the [serum size and supply-chain benchmark](./serum-packaging-size-supply-chain-benchmark.md): 10 mL Mini, 15 mL stock fallback, and 30 mL Full. Supplier mold availability does not override the consumer-use case.

### 1.2 Evidence states

Every consequential finding uses one of these states:

| Code | Meaning | Procurement treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **F — verified public fact** | Current Korean public-authority record, exchange/regulatory filing, or other independently attributable official record. | May establish legal identity or disclosed operations, but not item conformance. |
| **A — supplier assertion/specification** | A statement, product field, certification label, capacity claim, or commercial term on a supplier-controlled source. | Confirms what the supplier publishes; obtain controlled documents and test the exact assembly. |
| **I — supported inference** | Bounded conclusion from cited F/A evidence. | Useful for prioritization only. |
| **L — lead only** | Secondary marketplace or directory claim lacking adequate first-party or public-authority support. | Cannot support shortlist facts or scoring. |
| **U — unknown** | Not found, internally inconsistent, or not tied to the requested configuration. | Missing does not mean compliant, available, or zero-cost. |

Certification logos and supplier sustainability claims remain **A**, not independent qualification. A current certificate must identify the legal entity, issuing body, certificate number, standard, site, scope, and validity period.

### 1.3 Inclusion, exclusion, and role definitions

A company enters the shortlist only when current primary evidence establishes all three:

1. a South Korean legal, commercial, factory, or finishing/assembly presence relevant to contracting or delivery;
2. glass-bottle and/or dropper capability material to the target system; and
3. a public, technically plausible path to both Mini and Full through catalog capacity coverage, disclosed custom capability, or a named integration model. A hoped-for private SKU or a possible no-bid response is not enough for the core shortlist.

Inclusion means **worth a controlled RFQ**, not qualified.

- **Direct Korean glass manufacturer** means a disclosed South Korean site transforms raw/tube glass into bottles. A Korean office, warehouse, decorator, or assembler does not meet this definition.
- **Korean manufacturer/integrator** means meaningful Korean molding, decoration, component production, or assembly is disclosed, but the exact glass bottle or another major component may be sourced.
- **Korea-based integrator/distributor** means the contracting and/or delivery interface is in Korea while upstream item factories are U.
- **Scale-only Korean route** means Korean manufacture is credible but public MOQ or architecture conflicts with Helix's launch plan.
- **Complete dropper system** means the exact bottle, neck, closure/inner cap, metal or metal-look collar, bulb, bulb retention, gasket/liner/top seal, pipette, optional wiper/reducer, decoration, and assembly method are controlled as one BOM.
- **Stock** requires current repeatable inventory or a controlled replenishment program. A catalog page, existing mold, “standard” design, or made-to-order listing is not stock.
- **True amber** means amber/brown color intrinsic to the glass, supported by composition/CoA and wavelength-transmission data. A colored coating is a different option.

Routes were excluded from the shortlist when the public evidence showed a non-glass target architecture, only a component with no complete-system path, no meaningful South Korean operating presence, or only unverified third-party marketplace/sourcing claims.

### 1.4 Evidence accounting

Nineteen named routes were screened:

- **6 core RFQ candidates:** Pilko, Morae, MIWOO, LuxePackKorea, SMCG, and KUGIL.
- **3 controlled alternatives:** PUM-TECH, Yonwoo, and Verescence Pacific.
- **5 relevant near-misses:** JeongwooCos, DOOCO, Samhwa, FS Korea Industries, and Y1 Glass.
- **5 excluded sourcing/non-Korean routes:** DAMA Korea, DKHY, The Lime Korea, Douen, and PKGroom.

Across the six core candidates and three controlled alternatives:

- **4 disclose relevant South Korean glass-forming sites:** Pilko, Morae, SMCG, and Verescence Pacific.
- **2 disclose substantial Korean packaging manufacture/assembly but not the requested glass bottle's full site chain:** PUM-TECH and MIWOO.
- **3 require stronger upstream-factory proof or a target-glass path:** Yonwoo, LuxePackKorea, and KUGIL.
- **1—Pilko—publicly spans 10/15/30 glass and a broad conventional-dropper component menu.**
- **0 publicly prove the exact Helix system, filled-formula compatibility, current stock, complete Korean country of origin, and comparable P1/P3 cost.**

No secondary marketplace claim was promoted into a factual field. Two supplier PDFs required visual checks. Samhwa's 2024 catalog was rendered and printed pages 22–23 were inspected, confirming that the DAL 30 mL **bottle is PET** while the “GLASS/PP” field applies to the pipette. This corrects the potential misreading of “DAL glass/PP dropper” as a glass bottle. KUGIL's August 2024 company profile pages 3–5 were inspected to preserve the timeline and layout associations: it labels the company profile **KUGIL VINA CO., LTD**, describes establishment/expansion of the Vietnam factory, and labels the equipment/capacity panel “KUGIL VINA PLAN (2024–2026).”

### 2.4 What the table does not prove

- **A supplier catalog row is not an approved BOM.** Even Pilko's bottle and dropper pages do not publicly prove that the chosen parts are qualified together.
- **18/410, 18/415, 20/410, or “Ø18” is not a fit certificate.** Finish tolerances, land/seal geometry, pipette seating, thread start, application torque, removal torque, liner compression, and wiper interference must be validated on the exact drawing and sample.
- **Frost is not light protection.** Frosting can be acid etch, spray, lacquer, or another process; it does not establish transmission performance.
- **Brown is not necessarily true amber.** Pilko's iron-oxide statement is the best public lead, but Helix still needs exact-SKU composition and wavelength transmission. LuxePack and Jeongwoo show colored coatings, not intrinsic amber proof.
- **A black bulb and silver collar shown elsewhere in a range are not automatically configurable together.**
- **Catalog availability is not stock.** Verescence explicitly says made to order; “stock molds” at MIWOO and existing items at other suppliers do not prove current finished inventory.
- **Corporate certification is not item qualification.** Obtain current site/scope documents and execute the Helix/ODM test plan.

## 6. Landed-cost and logistics implications: hypotheses, not conclusions

### 6.1 What a Korean route may remove

If—and only if—the bottle, dropper components, decoration, assembly, and ship-from point are genuinely in South Korea, delivery to a Korean ODM may remove:

- international component freight;
- component import brokerage, customs clearance, and duty/tax timing;
- ocean/air variability and port handling before fill;
- an extra importer/warehouse touchpoint; and
- some replenishment lead and safety-stock exposure.

That is a supported **route hypothesis**, not proof of lower total cost.

### 6.2 What remains or can reverse the advantage

Every Korean route still carries:

- domestic freight, receiving, quarantine, inspection, storage, breakage, and line-trial cost;
- ODM nominated/outside-component handling and possible outside-pack fees;
- tooling, decoration setup, color matching, sampling, overrun, defect, and replacement terms;
- MOQ surplus, cash tied in inventory, storage, obsolescence, and forecast error;
- imported subcomponents hidden inside a Korean assembly;
- ownership and maintenance of buyer-paid tools;
- formula/line failures and rework; and
- finished-product freight, insurance, U.S. import/customs treatment, and Southern California delivery.

Examples:

- **LuxePackKorea's 1,000-unit MOQ** could reduce launch cash and surplus, but upstream origin, actual unit price, pipette, decoration minimum, and quality risk are U.
- **Morae and MIWOO's 10,000-unit examples** may lower conversion price but exceed the 1,000–3,000 launch range.
- **Verescence's 20,000-unit made-to-order MOQ** is likely a working-capital and obsolescence burden at launch despite strong Korean glass provenance.
- **KUGIL's Busan ship point** does not simplify the route if the components arrive from Vietnam before domestic delivery.
- **PUM-TECH and SMCG** may still import or subcontract meaningful BOM elements; their filings explicitly prevent an all-in-house assumption.

### 6.3 Required comparison basis

Apply the [packaging landed-cost evidence model](./packaging-landed-cost-evidence-model.md):

- normalize every supplier to the identical controlled BOM and finish;
- obtain **P1** cost delivered to the same named South Korean ODM, with domestic freight and all packaging charges explicit;
- combine that with the same ODM fill/assembly/inspection terms and model **P3** finished goods delivered to the same Southern California destination;
- report both first-run cash requirement and per-consumed-good economic cost, including surplus and tooling;
- use the same exchange-rate date, tax/duty treatment, demand scenario, defect/yield assumption, and inventory horizon; and
- record evidence maturity. Public pages are at most E1 context, not comparable E3 quotes or E5 invoices.

Until at least two configurations pass the mandatory gates and have normalized quotes, Helix must not state that any candidate is the lowest-landed-cost or highest-quality source.

### 7.2 Sustainability

Exact identity, BOM, site, route, and usable yield are data prerequisites, not the whole sustainability gate zero. Use the [packaging sustainability scoring model](./packaging-sustainability-scoring.md) only after all applicable gate-zero requirements pass: formula compatibility; fill and use performance; product protection; consumer and worker safety; legal and stewardship readiness; and claim substantiation. Record the method, acceptance limit, sample count, package revision, formula batch, date, site, and approver for each applicable gate before assigning sustainability points.

- Korean proximity may reduce component freight distance, but it does not automatically outweigh glass mass, rejects, heavy decoration, or imported closure parts.
- Verescence's published LOU masses (53 g at 15 mL and 76 g at 30 mL) are inputs, not a sustainability score.
- SMCG's 60% PCR/GRS statement earns no item credit until the certificate, chain of custody, accounting method, and quoted-SKU scope are supplied.
- Acid etch, spray frost, PVD/metallization, and multi-material silver closures have different process and recovery implications; require the exact construction.
- A wiper adds material and separation complexity, but can reduce mess or residual product. Decide it through a formula-specific comparison, not a generic “more sustainable” claim.

Unknown fields receive no credit.

## 9. Limitations

- No supplier was contacted. Availability, price, capacity, willingness to supply 1,000–3,000 pieces, and ODM acceptance are unknown.
- No controlled drawings, private technical sheets, certificates, CoAs, factory audits, samples, formula, stability results, or line trials were available.
- Supplier pages prove what the supplier currently publishes, not independent conformance.
- Several sites are dynamic, translated, duplicated, or internally inconsistent. LuxePack product codes, Jeongwoo company metrics, and Pilko's LDPE wording require correction in writing.
- KUGIL's Korean seller identity and Busan ship point do not prove Korean manufacture; the available corporate equipment material points to Vietnam.
- “Stock,” “standard,” and “existing mold” were not treated as equivalent to live stock.
- Certification, recycled-content, capacity, evacuation, and precision claims were not scored without exact site/SKU support.
- True amber was not verified by spectral data for any final assembly.
- The South Korean ODM, delivery address, formula chemistry/rheology, labeled dose, forecast, and launch schedule were not specified; each materially affects fit, testing, and cost.
- Public pages can change after the access date. Procurement should archive controlled responses and revisioned attachments.

## 11. Map-ready decision gist

**Decision:** After the confidential workspace and common RFQ are approved, run one controlled Korean RFI/RFQ; do not award or call any supplier “qualified” from public evidence.

**Core shortlist:** Pilko → Morae → MIWOO → LuxePackKorea → SMCG → KUGIL. KUGIL remains country-of-origin-gated, and SMCG must disclose its outsourced accessory chain.

**Controlled alternatives:** PUM-TECH and Yonwoo require a target-glass Mini/Full path; Verescence Pacific is a scale-only Korean-glass benchmark.

**Key finding:** Pilko is the only public-source strict-fit leader across 10/15/30 glass, brown/clear options, a broad conventional-dropper BOM, and disclosed Korean glass/dropper equipment. Morae is the strongest independently corroborated Korean manufacturing challenger. All other routes have a material Mini, bottle-source, BOM, MOQ, or factory-origin gap.

**First sample wave:** Pilko, Morae, and MIWOO, subject to an approved risk-based sample protocol.

**Hard stops:** no exact factory map; no controlled matched drawings/BOM; coated brown presented as true amber; nominal neck offered as compatibility proof; unverified Korea origin; unwillingness to provide change control/traceability; no production-equivalent samples; no exact-formula/ODM-line qualification.

**Commercial rule:** compare identical passed configurations at P1 delivered Korean ODM and P3 delivered Southern California. Public MOQ and catalog pages are hypotheses, not a landed-cost winner.
