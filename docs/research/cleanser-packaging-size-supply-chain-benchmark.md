# Cleanser Packaging, Size, and Supply-Chain Benchmark

**Research ticket:** [#255 — Benchmark cleanser sizes, packaging, and supply chains](https://github.com/brandon-y-lee/helix/issues/255)  
**Research date:** 2026-08-30  
**Decision served:** choose defensible Mini and Full Size bands and preserve two distinct white-tube experiences for the helix Core cleanser.  
**Market:** United States direct-to-consumer; benchmark prices are current U.S. list prices where the brand publishes them.

> **Research recommendation, not a purchase specification.** Public Product pages seldom disclose tube gauges, full layer structures, cap resins, decoration systems, filling sites, evacuation, pallet patterns, or named packaging manufacturers. No supplier relationship, country of manufacture, service life, or cost is inferred from a Product image. Final selection requires signed drawings, material declarations, compatibility and transit tests, samples from the exact production tool, and binding quotations at helix quantities.

## Executive decision

Launch diligence should center on:

- **Mini: 30 mL / approximately 1.0 U.S. fl oz.** This is the cleanest fit for a 14–30-day trial/travel role at twice-daily use across the explicit 0.5–1.0 mL-per-cleanse sensitivity range. A 40 mL / 1.35 fl oz Rhode-like Mini is a justified fallback when the formula or orifice needs a larger practical dose, but it is less precise for the 30-day ceiling.
- **Full Size: 120 mL / approximately 4.0 U.S. fl oz.** It centers the 60–90-day target at a provisional 0.75 mL twice-daily design dose, is already validated as a commercial cleanser size by KraveBeauty and Tatcha, and avoids the formula, freight, cube, and working-capital burden of the category-common 150 mL size.
- **Keep two white-tube tracks through sampling.** Track A is a truly collapsible, fold-retaining tube; Track B is a resilient tube that substantially returns toward its original profile. Specify the behavior through a test method, not by asking for an “Aesop wall” or “Rhode thickness.”
- **Do not call the Aesop reference aluminum.** Aesop identifies the current Purifying Facial Exfoliant Paste package only as a **multilayered tube** and says it is not recyclable. The reference image retains folds, but neither the image nor the official page proves metal, layer materials, or wall gauge. Aesop separately says three hand/body balm tubes—not this facial paste—moved to 100% post-consumer-recycled aluminum. [Aesop paste](https://www.aesop.com/skin-care/cleansers-and-exfoliants/purifying-facial-exfoliant-paste/SK33.html) · [Aesop packaging materials](https://us.assistance.aesop.com/hc/en-us/articles/7406664205327-What-materials-do-Aesop-utilise-in-its-packaging)
- **Treat supplier visibility as a diligence gap.** The only first-party, brand-specific supplier evidence located is Boox publicly naming Rhode among brands that trust it; Boox does not identify Pineapple Refresh, a specific shipper, dates, volumes, or whether the relationship remains active. Public shipping-data pages point toward ICONS America for Rhode cleanser tubes and Toly Korea for other Rhode components, but those are unverified leads, not confirmed Product-supplier relationships.

The practical implication is a 30/120 mL launch pair, subject to a gravimetric home-use study and filling-line feasibility, with Track A and Track B sampled against the same formula, cap/orifice, decoration, and evacuation protocol.

## Scope and evidence rules

The panel covers **17 current Products from 16 distinct brands**: four indie, four K-beauty, four masstige, four luxury daily-cleansing brands, plus the mandatory Aesop exfoliant-paste tube reference. Every benchmark fact comes from a brand-owned Product page or another first-party source. Prices and availability can change; their purpose is directional price-per-fill context, not retail forecasting.

Evidence is classified as:

- **Verified Product fact:** the brand or named supplier publishes the fact for the exact Product or relationship.
- **Verified brand-level fact:** first-party evidence names the brand but not the exact Product, component, service, or time period.
- **Supported inference:** a calculation or procurement implication from verified facts, with its assumptions shown.
- **Unverified lead:** a third-party record or visual resemblance that needs direct confirmation.
- **Unknown:** the public evidence does not establish the fact. Unknown does not mean absent.

For Product imagery, “tube,” “pump,” “flip-top,” and similar descriptions identify the visible format only. Images do not establish resin, barrier, layer count, wall gauge, treatment, printing process, compatibility, or supplier.

## Tube behavior: specify the outcome, not the guessed material

### Track A — fold-retaining / permanently collapsing

The user experience is an editorial, paint-tube-like white pack that records use through permanent folds. The most deterministic technical route is a seamless collapsible aluminum tube: finished-tube supplier ALLTUB describes aluminum tubes as airtight, light/UV blocking, heat- and corrosion-resistant, 100% recyclable, printable in multiple colors/finishes, available from more than 2 mL to 250 mL, and compatible with many caps and varnishes. The public page does not publish MOQ. [ALLTUB aluminum tubes](https://www.alltub.com/en-gb/products/aluminium-collapsible-tubes/)

This is a **candidate route**, not an identification of Aesop’s tube and not yet a recommendation to buy. Aluminum introduces material-specific questions: internal lacquer compatibility, shoulder/nozzle geometry, crimp and tail-seal aesthetics, denting, sharp fold formation, cap-material separation, decoration scuffing, metal detector and filler setup, transit abrasion, and recycling access. White is a decoration requirement—base coat/print/varnish—not a material identity.

The alternate route is a deliberately low-springback laminate. Aesop’s reference proves a multilayer construction can visibly retain folds, but its exact layers and mechanics are undisclosed. A supplier must therefore demonstrate the fold behavior on the exact sample; “multilayer” alone does not predict it.

### Track B — resilient / non-retaining

The Rhode-like experience keeps a calm, full silhouette through use. PE and coextruded tubes are the obvious family to test, but again the behavior depends on resin mix, diameter, wall, barrier, shoulder, print, and formula. Neopac states that its overextruded Polyfoil laminate has greater stability and restoring force and an inner layer designed for low residue; it manufactures PE, coextruded, and Polyfoil tubes. That is useful mechanism evidence, not a Rhode supplier relationship. Neopac’s public contact form states a **10,000-tube MOQ**, above helix’s current 1,000–3,000 primary-container objective, so it is presently a technical benchmark or later-scale option rather than a launch-fit supplier. [Neopac Polyfoil behavior and public MOQ](https://www.neopac.com/en/news-events/50-jahre-polyfoil-eine-symbiose-von-schutz-und-design)

KraveBeauty establishes a commercially current resilient-looking coextruded PCR route, and Rhode establishes quantified PCR content, but neither brand publishes full layer structure or mechanics. PCR percentage cannot be copied blindly: Krave publishes **85% PCR in Full versus 58% in Mini**, demonstrating that small tube geometry and performance can change the feasible material mix.

### Required behavior test for both tracks

Issue an identical, supplier-neutral protocol with the request for quotation:

1. Fill production-tool samples with the exact formula or a validated rheology/chemistry simulant; condition upright and inverted at ambient, elevated, and low temperature.
2. Record initial dimensions, empty package mass, brimful capacity, nominal fill, headspace, seal integrity, and opening torque.
3. Dispense a fixed mass through the proposed orifice twice daily. Photograph the same faces under controlled lighting after 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 95% nominal use.
4. After each dispense, apply a fixed compression and measure profile recovery after 1 minute and 24 hours. Track A should remain collapsed/folded; Track B should recover toward an agreed silhouette.
5. At end of use, allow ordinary hand evacuation, then a defined roll/squeeze protocol. Weigh residual formula and report usable evacuation percentage. Do not substitute a supplier’s qualitative “good emptying” claim.
6. Repeat after vibration/drop/transit conditioning and after formula compatibility aging. Inspect stress whitening, pinholes, delamination, cracking, print rub, cap leaks, odor transfer, paneling, and tail-seal failure.

The test converts an aesthetic preference into comparable evidence and prevents material folklore from deciding the pack.

## Closure, decoration, and residual implications

### Closure

The tube panel overwhelmingly shows broad screw/flip-top closures, while the pump panel uses repeatable actuators. Public pages almost never state orifice diameter, thread, liner, tamper evidence, cap resin, opening torque, or leak-test standard. These must be quoted as part of one package system rather than mixing a sleeve quote with an assumed stock cap.

For helix:

- Prefer a **single cap family and orifice** across 30 and 120 mL when feasible, but do not force commonality if the Mini leaks, overdoses, or cannot meet PCR/closure performance.
- Require inverted storage, pressure differential, thermal cycling, drop, vibration, cap-fatigue, and e-commerce leak testing.
- Size the orifice from formula rheology and measured dose. A larger opening can erase the service-life benefit of a smaller fill.
- A foil/membrane or other first-opening feature is a product-integrity decision, not automatically required by the category; quote its material, labor, opening usability, and disposal separately.

### Decoration

The target white body can be achieved across aluminum, PE/coex, and laminate families, but the cost and recovery stream differ. Quote at least:

- direct offset/flexographic or screen print;
- matte or satin varnish/overcoat;
- color masterbatch versus white base coat;
- any hot foil or metallization as an explicit premium option;
- rub resistance after filling, crimping/tail sealing, transit, shower exposure, and repeated hand contact;
- registration and color tolerance on both diameters/lengths.

Avoid a pressure-sensitive full-body label unless its seam, bathroom durability, recyclability, and aesthetic are intentionally accepted. A sparse design does not make decoration cheap: high-contrast white, fine typography, and large unprinted fields make dust, scuffs, pinholes, registration, and color drift more visible.

### Residual product

No benchmark brand publishes a measured package evacuation percentage for the exact cleanser. “Rinses clean,” recycling instructions to rinse a pack, or consumer comments do not quantify primary-package residual. Aesop’s visible folds and Rhode’s resilient silhouette likewise do not establish usable emptying. Residual must be weighed on the exact formula/package combination and reported both as grams and percentage of net contents.

## Secondary and tertiary packaging

### Unit cartons

Verified cleanser-specific carton evidence is limited but decision-useful:

- Rhode: recyclable, FSC-certified paper carton.
- KraveBeauty Full: 100% recycled FSC paper and soy-based inks; the Mini sustainability listing names no carton.
- Dieux: 100% recycled-paper outer carton.
- Augustinus Bader: FSC-certified carton, with carton/insert/leaflet stated recyclable.

The remaining Product pages either do not identify a carton or do not publish enough Product-specific evidence. Aesop says at brand level that it avoids outer cartons where possible, but that does not establish the exact facial-paste configuration. [Aesop packaging ethos](https://www.aesop.com/sustainability.html)

For helix, the carton should be conditional, not assumed. A tube can carry mandatory information, but a carton may protect decoration, present tamper evidence, improve rectilinear pick/pack and shelf handling, and provide label area. It also adds component MOQ, print setup, assembly, cube, material, and recycling burden. Quote **tube-only with a compliant first-opening system** against **FSC carton + tube** and make the trade after transit and labeling layouts exist.

### E-commerce and tertiary

Boox calls itself a sustainable e-commerce packaging supplier and publicly lists Rhode among trusted brands. It also describes international sourcing, quality assurance, compliance/logistics oversight, and fulfillment/3PL services. That is **verified brand-level evidence only**; it does not prove Boox supplies Pineapple Refresh’s unit carton, a specific current shipper, or any primary component. [Boox](https://boox.eco/) · [Boox packaging services](https://boox.eco/pages/packaging)

No benchmark source established exact master cases, dividers, pallet patterns, pallet materials, units per case, cases per pallet, or damage rates. Those are critical to landed cost and must be requested with the component quote. A small tube can still ship inefficiently if the cap or carton creates void, if two sizes do not tessellate, or if the master case is designed around a supplier default rather than the DTC parcel.

## Service-life model and optimal fill

### Why a model is necessary

Only one daily-cleanser benchmark publishes a dose convertible to volume: Aesop’s 100 mL cream cleanser directs half a teaspoon, approximately 2.47 mL, morning and evening. Pump-count directions from Dieux, Glossier, and BYOMA are not volumetric because output per stroke is unpublished. “Small,” “proper,” “adequate,” nickel-sized, and massage directions are not convertible without orifice/usage testing.

The helix choice therefore uses a transparent sensitivity model rather than pretending competitor service life is known. The working dose range is **0.5, 0.75, and 1.0 mL per cleanse**, at one or two cleanses per day. These are design scenarios, not observed helix use.

### Mini decision

**Recommend 30 mL nominal.** At twice-daily use it spans 15–30 days across the modeled 0.5–1.0 mL dose. It is already a real cleanser Mini at KraveBeauty, is far below the TSA ceiling, and communicates trial rather than “small Full Size.”

**Hold 40 mL as the fallback.** It reproduces Rhode’s commercial little size and provides more tolerance for high-viscosity formula, larger orifice, lower evacuation, shared tube tooling, or users who double cleanse. At the 0.75 mL twice-daily design case it lasts about 27 days; at low use it drifts to 40 days.

**Do not prefer 50–60 mL for the stated role.** These sizes are common travel formats, but at the design dose they last about 33–40 days twice daily. They are excellent generous travel sizes; they are weaker 14–30-day trial sizes.

### Full Size decision

**Recommend 120 mL nominal.** At twice-daily use it lasts 60 days at 1.0 mL, 80 days at 0.75 mL, and 120 days at 0.5 mL. No single fill can guarantee 60–90 days across all behaviors, but 120 mL centers the target at the provisional dose and has independent indie and luxury precedent.

**Keep 100 mL as a cost/cube alternative.** It reaches about 67 days at 0.75 mL twice daily and is common in Beauty of Joseon, Aesop, and Augustinus Bader. It risks falling below 60 days at a 1.0 mL dose, before residual.

**Use 150 mL only if measured dose supports it or the role changes to value.** At 0.75 mL twice daily it lasts about 100 days. It fits the K-beauty/category norm and creates a stronger value story, but overshoots the brief and adds 25% more nominal formula than 120 mL.

### Validation study before locking artwork or fill

Run a four-week, at-home gravimetric study on production-intent 30 and 120 mL packs:

- at least 24 participants spanning once/twice daily use, facial hair/shaving routines, makeup/SPF removal, and hand-size/strength variation;
- weigh pack before and after every use or daily; record wet/dry-hand use, first versus second cleanse, and any re-dispense;
- calculate median, P10/P90 grams per cleanse, uses per day, and residual after ordinary evacuation;
- measure formula density so grams become mL only with Product-specific evidence;
- separately test cap/orifice alternatives because dispenser geometry changes dose;
- lock claimed duration, fill, orifice, and net-contents label only after the data.

## Regulatory and travel implications

FDA’s cosmetic labeling guide requires an accurate net-contents statement on the principal display panel and generally distinguishes fluid measure for liquids from weight for solid, semisolid, or viscous cosmetics unless established trade custom applies. U.S. customary measure is required; metric may additionally be stated. The exact formula consistency and counsel/label review should determine whether helix declares volume or net weight. [FDA cosmetic labeling guide](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-regulations/cosmetics-labeling-guide) · [FDA summary](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-regulations/summary-cosmetics-labeling-requirements)

Commercially, publish both systems when lawful and accurate—for example, **30 mL / 1.0 fl oz** and **120 mL / 4.0 fl oz** after final rounding review—because the benchmark market does. Do not convert a viscous formula label from mL to grams without density and legal review.

TSA’s standard carry-on threshold is 3.4 oz / 100 mL per container. Both 30 and 40 mL Minis are comfortably below it; 120 mL is not a carry-on liquid. A 100 mL Full may meet the nominal limit but should not be positioned as the Mini: it consumes most of the one-quart bag, adds travel weight, and does not serve the 14–30-day trial role. [TSA travel guidance](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/factsheets/tsa-travel-tips)

## Stock-capacity, landed-cost, and logistics implications

### Formula and component exposure

At equal demand, 120 mL uses **20% less formula per Full unit than 150 mL**; 30 mL uses **25% less formula per Mini than 40 mL**. Those percentages are verified arithmetic, not a landed-cost forecast. Savings must be offset against potentially less efficient standard tools, higher package cost per mL, and a lower retail value signal.

Two sizes create at least two tube bodies/SKUs and potentially separate caps, cartons, artworks, filling setups, master cases, forecasts, quality releases, and safety stock. With a 1,000–3,000 primary-container MOQ, splitting demand across Mini and Full—and again across collapsible and resilient tracks if both launch—can multiply stranded inventory. The two behavior tracks should therefore be a development competition, not four launch SKUs.

### Cube and parcel effects

Formula mass usually dominates a cleanser unit’s incremental shipping weight, but an exact claim needs measured density and finished-package mass. A 120 mL Full removes 30 mL of nominal formula relative to 150 mL; if density were approximately 1 g/mL, that would be approximately 30 g before package differences. This is an inference to validate, not a specification.

Request and compare:

- finished filled dimensions and mass;
- units per inner/master case, case dimensions and gross mass;
- cube utilization, dividers, void fill, pallet pattern, and maximum stack;
- sample and production origins, Incoterm, port, duty classification, freight mode, and domestic drayage;
- decorated-component scrap allowance, overrun/underrun, and replacement lead time;
- filler line rate and changeover loss by size/material;
- DTC parcel fit with the Core set, unit cartons, and protective materials;
- ISTA/e-commerce test outcome and leakage/damage allowance.

Do not compare supplier unit prices without these fields. A cheaper tube can be more expensive landed if it ships mostly air, needs a carton/divider, fills slowly, scuffs, leaks, has higher residual, or forces excess safety stock.

### Sustainability as a weighted objective

Avoid a single-material shortcut. The weighted comparison should include at minimum:

- virgin and PCR mass by component, with chain-of-custody evidence;
- package-to-product ratio for 30 and 120 mL;
- formula waste from measured residual;
- breakage/leakage and product-loss risk;
- transport mass and cube from production through DTC delivery;
- actual collection/sortation/end-market access in launch geographies;
- separability of cap, tube, seal, label, and coating;
- secondary and tertiary material;
- supplier energy/process evidence and defensible LCA boundaries.

Rhode and Krave show that PCR can be quantified; Dieux and Augustinus Bader show metal/glass alternatives; La Roche-Posay shows refill material reduction. None proves the lowest total impact for helix’s formula and route. Recyclable-in-principle, PCR percentage, and lighter weight answer different questions.

## RFQ and due-diligence gates

For each candidate, require one comparable response covering:

1. **Identity:** legal manufacturer, production site, broker role, stock versus custom tool, tool ownership, and transferable rights.
2. **Commercials:** price breaks at 1,000/3,000/5,000/10,000; component-by-component MOQ; decoration MOQ; setup, plate, ink, sample, mold, test, overrun, payment, and annual-volume terms; quotation validity.
3. **Schedule:** sample, compatibility, decoration proof, production, transit, replenishment, and disruption-recovery lead times.
4. **Construction:** every layer/material and thickness; PCR percentage and basis; head/shoulder, barrier, adhesive/tie, lacquer/lining, cap, liner/seal, colorant, ink, varnish, and restricted-substance declarations.
5. **Dimensions:** drawing, nominal and brimful capacity, fill range, headspace, diameter/length, orifice, thread, cap, tail seal/crimp, tolerances, and package mass.
6. **Quality:** ISO/GMP scope, cosmetic-contact compliance, change control, lot traceability, AQL/critical-defect plan, certificates of analysis/conformance, migration, odor, torque, seal, drop, vibration, pressure, and thermal-cycle evidence.
7. **Formula fit:** supplier protocol plus helix/filler-owned compatibility, sorption, permeation, stress crack, corrosion, delamination, paneling, color/odor, and accelerated/real-time aging.
8. **Operations:** filler compatibility, line speed, cleaning/changeover, seal/crimp method, coding area, inspection, reject handling, and minimum run.
9. **Performance:** controlled tube-recovery/fold test, dose distribution, usable evacuation, opening usability, wet-hand grip, bathroom stability, and transit scuff/leak tests.
10. **Logistics and sustainability:** origin, Incoterm, carton/pallet configuration, freight and duty inputs, PCR evidence, recyclability qualification by market, LCA/EPD where available, and take-back limitations.

No supplier passes because it visually matches a reference. The award should use total landed cost per **saleable, passed unit**, quality and continuity gates, behavior score, evacuation, and weighted sustainability—not the lowest ex-works component quote.

## Final recommendation and open facts

Proceed to samples and quotations with a **30 mL Mini / 120 mL Full** brief and one cap/orifice family where performance permits. Run fold-retaining and resilient bodies as competing development tracks, then select one launch track after formula compatibility, dose, evacuation, transit, consumer handling, and total-landed-cost evidence. Keep 40 mL and 100/150 mL as explicit scenario alternatives, not silent scope drift.

The following facts remain open and block a production award:

- helix formula density, rheology, surfactant/package compatibility, and actual dose;
- exact net-contents convention and label rounding for the final formula;
- tube recovery/fold target and measured residual;
- acceptable cap/orifice, leak/tamper system, and shared-component feasibility;
- whether a carton is necessary for compliance, damage, or presentation;
- supplier quotes and MOQ fit at 1,000–3,000 primary containers;
- filling-line compatibility and route;
- component and finished-goods case/pallet data;
- direct confirmation or rejection of the ICONS America and Toly Korea leads;
- Product-specific scope of the Boox/Rhode relationship;
- exact materials and supplier behind the Aesop reference and Rhode tube, if either brand elects to disclose them.

Until those facts exist, the defensible decision is a **size and test architecture**, not a named winning supplier or a claimed copy of another brand’s package.
