# Helix treatment-serum competitive and user-expectation frontier

**Research ticket:** [#289 — Map the competitive and user-expectation frontier](https://github.com/brandon-y-lee/helix/issues/289)

**Evidence checked:** 4 September 2026

**Market:** United States

**Decision use:** compare the current competitive frame for a fragrance-free Helix TREAT Product intended for younger men; inform modified-base concept development with Ecoment and Haramoon

**Status:** research input, not a Product recommendation, approved Formula, claims substantiation file, legal opinion, supplier award, or price authorization

> Prices and Product pages change. The figures below are one-time US list prices visible on the review date; temporary promotions, subscriptions, tax, and shipping are excluded. A Complete INCI or highlighted Ingredient is composition evidence, not finished-Product efficacy evidence. Retailer badges, awards, reviews, popularity, ingredient count, and the word “clinical” were not treated as efficacy proof.

## Executive readout

The market does not support “most ingredients” as a credible definition of a super serum. The clearest Products combine one instantly legible lead promise with a supporting benefit bundle: glaze plus hydration, redness plus tone and lines, concentrated hydration plus barrier support, peptides plus visible firmness, or retinal plus renewal. Peptides, niacinamide, hyaluronic-acid materials, humectants, antioxidants, ferments, botanicals, and proprietary delivery stories are now common enough that their mere presence is not distinctive.

The most decision-relevant whitespace for Helix is narrower and more useful:

- a **fragrance-free, lightweight, fast-absorbing TREAT** that leaves a controlled healthy sheen rather than either a flat matte film or a wet glaze;
- an immediate **firm-but-not-tight** after-feel paired with longer-term hydration, smoothness, tone, and visible-firmness support;
- a simple AM/PM non-retinoid route for combination, oily, blemish-prone yet dehydrated skin, with any retinal/retinoid concept clearly separated as a PM, acclimation-required alternative;
- public disclosure that distinguishes the percentage of a proprietary premix from the amount of its neat active Ingredient;
- finished-Formula testing reported with population, ages, skin tones/types, dose, frequency, duration, control, method, mean effect, responder rate, and statistics; and
- a verified airless pump whose protection, delivered dose, evacuation, compatibility, and stability are demonstrated for the exact saleable Formula and package.

The current provisional **$60–$100 / 30 mL** Helix range sits above accessible ingredient-led Products and overlaps established clinical/prestige Products. It can be plausible, but only if the exact Formula, package, substantiation, and brand experience earn the premium. An ingredient list alone does not.

## How to read the evidence

This report uses five evidence descriptions rather than one numerical “proof score”:

| Evidence description | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Finished Formula — comparatively disclosed** | Brand identifies the tested finished Product and provides useful population, duration, use, and measurement details. The full report may still be absent. |
| **Finished Formula — partial** | A finished-Product study is described, but controls, population, method, magnitude, statistics, or claim mapping are materially incomplete. |
| **Consumer perception** | Self-assessment of the finished Product. Useful for sensory and perceived outcomes; not equivalent to instrumental measurement or expert grading. |
| **Ingredient / supplier only** | Evidence or percentages concern one Ingredient, branded raw material, or supplier complex rather than the marketed finished Formula. |
| **No decision-useful study disclosed** | The reviewed official page does not provide a quantitative finished-Formula study design and outcome. This is not proof that no internal study exists. |

“Clinically tested,” “dermatologist tested,” “hypoallergenic,” “non-comedogenic,” “safe for sensitive skin,” and similar labels are recorded only as supplier assertions unless a matching protocol and result were publicly disclosed.

## Comparator selection

The set is purposive rather than a sales ranking. It spans:

1. five modern, younger-audience or ingredient-literate Products that make hydration, glaze, calm, or emerging delivery technology easy to understand;
2. nine ingredient-led and prestige treatments that define dose disclosure, peptides, retinoids/acids, proprietary complexes, and clinical-style communication; and
3. five Products from male-coded or male-relevant brands that show the current gap between grooming convention and modern facial-serum expectations.

No comparator was included because of review count, social popularity, retailer status, or awards.

## What the market establishes

### 1. One lead promise must organize a multifunctional Formula

The accessible modern group is easy to remember because each Product owns a concise lead idea:

- Rhode: dewy glaze;
- Dieux: three-in-one redness, wrinkles and spots;
- Prequel: polyglutamic-acid multi-level hydration;
- Experiment: 30% glycerin saturation; and
- Naturium: accessible “exosome” renewal.

The supporting benefits are broad, but the entry point is singular. “Everything for everyone” without this hierarchy becomes hard to explain and even harder to substantiate. Helix can retain several equal Formula concepts while requiring each to state its own dominant Product promise.

### 2. Peptides and niacinamide are category grammar, not differentiation

Peptides appear across Rhode, Dieux, Medik8, Paula’s Choice, The Ordinary, both Naturium Products, NIOD, Allies of Skin, SkinCeuticals, Drunk Elephant and Jack Black. Niacinamide appears across many of the same Products. Hyaluronic-acid materials, glycerin and squalane are also routine support Ingredients.

The competitive distinction therefore lies in:

- exact Ingredient identity and usable level;
- whether “30% complex” means a supplier premix rather than 30% pure actives;
- Formula compatibility and stability;
- Package protection and delivered dose;
- sensory execution; and
- matched finished-Formula evidence.

Ingredient count cannot answer any of those questions.

### 3. Dose disclosure is strongest in accessible ingredient-led Products, but still uneven

Useful public disclosures include Experiment’s 30% glycerin and 2% niacinamide, Prequel’s 10% glycerin and PGA system, Dieux’s separated complex loads, Geologie’s four highlighted levels, NIOD’s 1% GHK-Cu plus 1% GHK, SkinCeuticals’ 5% PHA/5% niacinamide/1% laminaria, and Youth To The People’s 0.15% retinaldehyde/5% niacinamide.

In contrast, many peptide Products name several supplier technologies without disclosing the finished-Formula level of each active. That can be legitimate formulation practice, but it cannot support a “highest strength” comparison. Helix should request exact raw-material trade names, premix use levels, active-solids concentrations, specifications, and finished-Formula assay or justified release controls privately even where consumer copy remains simpler.

### 4. Fragrance-free is the modern baseline; male-coded incumbents lag

Every Product in the five-Product younger/modern group is explicitly fragrance-free. Most clinical/prestige comparators are also fragrance-free or omit added fragrance and essential oils. By contrast, Aesop, MALIN+GOETZ and Jack Black include aromatic oils; Geologie stops at “no artificial fragrance” and acknowledges its material-derived odor.

This does not establish that every fragrant Formula is unsafe. It establishes a clear positioning and tolerability opening for a modern male brand: no parfum, masking fragrance or essential oils, plus honest disclosure that raw materials may have a faint inherent odor.

### 5. The desired Finish occupies a real middle ground

The market polarizes among Rhode’s overt wet-look glaze, Experiment’s rich dewy humectant film, Aesop’s matte finish, oil-serum richness, and Products that simply say “lightweight” without defining drydown.

Helix’s target—lightweight, non-sticky, controlled glow/sheen, visibly fresh, with a firm but non-tight feel—is not identical to those poles. It needs a quantified sensory target and comparator panel rather than adjectives alone: spread, cushion, tack at fixed timepoints, pilling under moisturizer/SPF, shine/gloss readings or trained-panel anchors, immediate tightness versus comfortable firmness, and wear across oily zones, facial hair and freshly shaved skin.

### 6. Pumps dominate, but “pump” does not mean “airless”

At least 13 of the 19 reviewed Products use or appear to use pumps, with MALIN+GOETZ's current dispenser unresolved; droppers/pipettes remain common in legacy or clinical-coded prestige. Dieux communicates recycled package construction; Experiment communicates refill/recycled-plastic details; most brands do not publicly prove oxygen ingress, light protection, dose repeatability, evacuation, leakage, Formula contact materials or end-of-life reality.

For Helix, an airless pump is a defensible direction only after the supplier provides the exact system identity and the final Formula passes package compatibility, stability, leakage, dose, evacuation and distribution work. The word “airless” or an opaque bottle is not proof.

### 7. Retinal and acid routes buy broader claims with real routine friction

Youth To The People’s 0.15% retinaldehyde route is PM-only, starts once weekly and requires acclimation plus daily SPF. Drunk Elephant’s 10% lactic-acid Formula carries limited-use and extended sunscreen guidance. The Ordinary publishes extensive incompatibility guidance for its peptide serum despite its AM/PM cadence.

These are poor templates for promising that every high-performance Formula is a universal, frictionless AM/PM layer. A Helix retinoid route can remain one coequal concept, but its cadence, incompatibilities, warning architecture and evidence burden must remain separate from non-retinoid concepts intended to coexist with a Customer’s external prescription-retinoid Routine.

### 8. Public evidence quality is itself a competitive surface

Dieux most clearly connects tested Formula, cohort, timepoint, measurement class and the exact visible outcome. Caldera’s campaign pages disclose male cohort sizes, ages, durations and instruments, although its primary Product page does not consolidate that evidence and the cohort skews older than Helix’s target. Allies of Skin discloses multiple methods and timepoints but wraps one result in an easily misread “reverse aging” headline. Other pages range from small consumer-perception studies to vague “clinically proven” or supplier-data language.

Helix can differentiate without making the largest numerical claim. A public evidence card should identify:

1. exact finished Formula and final package tested;
2. sample size and completed sample;
3. participant ages, sexes/genders where collected, Fitzpatrick types/skin tones and relevant Skin Types;
4. exclusion criteria and whether facial hair, shaving and prescription actives were represented;
5. dose, frequency, Routine and duration;
6. control, split-face design or comparator when applicable;
7. instrument, expert scale and/or questionnaire;
8. baseline, mean/median magnitude, responder threshold, confidence interval and statistics;
9. adverse events, withdrawals and tolerability; and
10. exact claim wording supported by each endpoint.

Consumer perception can validly support perceived hydration, absorption and Finish. It should not be relabeled as measured collagen, barrier repair, tissue regeneration or biological age reversal.

## Implications for modified ODM bases

This market evidence supports a disciplined retain/remove/substitute/add review rather than combining every attractive feature across unrelated bases.

For each Ecoment or Haramoon candidate, the next concept dossier should record:

1. **Retain:** the validated chassis, preservation system, sensory structure, delivery system and Ingredients that already serve the concept’s dominant promise.
2. **Remove:** fragrance/essential oils, duplicative headline Ingredients, unsupported drug-adjacent claims, and any material that blocks the agreed tolerance or positioning target—only where supplier stability and challenge work permits.
3. **Substitute:** a lower-conflict Ingredient or package only when function, compatibility, preservation and new evidence requirements are explicit.
4. **Add:** at most the few Ingredients needed to close a documented benefit/evidence gap, with raw-material use level, active-solids basis and claim purpose defined.
5. **Requalify:** treat any change that disturbs preservation, pH, rheology, phase structure, delivery system or package interaction as a new Formula requiring proportionate stability, microbiology, compatibility, safety and claim work.

The three coequal concepts do not need the same hero Ingredients or the same cadence. They do need the same evidence accounting, sensory target, blacklist logic, package diligence and commercial model so the board can compare them without a hidden methodological advantage.

## Decision-ready conclusions

1. **Keep several equal concepts, but force one dominant promise per concept.** A multifunctional Formula still needs an understandable front door.
2. **Make fragrance-free and the controlled glow/firm-feel sensory target common to all concepts.** Those are clearer Helix differentiators than adding another undosed peptide.
3. **Treat 30 mL as a valid prestige norm, not an automatic value.** Larger 40–60 mL competitors reset value expectations; delivered uses and cost per use should accompany MSRP.
4. **Prefer pump feasibility, not pump theater.** Verify airlessness, protection, dose, evacuation and compatibility for each exact Formula.
5. **Do not borrow the simplicity promise across retinoid and non-retinoid concepts.** Retinal/retinoid and high-acid routes need different cadence, caution and evidence.
6. **Use Dieux and Caldera’s more specific evidence disclosures as communication benchmarks, not proof that their Ingredients or Formulas are superior.** Helix can improve further by publishing effect magnitude and statistics.
7. **Do not hero “exosome” language from catalog copy alone.** Accessible and prestige competitors already use the term with unresolved identity and delivery details; documentation is the differentiation.
8. **Research MSRP and landed COGS rather than inheriting the workbook guesses.** The current $60–$100 hypothesis spans materially different market positions.

## What this research cannot establish

- Official Product pages show the expectations brands are attempting to create; they do not establish which benefits, formats, or prices younger men actually prioritize. Interviews, concept testing and willingness-to-pay work remain separate inputs.
- Manufacturer sensory copy does not replace blind physical sampling on Helix's intended users or trained-panel assessment against defined Texture and Finish anchors.
- List price and normalized price do not establish realized selling price, sales velocity, repeat purchase, willingness to pay, COGS, gross margin or commercial success.
- Public Ingredient and study disclosures do not permit efficacy ranking across unlike Formulas, populations, endpoints, instruments, durations and controls.
- This scan does not establish freedom to operate, trademark availability, regulatory compliance, raw-material availability or whether an ODM can reproduce a competitor's performance without infringing protected rights.

## Source and interpretation controls

- All competitor facts above come from the linked brand-owned US Product, policy, evidence or update pages checked on 4 September 2026.
- Product-page language is reported as the brand’s representation, not adopted as a Helix Product Fact.
- A Complete INCI establishes declared composition at the time of publication; it does not disclose every raw-material concentration, impurity, process, supplier, specification or stability result.
- “No parfum named” is not upgraded to “fragrance-free” unless the brand makes that claim; essential oils and material-derived odor are described separately.
- Brand-sponsored finished-Product studies can be decision-useful while still requiring the full signed report, protocol, statistical output and exact Formula identity before Helix relies on them.
