# Mei Pelle Core and PROTECT formulation portfolios

**Research ticket:** [#128 — Compare cohesive Core and PROTECT formulation portfolios](https://github.com/brandon-y-lee/mei-pelle/issues/128)

**Decision date:** 9 August 2026

**Decision status:** research recommendation; not a supplier order, safety sign-off, claims approval, or U.S. sunscreen launch authorization

**Market:** United States direct-to-consumer

**Formula constraint:** evaluate the documented fixed Formulas. Any changes are separated as future supplier suggestions.

> This is cosmetic Product strategy, not medical advice. The U.S. sunscreen analysis is a research classification against current public FDA materials, not a legal opinion. Final Formula, safety, labeling, claims, and OTC-drug decisions require the signed supplier dossier and qualified regulatory review.

## Executive decision

The strongest low-friction Core is:

1. **CLEANSE — Calming Biotics Gel Cleanser**
2. **TREAT — PDRN 5% Active Ampoule**
3. **SEAL — Calming Biotics Intensive Cream**

This is the most coherent available fixed-Formula system because the cleanser is positioned as a low-pH, non-tight daily wash; the ampoule supplies one distinctive tone-and-texture story in a light layer; and the cream supplies the clearest documented emollient, humectant, oat, centella, ferment, fatty-acid, and ceramide support. It avoids a daily stack of multiple exfoliating or acne-targeted Products. The known TREAT and SEAL ingredient lists do not name fragrance or essential oils. The central concept is **calm cleanse → concentrated appearance care → moisture retention**, with PROTECT as the essential daytime extension.

The recommendation is conditional, not ready for production release:

- The current Korean Gel Cleanser page supplies a complete ingredient disclosure, but the proposed OEM Formula still needs a signed identity match, measured pH, surfactant active-matter specification, LHA concentration, and tolerance data. Sodium C14-16 olefin sulfonate is second in that public list, and ethanol and LHA are also present, so “low pH” does not by itself establish gentle post-shave use.
- The PDRN ampoule needs exact PDRN/Sodium DNA identity and origin, active-solids basis for “5%,” molecular-weight and purity specifications, residual-protein/allergen documentation, and finished-Product claim support. The private supplier material describes the PDRN line as salmon-derived, while the current public Product page says only “Sodium DNA.”
- The Intensive Cream's public page claims a 3:1:1 ceramide/cholesterol/fatty-acid system, but its published INCI does not name cholesterol. That inconsistency must be resolved before repeating the ratio or “barrier repair” story.

This recommendation intentionally replaces the current Green Collagen Hydrate Boosting Cream as the default SEAL. Its complete Korean disclosure names bergamot, rosemary, spearmint, litsea cubeba, and clary oils, and it does not name the niacinamide highlighted on the U.S. page. That does not make the Product intrinsically unsafe; it makes it a poorer broadly usable, shave-aware default and leaves the exact market/OEM version unresolved.

For **PROTECT**, the best candidate is **Comfort Barrier Sun Cream SPF50+ PA++++**, but only as a **plausible-but-unverified U.S. OTC path**. Its current Korean ingredient disclosure identifies zinc oxide as the only sunscreen active, which could fit current OTC Monograph M020 if the exact concentration is no more than 25% and every other monograph, testing, labeling, manufacturing, registration, and listing condition is met. No exact U.S. Drug Facts label, active concentration, U.S. SPF/broad-spectrum report, current drug listing, or manufacturing-compliance dossier was located. It therefore must not enter a sellable U.S. Catalog merely because the Korean Product says SPF50+ PA++++.

No evaluated Leaders face sunscreen has an **established** U.S. launch path on the evidence reviewed:

- **Comfort Barrier:** plausible but unverified; zinc-only filter disclosure, missing concentration and U.S. dossier.
- **SUNBUDDY Daily Moisture:** plausible but unverified; publicly listed filters are monograph actives, but concentrations and exact version are unresolved, and public sources conflict with the supplier deck on size and PA grade.
- **Water Drop Essence:** unavailable through the ordinary monograph route as the documented fixed Formula because it includes several filters not in current M020.
- **Glow Tone Up:** unavailable through the ordinary monograph route as the documented fixed Formula for the same reason, despite also containing permitted zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, and bemotrizinol.

The commercially honest near-term decision is therefore:

- approve the recommended three-Product Core for supplier-dossier diligence;
- keep PROTECT in the Catalog architecture and brand story;
- do **not** publish a U.S. sunscreen Product for sale until one exact Formula passes the OTC gate;
- lead the age-support story with consistent daily UV protection only after that gate, because sunscreen has materially stronger direct age-preservation evidence than the topical PDRN Formula.

## What was evaluated

### Evidence set

The assessment triangulates four source classes:

1. **Canonical Catalog snapshot.** The current Mei Pelle Catalog was inspected read-only on 9 August 2026. It contains the Calming Biotics Gel Cleanser, PDRN 5% Active Ampoule, and Green Collagen Hydrate Boosting Cream as the current CLEANSE, TREAT, and SEAL basis. No project identifier or private provider payload is reproduced here.
2. **Private supplier source.** The 51-page bilingual Leaders Product deck dated 13 March 2026 was rendered and visually inspected page by page. It is image-only: text extraction returned no substantive text, and no fillable fields were found. Only decision-relevant Product facts are summarized here; the private file location and nonessential deck content are intentionally omitted.
3. **Exact Product research.** Each finalist, elimination candidate, and credible face PROTECT candidate was checked against its exact public Product page or, when Leaders did not provide a usable public page, the best available exact-Product ingredient source. Public retail availability was not used as an elimination criterion because the OEM relationship may permit a production restart.
4. **Claims and regulatory evidence.** Current FDA/FTC materials and original human studies were used for claim boundaries, ingredient-level plausibility, shaving context, moisturization, photoaging, and the sunscreen route.

### Evidence labels used below

| Label | Meaning | What it can support |
|---|---|---|
| **Exact Formula fact** | Complete or partial ingredient disclosure and use/texture facts for the exact named Product | Formula comparison; not efficacy by itself |
| **Finished-Product evidence** | Human testing on the exact Formula, dose, population, use, duration, and outcome | A matched claim, after full report and legal review |
| **Ingredient-level evidence** | Human research on the ingredient in another Formula, often at a known concentration | Biological or cosmetic plausibility; not a “clinically proven Product” claim |
| **Supplier assertion** | Deck or public marketing statement without the underlying report | A diligence lead only |
| **Inference** | Formulation or routine interpretation from the disclosed facts | Product strategy; must be stated as inference |

Scores in the matrices are comparative decision aids, not clinical safety or efficacy grades. The weighted score is: routine cohesion 25%, broad/shave fit 20%, documentation 20%, sensitivity/fragrance profile 15%, texture/finish 10%, and option differentiation 10%. **A hard evidence or regulatory gate overrides the score.**

## Recommended portfolio and cadence

### System A — Resilient age-support Core (recommended)

| Step | Product | Job in the system | Why it belongs |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLEANSE | Calming Biotics Gel Cleanser | Remove daily buildup and the prior sunscreen film without a tight finish | Best broad-use cleanser positioning; low-pH and soft gel-to-foam claims; no unnecessary second treatment story |
| TREAT | PDRN 5% Active Ampoule | The single signature tone, texture, hydration, and fine-line-appearance layer | Complete public INCI; light finish; 50,000 ppm Sodium DNA plus niacinamide, humectants, adenosine, allantoin, and peptides |
| SEAL | Calming Biotics Intensive Cream | Reduce dry/tight feel and retain moisture, particularly at night and after shaving | Best documented barrier-oriented composition; rich but publicly described as non-greasy; no named fragrance or essential oil in the published INCI |
| PROTECT | Comfort Barrier Sun Cream, conditional | Last AM step; U.S.-compliant broad-spectrum protection if and only if the exact U.S. OTC dossier is complete | Zinc-only public filter disclosure offers the cleanest plausible monograph route; Formula is otherwise aligned with niacinamide, oat, centella, panthenol, and humectant story |

### Simple customer routine

**Morning**

1. Rinse with water, or use CLEANSE when oil/buildup warrants it.
2. Apply TREAT to intact skin.
3. Apply a thin SEAL layer only when needed; oily users may skip it if the future PROTECT is sufficiently moisturizing.
4. Apply a verified U.S. Broad Spectrum SPF Product in the labeled amount. Reapply according to its Drug Facts.

**Evening**

1. CLEANSE. If a high-resistance sunscreen is difficult to remove, a separate first-cleanse Product can be tested, but it should not make the baseline three-step routine mandatory-double-cleanse.
2. Apply TREAT.
3. Apply SEAL; use less in warm/humid conditions and more on dry zones.

**Tolerability ramp**

None of the recommended disclosed Formulas contains a retinoid, strong leave-on acid, or other ingredient that inherently establishes a clinical cycling schedule. A conservative adoption pattern is still sensible: introduce one Product at a time, begin TREAT once daily or every other night for the first week, then increase only if comfortable. This is a tolerability protocol, not evidence that “5% PDRN” behaves like a potent drug active.

**Shave days**

Electrical shaving transiently increased facial transepidermal water loss and perfusion in a controlled human study, with most measured parameters returning toward baseline by 24 hours ([Shin et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38951871/)). That supports a practical, not medical, routine rule: use the gentlest wash, avoid repeated scrubbing or acne-foam/LHA exposure on freshly abraded areas, apply simple moisture layers only if they do not sting, and follow the eventual sunscreen Drug Facts instruction not to apply to damaged or broken skin. Supplier testing should explicitly include clean-shaven faces, beard stubble, razor nicks, and neck application.

### 1. Calming Biotics Gel Cleanser — Core hero

The current [official Leaders U.S. page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/copy-of-leaders-calming-biotics-cream-mask-80ml) describes a 200 mL low-pH gel-to-foam cleanser that rinses without a tight or greasy feel and is intended for AM/PM use. The [current official Korean Product page](https://m.leaderscosmetics.com/product/detail.html?cate_no=0&display_group=0&product_no=877) supplies the fuller ingredient evidence: water, sodium C14-16 olefin sulfonate, cocamidopropyl hydroxysultaine, glycerin, sodium cocoyl alaninate, trehalose, allantoin, ferments and centella materials, later-listed ethanol and jojoba oil, and capryloyl salicylic acid (LHA) last. No fragrance or essential oil is named. Public market pages are useful evidence, but only a signed supplier master can establish that the OEM Formula is identical.

**Cohesion.** This is the best match to the PDRN ampoule and richer cream because it does not make the user start with an acne or clay treatment. A mildly cleansing, low-residue first step should protect adherence and leave the signature benefit to TREAT. The gel texture and larger size also support a direct, masculine-but-not-aggressive daily ritual.

**Sensitivity and shave context.** “Low pH,” “sulfate-free,” and “for sensitive skin” remain supplier assertions until the signed specification and studies are reviewed. Sodium C14-16 olefin sulfonate is not a sulfate, but its second-place position makes surfactant active matter and finished-Product tolerability decision-relevant. LHA is an exfoliating salicylic-acid derivative, and the list also contains ethanol; their concentrations, rinse-off exposure, and combined irritation profile are unknown. Do not promise that the cleanser cannot strip, never irritates, or is safe immediately after shaving.

**Hard gates.** Signed master Formula and revision matched to the public Korean list, measured finished-Product pH and range through shelf life, surfactant system and active-matter level, LHA and ethanol concentrations, fragrance/essential-oil confirmation, eye-sting and sensitive-skin reports, preservative/challenge testing, and cleansing/after-feel data.

### 2. Calming Clear Milk Peel Cleanse Balm — sunscreen-removal option

The exact [Leaders Product page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/calming-clear-milk-peel-cleanse-balm) publishes a complete INCI led by emollient esters and an emulsifier, followed by bamboo stem powder, polyethylene, lavender oil, bergamot fruit oil, several plant oils, and chitosan. Leaders positions it as a balm-to-oil-to-pilling/exfoliating cleanser.

**Differentiation.** It has a real job once PROTECT exists: breaking down persistent sunscreen, sebum, and makeup in an evening first cleanse. It is meaningfully different from the gel cleanser and can be an optional “deep remove” choice.

**Why it is not the Core hero.** The physical-pilling concept adds friction and a second exfoliation signal to a routine already containing LHA in CLEANSE. Lavender and bergamot oils conflict with the preference for a broadly usable fragrance/essential-oil-minimized Core, particularly around shaving. Chitosan's source is not disclosed. The current public page also makes acne, scarring, eczema, and wrinkle claims that Mei Pelle should not inherit; those claims cross into disease or structure/function territory without a matched U.S. drug path and finished-Product substantiation.

**Hard gates.** Essential-oil allergen and phototoxicity specifications, chitosan source, eye-area removal data, sunscreen-removal test against the selected PROTECT, microplastic/material status for polyethylene, and irritation testing on shaved/sensitive skin. It should remain optional even if those gates pass.

### 3. Calming Clear Acne Foam Cleanser — oily/blemish specialist

The exact [Leaders Product page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/leaders-calming-clear-acne-foam-cleanser) lists water, glycerin, palmitic/stearic/lauric/myristic acids and potassium hydroxide near the top, plus amphoteric and glycinate surfactants, beeswax, salicylic acid, alcohol, panthenol, allantoin, centella components, and botanicals.

**Differentiation.** It is the clearest oily/blemish choice and has a complete public ingredient list. It could serve customers who actively prefer a dense foam and stronger sebum-removal feel.

**Why it is not broad Core.** The fatty-acid/potassium-hydroxide system is consistent with in-situ soap formation; without a measured pH, it is a formulation inference—but one that raises a credible concern about a higher-pH, more degreasing wash than the gel cleanser. Salicylic-acid concentration is undisclosed, and the Formula combines salicylic acid, alcohol, and a dense foam concept. That makes twice-daily use and immediate post-shave use a poor default. Beeswax also prevents a vegan claim.

**Hard gates.** Finished pH, free-alkali specification, salicylic concentration and regulatory purpose, sebum/cleansing study, irritation and eye-sting data, and an exact U.S. claim decision. If sold only as a cosmetic cleanser, do not use “treats acne,” “prevents breakouts,” or antibacterial claims.

### Elimination: Milk Sponge White Mud Pack to Foam

The exact [Leaders Product page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/leaders-milk-sponge-white-mud-pack-to-foam) lists 60,000 ppm kaolin, 29,700 ppm bentonite, a fatty-acid/potassium-hydroxide cleansing base, quartz, milk ferment/protein, and LHA. It offers a distinct clay-mask-to-foam experience, but its oil absorption, soap-forming base, mineral particles, and exfoliation signal are mismatched to an everyday low-friction Core. It is a credible future weekly congestion/texture Product, not one of the three daily CLEANSE finalists.

### 1. PDRN 5% Active Ampoule — signature TREAT

The current [official Leaders Product page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/pdrn-5-active-ampoule) publishes the exact 30 mL INCI: water, dipropylene glycol, butylene glycol, glycerin, propanediol, Sodium DNA at 50,000 ppm, 1,2-hexanediol, niacinamide, trehalose, emulsifier/thickener, allantoin, preservation aids, adenosine, EDTA, and six named peptides. No fragrance or essential oil is named. Leaders describes a light, fast-absorbing, non-sticky finish for AM/PM layering.

**Cohesion.** This is the only available TREAT with enough distinctiveness to anchor the system without adding retinoid-style cadence complexity. Humectants, allantoin, niacinamide, adenosine, and peptides complement the richer SEAL. The Formula has no disclosed exfoliating acid, retinoid, or benzoyl peroxide, so the recommended system avoids stacked leave-on irritation.

**Evidence.** A 2026 randomized, double-blind, split-face study found short-term periocular improvements with a 0.1% PDRN-850K eye cream versus a 0.1% retinol comparator, but it studied a defined molecular-weight material, a different concentration, an eye-cream vehicle, 31 participants, and 28 days—not this ampoule ([Ye et al., 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0350905)). The authors also note that early ultrasound findings may partly reflect hydration/plumping and that longer studies are needed. A separate 5% niacinamide split-face trial in 50 women reported improvements in several visible photoaging endpoints over 12 weeks, but the ampoule's niacinamide concentration is undisclosed ([Bissett et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16029679/)). Adenosine-containing Products have randomized evidence for skin-smoothness/wrinkle appearance, again in different Formulas and at disclosed study conditions ([Abella, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18489289/)). These studies create ingredient-level plausibility, not finished-Product proof.

**PDRN provenance and animal-derived uncertainty.** The private supplier deck describes high-purity salmon PDRN; the public Formula only says Sodium DNA. Those facts should not be silently merged. “PDRN,” “5%,” species/tissue, molecular weight, purity, and active solids must all be tied to the exact OEM Formula. Until then:

- do not call the Product vegan or plant-derived;
- do not imply that topical use reproduces injectable PDRN outcomes;
- do not say it regenerates cells, repairs DNA, heals skin, creates collagen, reverses aging, or preserves youth;
- do not make a fish-allergy safety assurance—request residual protein and allergen documentation instead.

**Hard gates.** Signed Formula/INCI and revision, relationship between Sodium DNA and the trade name PDRN, source species and tissue, extraction and purification, average/range molecular weight, active-solids basis of 50,000 ppm, residual protein/endotoxin/bioburden/heavy-metal specifications, fish-allergen assessment, animal-origin declarations, stability, package compatibility, and the full finished-Product clinical report behind any visible-result claim.

### 2. Calming Biotics Blemish Serum — clarity alternative

The exact [Leaders Product page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/calming-biotics-ampoule-cooler-kit-copy) lists a water/glycol/centella base with betaine, niacinamide, multiple ferment lysates, squalane, glycerin, panthenol, centella actives, beta-glucan, Ceramide NP, a small amount of ascorbic acid, and sunflower oil. No fragrance or essential oil is named. The public list appears to contain typographic/transcription issues such as “12-Hexanediol,” reinforcing the need for a signed regulatory INCI.

**Differentiation.** This is not “PDRN-lite.” It is the better alternative for customers prioritizing the appearance of post-blemish uneven tone, comfortable hydration, and a lighter barrier-support story. It is also the better TREAT fallback if PDRN provenance or animal-origin positioning is unacceptable, subject to supplier confirmation of every ferment/feedstock source.

**Overlap and caution.** Niacinamide appears in both this serum and several SEAL/PROTECT candidates. That is not automatically unsafe, but it weakens the value of offering multiple niacinamide-heavy options without clear texture or benefit differences. The Leaders page's “targets acne bacteria,” breakout-prevention, inflammation, and repair language should not be inherited as cosmetic copy. Ascorbic acid appears late in the list, and its concentration, pH, stability, and contribution are unknown; it should not be marketed as a high-potency vitamin C treatment.

**Hard gates.** Authoritative INCI, niacinamide and ascorbic-acid percentages, finished pH, ferment identity/source and preservation, sensitive/blemish-prone skin report, non-comedogenic methodology if that claim is desired, and exact finished-Product tone/mark endpoints.

### 3. First Shot Active Essence Age Control — low-step alternative

The exact [Leaders Product page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/first-shot-active-essence-age-control) lists 94% silkworm extract first, followed by betaine, water, texture agents, glycerin, ferment, sodium hyaluronate, adenosine, sodium mannose phosphate, and related excipients. Leaders positions the 150 mL Product as a toner/essence/serum hybrid. That exact large-format page remains public, but the current Korean storefront evidence located during this review showed only a 30 mL mini; an OEM restart and the intended fill size therefore require supplier confirmation.

**Differentiation.** Its real advantage is behavioral: one large-format liquid can replace preparation and serum layers. It fits a customer who values low friction more than a “potent active” story. The Formula does not name fragrance or essential oils in the published list.

**Why it ranks third.** It is explicitly animal-derived and therefore does not resolve the PDRN origin issue. Public “Agefinity,” cell-reshaping, elasticity, resilience, and age-control language is not supported by a located finished-Product report. Its toner/essence identity is less concentrated and less clearly TREAT-like than the ampoule and blemish serum. Keep claims to hydration and visible smoothness unless exact data support more.

**Hard gates.** Silkworm species/source and allergen/protein dossier, exact 94% basis, finished-Product study, vegan/animal-origin labeling decision, stability, and U.S. cosmetic claim review.

### Reserve: Green Collagen Moist Firming Serum

The current [official Leaders U.S. page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/green-collagen-moist-firming-serum) describes a 30 mL lightweight serum with plant-derived “phyto-lacto collagen,” centella-derived Lactobacillus, sodium hyaluronate, and adenosine, and calls it vegan and clinically tested for comfort. The [official Korean Product page](https://leaderscosmetics.com/product/detail.html?cate_no=0&display_group=0&product_no=840) provides a complete ingredient list that includes bergamot, rosemary, spearmint, litsea cubeba, and clary oils alongside humectants, panthenol, allantoin, adenosine, sodium hyaluronate, collagen/ferment materials, and Ceramide NP. The public clinical report and certification scope were not located.

That evidence resolves the fragrance screen against this Formula: it is not an EO-free alternative. It remains a strategically useful plant-forward, light-texture reserve for customers who knowingly accept fragrant botanicals, but it should not displace the fragrance/EO-free disclosed PDRN or Blemish Serum options in a broadly usable Core. Signed OEM identity, essential-oil/allergen specifications, raw-material origin declarations, vegan certification scope, and the complete exact-Product study are still hard gates.

### No credible high-potency cadence alternative yet

The supplier deck includes stronger-sounding mask and “retinol” concepts, but no exact fixed Formula with a complete ingredient disclosure, quantified retinoid, finished-Product evidence, and routine-compatible leave-on format was found that could responsibly become a stronger Core TREAT. A sheet mask or undefined “phyto-retinol” Product is not a substitute for a documented retinoid serum. The correct decision is to acknowledge the gap, not manufacture a potency ladder from Product names.

### 1. Calming Biotics Intensive Cream — Core hero

The exact [Leaders Product page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/calming-biotics-intensive-cream) publishes a full INCI led by water, glycols, glycerin, emollient esters, fatty alcohols, a ferment filtrate, silicone, olive-derived emulsifiers, oat extract, centella, hydrogenated lecithin, Lactobacillus ferment, Ceramide AP, phytosterols, palmitic and stearic acids, tocopherol, and peptides. No fragrance or essential oil is named. Leaders describes a thick, rich, non-greasy cream for dry, sensitive, or compromised-feeling skin.

**Cohesion and shave context.** This Formula gives the system what the light ampoule does not: substantial emollience and occlusive feel. Human moisturizer studies in other ceramide-containing Formulas support the general proposition that properly formulated moisturizers can improve hydration and transepidermal water loss, but results are Formula-specific ([Lueangarun et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31585489/); [Danby et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34921679/)). The exact cream still needs its own data. Its lack of named fragrance/essential oils and inclusion of glycerin, oat, emollients, fatty components, and ceramide make it the best post-shave *candidate* among the documented SEAL Formulas—not proof that it cannot irritate.

**Critical inconsistency.** The public page claims a patented 3:1:1 “ceramides, cholesterol, and fatty acids” ratio and 100-hour moisture. The published INCI names Ceramide AP, phytosterols, palmitic acid, and stearic acid, but not cholesterol. Phytosterols should not be silently relabeled as cholesterol. Do not use the ratio, “100-hour,” “barrier repair,” or penetration claims until Leaders supplies the exact patent/license reference, composition calculation, current signed INCI, and complete study reports.

**Texture tradeoff.** This may be too rich under a mineral sunscreen for oily users or humid climates. It can remain the Core SEAL if the routine explicitly permits a thin layer or AM skip. Pilling, sunscreen-film integrity, white-cast interaction, and beard/stubble behavior must be tested with the exact PROTECT.

### 2. Green Collagen Balancing Emulsion — light option

The [official Leaders U.S. Product page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/collections/whats-new/products/green-collagen-balancing-emulsion) describes a 150 mL rich-yet-breathable emulsion with plant-derived phytocollagen, squalane, and Ceramide NP for dry, dehydrated, and combination skin. Its page has visible content-reference artifacts. The [official Korean ingredient disclosure](https://m.leaderscosmetics.com/product/zoom.html?product_no=1112) is more decision-useful: it names bergamot, rosemary, litsea cubeba, spearmint, and clary oils. A signed master is still required to establish which market Formula Leaders proposes for OEM production.

**Differentiation.** The high-volume emulsion is the clearest **Light** SEAL for combination skin, warm weather, or customers who dislike a dense cream. That texture job is meaningfully different from Intensive Cream as **Rich**.

**Why it is not first.** The five named essential oils make it a poor broadly usable or immediate post-shave default, even though its texture may be lighter. It should be an informed fragrance-tolerant option, not an unexplained variant or sensitive-skin recommendation. Hard gates are the signed market-version match, essential-oil and declarable-allergen specifications, raw-material origin/vegan scope, complete tolerance and hydration reports, and head-to-head wear testing against Intensive Cream.

### 3. Green Collagen Hydrate Boosting Cream — balanced option

The current [official Leaders U.S. page](https://www.leaderscosmeticsusa.com/products/green-collagen-hydrate-boosting-cream) describes a 50 mL rich but non-heavy cream for dry, normal, and combination routines and highlights plant-derived green collagen, sodium hyaluronate, panthenol, and niacinamide. The [current official Korean ingredient list](https://leaderscosmetics.com/product/detail.html?product_no=841) names bergamot, rosemary, spearmint, litsea cubeba, and clary oils; it also lists squalane, meadowfoam seed oil, panthenol, allantoin, collagen/ferment materials, shea butter, and Ceramide NP—but does **not** name niacinamide. The U.S. and Korean public descriptions therefore cannot be assumed to represent one identical Formula.

## PROTECT: exact Formula and U.S. path

### Classification method

For this report:

- **Established** means public evidence was found tying the exact current Formula to a compliant U.S. OTC path: monograph-permitted active(s) at disclosed concentrations, current U.S. Drug Facts/labeling, appropriate exact-finished-Product testing, and credible manufacturing/registration/listing evidence.

### The U.S. OTC launch gate

A Korean Formula is not converted into a U.S. sunscreen by changing the carton. FDA treats sunscreens as drugs. Imported OTC drugs must have either an approved application or conform to the applicable monograph and other requirements; foreign establishments must register, distributed drugs must be listed, labels must comply with 21 CFR part 201, and drugs must be manufactured under cGMP ([FDA, Importing OTC Drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-imports/importing-over-counter-drugs)). Registration or listing is not FDA approval.

Before a PROTECT Product becomes sellable, obtain and independently review:

1. **Exact Formula identity** — signed master Formula and revision; every sunscreen active's U.S. established name and percentage; inactive ingredients; color additives; raw-material specifications; batch link between test samples and commercial Formula.
2. **Monograph fit or approved application** — active concentrations, combinations, dosage form, indications, directions, warnings, and all current M020/general OTC conditions. A Product containing a non-M020 sunscreen active needs a different lawful path; a cosmetic INCI list cannot hide that active.
3. **Exact finished-Product testing** — U.S. SPF in-vivo test; U.S. Broad Spectrum in-vitro critical-wavelength test; 40- or 80-minute water-resistance test only if claimed; photostability where relevant; stability across shelf life and packaging; micro/preservative and safety testing. FDA's guidance explains that the SPF and Broad Spectrum tests support the corresponding U.S. claims and that water resistance must be established as 40 or 80 minutes ([FDA sunscreen labeling/testing guide](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/labeling-and-effectiveness-testing-sunscreen-drug-products-over-counter-human-use-small-entity)).
4. **U.S. label and listing** — principal display panel, exact Broad Spectrum SPF statement, Drug Facts with active names/percentages, uses, warnings, directions, other information, inactive ingredients, responsible firm/contact, and submitted labeling. SPF50+ PA++++ artwork is not a U.S. Drug Facts panel.
5. **Manufacturing and import readiness** — cGMP records, quality agreement, foreign drug establishment registration and U.S. agent, drug listing/NDC/SPL, batch release, deviations/OOS/CAPA, complaint and adverse-event processes, traceability, recall plan, and importer roles.
6. **Wear and system compatibility** — validated application dose; white cast across a diverse panel; eye sting; sweat; beard/stubble accumulation; collar transfer; pilling; finish over each SEAL; impact of layering on film formation; and removal with CLEANSE.

An exact public Drug Facts/NDC/listing record for the four named Products was not located in the sources reviewed. That absence is an evidence gap, not proof that no private registration or unpublished work exists; Leaders must supply current records.

## What the evidence can truthfully say about age support

### Evidence hierarchy for this Catalog

| Proposition | Best evidence found | What Mei Pelle may infer | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily sunscreen helps limit visible photoaging | A randomized community trial of 903 adults found less skin aging in daily broad-spectrum sunscreen users than discretionary users over the study period ([Hughes et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23732711/)) | PROTECT is the routine's strongest evidence-backed long-horizon age-support behavior, once the exact Product is lawfully substantiated and labeled | That any Korean SPF/PA claim establishes a U.S. Product's performance |
| 5% niacinamide can improve several visible facial-aging endpoints | Double-blind split-face study in 50 women over 12 weeks ([Bissett et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16029679/)) | Niacinamide is a plausible contributor to tone/texture appearance | The undisclosed niacinamide level in an exact Leaders Formula reproduces that trial |
| Adenosine Formulas can improve measured skin smoothness/wrinkle appearance | Randomized placebo-controlled facial study in 126 women with specific adenosine cream/film Formulas ([Abella, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18489289/)) | Adenosine supports a carefully qualified appearance story | That every adenosine Product has the same effect or that stacking Products increases it |
| Defined topical PDRN may affect visible periocular endpoints | One 2026 short randomized split-face trial of 0.1% PDRN-850K eye cream versus 0.1% retinol ([Ye et al., 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0350905)) | Topical PDRN is no longer purely an injectable/in-vitro story; a defined topical material has emerging human evidence | That Sodium DNA 50,000 ppm in this ampoule is PDRN-850K, penetrates similarly, or has the same vehicle, dose, endpoint, durability, or benefit |
| Moisturizers can improve hydration and barrier measurements | Formula-specific randomized studies of ceramide/lipid moisturizers ([Lueangarun et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31585489/); [Danby et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34921679/)) | A well-formulated SEAL can reduce dry feel and support moisture retention | The Intensive Cream's 3:1:1 ratio, 100-hour claim, or exact performance without its reports |

### Cosmetic claim lane

FDA determines Product category from intended use and the net message, not from whether copy sounds elegant. A cosmetic can cleanse, moisturize, beautify, and alter appearance; claims to regenerate cells, increase collagen production, treat disease, or otherwise affect body structure/function can make it a drug ([FDA, “Is It a Cosmetic, a Drug, or Both?”](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap); [FDA, wrinkle and anti-aging Products](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/wrinkle-treatments-and-other-anti-aging-products)). FTC also expects objective health-related claims to be truthful, nonmisleading, and supported before publication; ingredient studies do not automatically substantiate the marketed finished Product ([FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)).

**Appropriate cosmetic lane, subject to exact substantiation**

- cleanses away surface oil, sunscreen, and daily buildup;
- leaves skin feeling fresh, comfortable, soft, or not tight;
- hydrates and helps reduce the look of dryness;
- skin looks smoother, more even, radiant, rested, supple, or resilient;
- helps soften the appearance of fine lines **through moisturization**, when that mechanism and claim are substantiated;
- “daily age-support,” “long-term visible skin quality,” or “care for skin that looks smooth and resilient over time,” provided the full net impression remains cosmetic;
- the exact FDA-authorized early-skin-aging statement for a U.S. Broad Spectrum SPF 15+ sunscreen that passes the required tests and is used as directed with other sun-protection measures.

**Do not use for the cosmetic Core without a lawful drug path and matching evidence**

- preserves youth, reverses/stops aging, turns back time, or makes skin biologically younger;
- regenerates cells, repairs DNA, rebuilds tissue, heals microcuts, stimulates collagen/elastin production, or changes skin structure;
- repairs a damaged barrier, treats inflammation, eczema, acne, or infection;
- kills acne bacteria or prevents breakouts/scarring;
- “medical grade,” “clinic-level results,” “professional-grade protection,” or “clinically proven” without exact support;
- “safe for sensitive skin,” “non-irritating,” “hypoallergenic,” or “allergy-safe” as absolutes;
- implying that topical PDRN provides injectable-equivalent rejuvenation.

**Recommended language posture**

The strongest age-support story is behavioral and visible, not biological: **“Cleanse gently. Treat visible tone and texture. Seal in moisture. Protect every morning.”** Product-level copy can speak to skin looking smoother, more even, hydrated, rested, and resilient. “Preserve youth” should not be a headline or efficacy promise. If the brand wants emotional longevity language, prefer **“care for how your skin looks over time”** and keep every supporting Product claim specific and substantiated.

For PROTECT, do not paraphrase the regulated use into broader promises. A qualifying U.S. Broad Spectrum SPF 15+ Product may use FDA's specified risk-reduction statement only under its tested and labeled conditions. Korean PA++++, “UV defense,” or “anti-aging sunscreen” does not independently authorize that U.S. claim.

10. Supply stability and effectiveness data through expiry in the final container; a foreign SPF value at manufacture is not enough to support a U.S. launch decision.

## Launch sequence and decision gates

### Gate 1 — Formula identity

Do not name, claim, or package a finalist until the signed Formula matches the intended OEM Product and the public/reference Product facts are reconciled. A Product with missing INCI, mismatched size, copied page, or ambiguous ingredient source is not ready for brand work.

### Gate 2 — Core safety and claim file

For each Core Product, approve complete safety substantiation, label INCI, stability, packaging, adverse-event handling, and a claim matrix that maps every objective statement to exact evidence. U.S. cosmetics also carry current facility registration/product listing and safety-substantiation obligations under MoCRA, subject to statutory exemptions and role allocation ([FDA, Cosmetics & U.S. Law](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/cosmetics-us-law); [FDA, cosmetic facility registration and Product listing](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/registration-listing-cosmetic-product-facilities-and-products)). Registration/listing is not approval.

### Gate 3 — System wear validation

Test exact production-equivalent Core batches as a routine, not only alone:

- immediate and 8-hour feel;
- pilling and residue;
- shine and tack;
- eye/neck/beard/stubble behavior;
- shave-day tolerance;
- climate and skin-type spread;
- PROTECT film compatibility and removal;
- diverse skin-tone white cast for mineral/tinted sunscreen.

The minimum useful study includes enough participants to observe meaningful finish and tolerability patterns across oily, combination, dry, sensitive-leaning, clean-shaven, and facial-hair users. It should not be represented as clinical efficacy unless designed and powered for that purpose.

### Gate 4 — PROTECT regulatory release

PROTECT has an independent drug release gate. Do not let a completed Core, supplier inventory, Korean approval, or attractive wear feel bypass active-concentration, monograph/application, testing, Drug Facts, cGMP, registration, listing, and import review.

### Gate 5 — Catalog option discipline

Launch one default Core first. Add an alternative only when:

- its exact Formula is fully documented;
- it solves a clearly different customer job;
- its ingredient/origin/claim information is independently visible;
- the customer can choose using texture and need, not ingredient hype;
- it does not force a more complicated routine for everyone.

## Final call

Advance **Calming Biotics Gel Cleanser + PDRN 5% Active Ampoule + Calming Biotics Intensive Cream** as the single supplier-diligence Core. It is the clearest blend of low friction, a signature TREAT, moisture support, shave awareness, and visible tone/texture/age-support language. Keep CLEANSE once-daily-by-default at night for dry/sensitive users, make AM cleansing optional, and make SEAL quantity flexible.

Advance **Comfort Barrier Sun Cream** only to an OTC dossier request and wear-test planning. It is the best PROTECT candidate, not an approved launch Product. Keep **SUNBUDDY** as the backup if Leaders proves an exact current Formula whose active percentages comply and resolves its version/fragrance contradictions. Do not plan U.S. fixed-Formula launches of **Water Drop Essence** or **Glow Tone Up** through the ordinary monograph route.

Retain two conditional alternative systems for later discernment:

- **Clear & Light:** Gel Cleanser + Calming Biotics Blemish Serum + Green Collagen Balancing Emulsion.
- **Minimal Moisture:** Gel Cleanser + First Shot Active Essence + Green Collagen Hydrate Boosting Cream.

Do not add a stronger cadence-based TREAT simply to complete a ladder; the reviewed fixed-Formula evidence does not yet support one. Do not let “PDRN,” “green collagen,” “microbiome,” “3:1:1,” “100-hour,” Korean SPF/PA, or youth-preservation language outrun the exact Product dossier.

The resulting brand promise is restrained and stronger for it: **a simple daily system that keeps skin clean, visibly even, comfortable, and protected over time.**
