# Treatment-Serum Ingredient Frontier: 2026 Launch Set and 2027 Watchlist

- **Status:** Public research report
- **Decision scope:** Ingredient and serum-chassis options for a modified-base helix TREAT Product; US/California cosmetic launch targeted for Q1 2027
- **Research date:** 2026-09-04
- **Commercial context:** One 30 mL SKU, approximately 3,000-unit opening MOQ, fragrance-free, lightweight controlled glow/sheen, firm-but-not-tight afterfeel; intended to replace Peptide Bounce
- **Working names:** The Super Serum / The Maxxing Serum; naming is not evaluated here
- **Decision boundary:** This report is not a master Formula, safety assessment, medical recommendation, claim approval, or endorsement of an ODM base. Concentrations below are human-study or supplier-use contexts, not instructions to combine them.

## Executive decision

The credible 2026 frontier is **evidence density plus delivery discipline**, not the longest INCI list. For helix's younger, combination/oily yet dehydrated user, each credible launch architecture should use a restrained base of niacinamide, a compatible humectant system, ectoin, and—if the selected chassis can genuinely disperse it—a defined ceramide/co-lipid system. One or two identity-resolved peptides, one protected retinoid **or** one differentiated antioxidant/pigment route can then carry the treatment story. Every hero claim must be earned on the final Formula in its saleable pack.

The board should treat ingredient classes in four tiers:

| Tier | 2026/27 decision | Classes | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Launchable backbone** | Build into any compatible concept | Niacinamide; N-acetyl glucosamine (NAG); ectoin; defined hyaluronic-acid materials; panthenol/betaine; selective ceramide systems; tremella as a sensory humectant | Human topical support exists, formulation behavior is tractable, and the benefits fit dehydration, visible tone/texture, and barrier comfort. These are useful but not automatically differentiating. |
| **Launchable hero, Formula-dependent** | Select sparingly and substantiate | Retinal or retinol; exact signal peptides; bakuchiol; acetyl zingerone; defined vitamin-C derivative; selected postbiotic/ferment | Human evidence exists for exact materials or finished products, but effect size, stability, tolerance, and attribution depend on identity, carrier, dose, vehicle, and pack. |
| **Conditional frontier** | Advance only after specific gates | Oleyl Adapalenate/ADAPINOID; plant-derived Sodium DNA marketed as PDRN; complex ceramide glaze; multi-active peptide systems; encapsulated delivery claims | Differentiation is plausible, but regulatory, IP, identity, evidence-transfer, or Formula-fit risk is material. A supplier deck is insufficient. |
| **2027 watchlist / no lean launch** | Do not make a Q1 2027 hero | True extracellular vesicles/exosomes; NMN/NAD/NADH; sponge/silica spicules; poorly characterized “plant exosomes”; new long-release peptide carriers without full dossiers | Current ordinary-topical human evidence is sparse or non-portable, while identity, safety, claims, quality, or regulatory burdens are disproportionate to a fast US cosmetic launch. |

Three **coequal design lanes** follow from this evidence. They are formulation briefs, not final formulas:

1. **Barrier + visible clarity, AM/PM:** niacinamide/NAG, ectoin, defined HA, a low-tack fungal polysaccharide or beta-glucan, a technically credible ceramide system, and an identity-resolved peptide or antioxidant. It is compatible in principle with a separate prescription-retinoid routine, subject to finished-product tolerance testing.
2. **Protected retinal performance, PM-first:** retinal or retinol in a protected emulsion/encapsulation system with a deliberately short supporting stack—ectoin, barrier lipids, humectants, and perhaps one compatible peptide. Retinoid stability and tolerance, rather than ingredient count, control the concept.
3. **Vegan biotech differentiation, AM/PM:** an identity-resolved plant Sodium DNA material **or** defined postbiotic, combined with niacinamide, ectoin, humectants, and peptide support. This lane is coequal only if the source, analytical identity, vegan chain of custody, finished-Formula data, and cosmetic claim language clear the gates below; otherwise it collapses to a conventional ferment serum.

Oleyl Adapalenate can replace retinal in a fourth technical prototype, but should not become a launch commitment until US classification counsel, patent/license diligence, identity analytics, and long-term finished-Formula data are complete. True exosomes, NMN/NAD, and spicules should not be used to make “The Super Serum” feel advanced; in 2026 they mainly add evidence debt.

## How to read the evidence

### Evidence maturity scale

| Grade | Minimum meaning | What helix may infer | What helix may not infer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — direct** | Randomized, blinded, vehicle-controlled human topical study on the same active identity or substantially the same finished Formula and use pattern | The tested material or Formula can improve the measured cosmetic endpoint in the tested population and vehicle | That another supplier grade, carrier, concentration, combination, demographic, or pack will reproduce the result |
| **B — comparative** | Randomized human topical comparator study without a vehicle arm, or a small direct study | Directional performance and tolerance relative to the comparator | Absolute active contribution, equivalence, superiority across vehicles, or durable real-world benefit |
| **C — Formula-level** | Human study of a multi-active finished Formula, often uncontrolled or supplier-sponsored | The exact Formula may support the measured claim | Attribution to one named active or portability to a modified base |
| **D — indirect human** | Procedure-assisted delivery, post-procedure use, oral use, uncontrolled case series, or a different body site/use pattern | Safety or mechanistic context worth investigating | Ordinary intact-skin serum efficacy |
| **E — preclinical/supplier** | Ex vivo, in vitro, animal, computational, unpublished supplier deck, press release, or patent example | Hypothesis, formulation lead, or RFI question | Consumer efficacy or a finished-product claim |

Evidence grades do not score safety, IP freedom, regulatory status, sustainability, sensory fit, or supplier execution. A Grade A study can still be commercially unusable; a mature supporting humectant may be strategically valuable despite modest differentiation.

### Transfer rules

- **Raw material is not a finished Formula.** A peptide at a stated active ppm is not equivalent to the supplier solution percentage; a 2% postbiotic blend is not necessarily 2% microbial lysate; a “3HA” premix may contain little active polymer.
- **Trade materials are not ingredient classes.** Carrier, preservative, solvent, molecular-weight distribution, salt form, purity, assay, and processing history can change stability and performance.
- **Vehicle effects are real.** In several barrier and hydration studies, vehicle improved outcomes substantially. Helix should never credit the hero active with the full before/after change unless a matched vehicle comparison supports it.
- **Procedure evidence is non-portable.** Microneedling, laser, electroporation, ultrasound, and injection breach or alter the barrier and cannot substantiate an ordinary daily serum.
- **Demographic transfer is unresolved.** Much of the evidence below comes from women aged 30–60. Helix still needs final-Formula testing in its intended 18–35 male population, including combination/oily-dehydrated skin, stubble and recent shaving, acne-treatment or prescription-retinoid users where appropriate, and a meaningful range of Fitzpatrick skin types.
- **Supplier-sponsored is not automatically invalid.** It does require complete protocol, registration if available, comparator, statistical plan, attrition, adverse events, funding/conflicts, and enough raw output to distinguish a robust result from a marketing chart.

## Benefit architecture for helix

| Consumer job | Defensible cosmetic outcome | Best-supported ingredient roles | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dehydrated but not greasy | Immediate and sustained hydration; reduced tight feel; lightweight sheen | HA by defined molecular weight, ectoin, glycerin/propanediol, betaine, panthenol, tremella/beta-glucan in a low-tack polymer load | “Deeply hydrates all skin layers” without a validated method; excessive polymer stack that pills |
| Barrier comfort and resilience | Supports the moisture barrier; reduces visible dryness/flaking; improves comfort after routine stress | Niacinamide, ceramide/co-lipid system, ectoin, panthenol, squalane/emollients | “Heals,” “treats eczema,” or “repairs damaged tissue” |

| Uneven-looking tone | More even, brighter-looking tone; reduced appearance of spots | Niacinamide + NAG; selected antioxidant; optionally bakuchiol | Melasma treatment, bleaching, tyrosinase-treatment language, or implying sun protection |
| Blemish-prone appearance | Less oily-looking, calmer-looking, more even surface without stripping | Niacinamide, light barrier chassis, non-occlusive sensory design | Acne prevention/treatment, antimicrobial acne claims, or adapalene-equivalent positioning |
| One-step treatment convenience | Multiple compatible appearance benefits in one daily step | A coherent chassis plus one hero pathway | Calling the Product a replacement for cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen, or prescribed treatment |

### Mechanism map: selection rationale, not consumer copy

| Class | Working mechanism | What remains Formula-dependent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Niacinamide/NAG | Niacinamide participates in NAD/NADP cofactor biology and supports barrier-lipid synthesis; niacinamide and NAG can reduce melanin-transfer/formation signals in models | Skin delivery, tolerance and visible tone/barrier effect in the final vehicle |
| Ectoin/betaine | Osmolytes help stabilize hydration shells and proteins/membranes under environmental stress | Deposited amount, persistence, humectant synergy and consumer-visible benefit |
| HA/tremella | Water binding, film formation and rheology; lower-MW fractions may interact differently with skin than large film-forming polymers | MW distribution, purity, penetration, tack/pilling and sustained hydration |
| Ceramide/co-lipids | Replenish or support organized intercellular lipids of the stratum corneum | Whether the Formula actually deposits a useful lamellar/crystalline state rather than undispersed label claim |
| Retinal/retinol | Metabolic conversion toward retinoic acid followed by retinoid-receptor signaling | Conversion, stability, delivery, irritation and appearance benefit from the protected system |
| Signal peptides | Sequence-specific signaling intended to influence matrix-related pathways | Peptide purity/active ppm, proteolysis, delivery and whether a visible effect survives the complete Formula |
| Expression-line peptides | Proposed modulation of neurotransmission or contraction-related pathways | Ordinary cosmetic penetration and clinically visible result; injection or “muscle relaxing” analogies are not justified |
| Antioxidants | Radical quenching, metal chelation, excited-state quenching or regeneration of other antioxidants, depending on molecule | Redox network, concentration, skin availability, degradants and visible outcomes; an ORAC-type assay alone is insufficient |
| Postbiotics/ferments | Strain/process-specific cell components or metabolites can interact with barrier and host/microbial signaling | Identity, dose, preservation and microbiome/appearance effects of the exact lysate/filtrate |
| PDRN/Sodium DNA | Nucleotide supply and purinergic pathways are proposed in biomedical/preclinical work | Intact-skin delivery, fragment identity, human effect and cosmetic claim relevance—especially for plant material |
| NMN/NAD | Cofactor precursor or cofactor replenishment is the commercial theory | Intact-skin uptake, conversion, stability and human visible benefit |
| Exosomes/EVs | Vesicles can carry proteins/lipids/nucleic acids between cells | Almost everything commercial: identity, cargo, dose, penetration, potency, safety and classification |
| Spicules | Mechanical microdisruption can increase penetration | Depth, retained particles, inflammation, granuloma/infection risk and unintended delivery of the whole Formula |

## 2026 launchable backbone

### Niacinamide

- **Identity:** `Niacinamide` (nicotinamide), a single water-soluble molecule; verify assay, residual nicotinic acid, metals, microbial limits, and grade. “Vitamin B3 complex” is not adequate identity.
- **Mechanism and evidence:** Niacinamide supports epidermal energy/cofactor biology, reduces melanosome transfer in model systems, and can increase synthesis of stratum-corneum lipids. In a double-blind split-face study, 5% niacinamide used twice daily for 12 weeks by 50 women improved fine lines, spots, blotchiness, sallowness, and elasticity versus vehicle ([Bissett et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16029679/), Grade A). Topical nicotinamide also increased stratum-corneum ceramides/free fatty acids and reduced TEWL in dry skin ([Tanno et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10971324/)). A separate clinical program found visible pigmentation benefit at 2–5% in tested moisturizers ([Hakozaki et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12100180/)).
- **Use context, not formula target:** Human facial studies commonly anchor around 2–5%. Higher label percentages are not proven to be linearly better and can increase sting, tack, crystallization risk, and formulation burden.
- **Formulation:** Add in the aqueous phase under supplier-defined temperature and pH conditions. Niacinamide is generally tractable, but prolonged strong-acid/high-heat conditions can increase nicotinic-acid risk; a low-pH L-ascorbic-acid chassis should therefore be developed and assayed rather than treated as automatically compatible. Check color/odor drift, assay, pH, and crystallization through shelf life.
- **Tolerance and claims:** Usually well tolerated at evidence-anchored levels, but flushing/stinging can occur if nicotinic acid or an aggressive Formula is present. Appropriate claims concern appearance, hydration, and barrier support—not treatment of acne, rosacea, or hyperpigmentation disorders.
- **Vegan/supply:** Usually synthetic, but require a signed origin/processing-aid statement, non-animal testing declaration, country compliance, and change control. Commodity naming does not guarantee equal purity.
- **Decision:** **Launchable backbone.** It is more credible as a multi-benefit anchor than as “frontier” theater.

### N-acetyl glucosamine

- **Identity:** `Acetyl Glucosamine`; distinguish the N-acetyl form from glucosamine salts and disclose the exact supplier grade.
- **Evidence:** In an eight-week double-blind split-face test, 2% NAG reduced the appearance of facial hyperpigmentation; the same report described greater effect in combination with 4% niacinamide ([Bissett et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17348991/), Grade A/B). A larger ten-week double-blind vehicle-controlled study of two daily products containing 4% niacinamide + 2% NAG enrolled 202 women and improved multiple image-based pigmentation endpoints versus matched vehicles ([Kimball et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19845667/), Grade A for the regimen, not either ingredient alone).
- **Formulation:** Water-soluble and generally compatible with a neutral-to-mildly acidic aqueous serum, but confirm assay, browning/color, reducing-sugar impurities, pH, preservation, and polymer interactions in the final Formula.
- **Vegan/supply:** Glucosamine can be shellfish-derived or fermentation/fungal-derived. Vegan status is a **lot- and process-level hard gate**, not an assumption from the chemical name.
- **Decision:** **Launchable companion** when visible tone is a central benefit; less necessary in a retinoid-led concept where stack complexity should stay low.

### Ectoin

- **Identity:** `Ectoin`, a defined cyclic amino-acid derivative/osmolyte. Do not confuse it with an ectoin-containing supplier blend.
- **Evidence:** A randomized double-blind vehicle-controlled study in 104 women evaluated 2% ectoin and reported improvement in hydration, surface structure, and elasticity without reported side effects ([Heinrich et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17519560/), Grade A, older female population). Studies of ectoin/HA medical devices in dermatitis provide tolerability context but are not cosmetic-serum evidence ([Alexopoulos et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36038984/), Grade D).
- **Formulation:** bitop's raw-material sheet describes high water solubility, broad raw-material pH/temperature/light stability, and a supplier-use context of 0.3–2%, with hot- or cold-process aqueous addition ([bitop technical flyer](https://bitop.de/assets/images/downloads-cosmetic-ingredients/flyer_ectoin-natural_3-pages.pdf)). Those are supplier facts; helix still needs final-Formula assay, preservation, viscosity, pack, and claim data.
- **Vegan/supply:** Commonly produced by microbial fermentation. Require organism, substrate, processing-aid, GMO, residual protein, allergen, and vegan declarations.
- **Decision:** **Launchable backbone/support.** Good fit for post-shave comfort and low-irritation positioning; insufficient as a lone premium hero.

### Hyaluronic-acid materials

- **Identity:** Require each exact INCI—commonly `Sodium Hyaluronate`, `Hyaluronic Acid`, `Hydrolyzed Hyaluronic Acid`, `Hydrolyzed Sodium Hyaluronate`, or `Sodium Hyaluronate Crosspolymer`—plus weight-average molecular weight, distribution, active solids, carrier, preservative, and assay. “3HA,” “8D HA,” or “multi-molecular HA” is a marketing label, not an analytical specification.
- **Evidence:** In a randomized split-face vehicle-controlled study of 76 women, 0.1% HA preparations spanning 50–2,000 kDa improved hydration and elasticity after 60 days; 50 and 130 kDa materials also improved wrinkle-depth measures ([Pavicic et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22052267/), Grade A for the tested preparations). A 0.1% 2-kDa hydrolyzed HA study reported eight-week human benefits, but supplier employees were authors and identity/penetration findings should not be generalized to all low-MW HA ([Abe et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35587723/), Grade B). In a small 2024 study in older adults, low-MW HA improved capacitance more than high-MW or vehicle, while TEWL did not differ ([Muhammad et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38829483/), Grade B).
- **Formulation:** Molecular weight drives hydration, film, rheology, tack, pilling, and potential penetration. High polymer load can destabilize electrolyte-rich systems or produce stringing; crosspolymers behave differently from linear HA. Hydrate under the exact supplier process, manage microbial quality, and test viscosity/recovery and dose through the chosen pump.
- **Tolerance/safety:** “Smaller penetrates deeper” is not automatically better. Biological response can vary with fragment size and impurity/bioburden; request endotoxin or other impurity controls where the process warrants it.
- **Vegan/supply:** Modern cosmetic HA is often fermentation-derived, but animal-derived HA exists historically. Require source organism, media/processing aids, residual protein, GMO, and vegan chain of custody.
- **Decision:** **Launchable hydration system.** Use a small number of analytically defined materials chosen for feel and performance, not a count-based story.

### Tremella and other fungal polysaccharides

- **Identity:** Likely `Tremella Fuciformis Sporocarp Extract` or `Tremella Fuciformis Polysaccharide`; these are not interchangeable. Tremella is fungal, not botanical. Require species/strain, cultivated material versus fruiting body, extraction solvent, polysaccharide assay, molecular-weight distribution, carrier, solids, ash/protein, color/odor, and microbial limits.
- **Evidence:** A formulation study compared tremella emulsions with a base but included only five female volunteers and short, five-hour measurements; it supports humectancy/film formation, not durable anti-aging superiority ([Nowak et al.](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9284/10/3/82), Grade B/E depending endpoint). New animal, cell, or thesis findings do not establish an ordinary serum claim.
- **Formulation:** Can contribute cushion and controlled glow, but also viscosity, tack, stringing, discoloration, odor, and preservation load. Its benefit can overlap with HA and beta-glucan; screen combinations for pilling and pumpability rather than stacking all polymers.
- **Vegan/supply:** Potentially vegan, subject to culture media, processing aids, and contamination controls.
- **Decision:** **Launchable sensory/support ingredient**, not an evidence-equivalent HA substitute or headline anti-aging active.

### Ceramides and barrier-lipid systems

- **Identity:** Require exact species—such as `Ceramide NP`, `Ceramide AP`, `Ceramide EOP`, `Ceramide NG`, or a named pseudoceramide—plus active amount within the premix. Record all carriers and co-lipids. “Ceramide complex” is not enough.
- **Mechanism and evidence:** Ceramides are structural stratum-corneum lipids; performance depends on deposition and organization with other lipids, not label presence. A 2026 double-blind vehicle-controlled split-body study of Ceramide NP C15 in 50 people found both active and vehicle improved subjective dryness; the impaired-barrier subgroup had a significant TEWL response and exploratory microbiome differences ([Dimmers et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41758783/), Grade A/B). A 2025 randomized pseudo-ceramide spray study in dry sensitive skin reported barrier and lipid-profile effects, but the exact material and vehicle remain decisive ([Akahane et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39492723/), Grade B). In a ceramide-dominant eczema regimen, TEWL and hydration improved but disease-severity differences versus placebo were not significant, illustrating the vehicle problem ([Spada et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33984185/), Grade A for barrier endpoints, not a helix claim).
- **Formulation:** Ceramides can be crystalline and difficult to disperse. A credible system may require cholesterol and free fatty acids in a controlled lamellar architecture, heat, solubilizers, or a supplier premix. That can conflict with a transparent watery serum and may add haze, drag, richness, or instability. Confirm microscopy/thermal behavior where useful, particle/crystal stability, active recovery, freeze-thaw, centrifuge, and pump performance.
- **Vegan/supply:** Synthetic, fermentation, plant-derived, and animal-derived routes all exist. Cholesterol is especially provenance-sensitive; require a non-animal alternative or documented plant/synthetic route, along with fatty-acid and emulsifier origin. Exact SKU matters: Evonik, for example, presents SK-INFLUX V MB as using vegetal-derived semi-synthetic cholesterol ([supplier grade page](https://www.evonik.com/en/products/cs/pr_52023320.html)); that fact cannot be transferred to another SK-INFLUX grade or generic ceramide blend.
- **Decision:** **Launchable if the base is an emulsion/lamellar serum built for it.** In a clear aqueous base, niacinamide plus lighter emollients may be more honest than token ceramide dusting.

### Low-drama support materials

| Material | Identity and role | Evidence and limits | Technical / vegan gate | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Panthenol | `Panthenol`; humectant/comfort support | Human data often concern combination moisturizers or post-procedure products, so attribution is weak. A randomized post-laser trial used a 5% panthenol/madecassoside/mineral cream, not daily intact-skin serum ([Lueangarun et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30945430/), Grade D). | Confirm D- or DL-form, assay, water activity/preservation, and synthetic provenance. | Launchable support, not hero. |
| Betaine | `Betaine`; osmolyte/humectant and sensory modifier | Mechanistic and moisturization support is plausible; little need for a high-stakes claim. | Can be sugar-beet or synthetic; verify active solids and residuals. High levels can alter viscosity or tack. | Launchable support. |
| Squalane | `Squalane`; light emollient, slip, barrier comfort | Finished-Formula benefit depends on emollient blend; not a treatment hero. | **Require sugarcane/olive/other non-shark source**, oxidation indices, and traceability. Adds oil phase and can change glow/occlusion. | Launchable sensory tool. |
| Madecassoside | `Madecassoside`; triterpene glycoside from Centella or synthetic/biotech route | Human evidence is usually in combination and post-procedure products; do not transfer wound-healing language to a cosmetic serum. | Require assay, residual solvent/pesticide/heavy-metal controls, color, solubility, and botanical traceability. | Optional comfort support. |
| Allantoin | `Allantoin`; comfort/skin protectant in many contexts | Mature supporting ingredient, not differentiated efficacy. | Solubility and recrystallization can limit level; synthetic route can meet vegan policy. In the US, drug-context uses do not authorize drug claims in this cosmetic. | Optional support. |

## 2026 launchable heroes and conditional bets

### Retinal and retinol

**Identity and evidence hierarchy**

| Material | What it is | Best human evidence found | 2026 interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Retinal` (retinaldehyde) | Vitamin-A aldehyde one oxidation step from retinoic acid | A three-month randomized double-blind study in 40 Korean women tested 0.05% and 0.1% retinaldehyde twice daily. Both improved texture, hydration, and TEWL; the 0.1% Formula also improved a melanin measure ([Kwon et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29663701/), Grade B because the abstract does not establish a matched vehicle arm). A separate eight-week study in 23 Korean adults compared 0.05%/0.1% multilamellar retinal with matched-level retinol and reported broad instrumental improvement with no adverse events ([Kim et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33569865/), Grade B; small and vehicle/system-specific). | Strongest “modern retinoid” candidate if the ODM can document stabilized raw material, encapsulation/emulsion identity, active recovery, and final-pack stability. |
| `Retinol` | Vitamin-A alcohol requiring two oxidation steps to retinoic acid | A 26-week vehicle-controlled study of 0.075% retinol in 57 Japanese women found improvement in facial photoaging endpoints ([Kikuchi et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20078381/), Grade A/B). Retinol has a larger evidence and manufacturing base than novel esters, but tolerance and oxidation remain limiting. | Launchable, familiar, and easier to benchmark than novel retinoids; less differentiated. |
| `Hydroxypinacolone Retinoate` | Retinoic-acid ester commonly sold in a solvent blend | Published human data located are largely multi-retinoid or multi-active Formulas, so the contribution of HPR is not isolated. A nanoparticle serum study combined HPR with retinyl propionate and other technologies ([Wang et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36762391/), Grade C). | Conditional support only; do not claim it is “as effective as retinol without irritation” absent exact-material comparative data. |
| Retinyl esters | `Retinyl Propionate`, `Retinyl Palmitate`, and others | Some large regimen studies exist, but multiple products and co-actives prevent clean attribution—for example a 196-woman regimen containing niacinamide, peptides, antioxidants, and 0.3% retinyl propionate ([Fu et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20374604/), Grade C). | Lower differentiation and uncertain active contribution. Use only for a deliberate mildness/stability strategy with Formula evidence. |

Retinoids bind nuclear retinoid pathways after metabolic conversion and can influence epidermal turnover and dermal matrix biology. For a cosmetic brief, the substantiable output should remain appearance-based: smoother-looking texture, reduced appearance of fine lines, and more even-looking tone. Do not position the Product as equivalent to tretinoin or adapalene, and do not use acne-treatment, cellular-remodeling, or structural claims.

Retinal and retinol are lipophilic and sensitive to oxygen, light, heat, and the surrounding Formula. “Encapsulated” is not a stability result. Require exact encapsulant/full INCI, loading, free versus encapsulated active, particle size/distribution where relevant, encapsulation efficiency, release method, retinoid assay and degradants at initial and every stability interval, oxygen/headspace controls, and data in the exact pack. An opaque or UV-protective airless pack is the presumptive development path; a dropper repeatedly exchanges air and must prove equivalence rather than inherit a premium aesthetic exemption.

Tolerance is a commercial constraint. Develop PM-first use, an acclimation instruction if counsel approves it, and explicit separation from compromised/post-procedure skin. Recruit retinoid-naive and retinoid-experienced target users in controlled testing, and measure erythema, dryness, scaling, sting, discontinuation, and shaving interaction. A retinoid-led Formula should avoid unnecessary low-pH acids, spicules, aggressive penetration enhancers, and redundant irritants.

Vegan status is likely feasible for synthetically produced retinoids, but carriers can contain animal-origin gelatin, cholesterol, lecithin, glycerin, or processing aids. The finished trade material—not just the active molecule—must pass provenance review.

### Oleyl Adapalenate / ADAPINOID

- **Identity:** `Oleyl Adapalenate`, described by the Korean cosmetic ingredient database as the reaction product of oleyl alcohol and adapalene ([Korea Cosmetic Association ingredient record](https://kcia.or.kr/cid/search/ingd_view.php?no=23996)). “ADAPINOID” is Actera's trade name, not a generic active class.
- **Evidence:** A prospective randomized double-blind 12-week study enrolled 48 and analyzed 39 participants, almost all women, comparing 0.5% Oleyl Adapalenate with 0.5% retinol in closely matched creams. OA produced a larger reported wrinkle reduction (9.45% versus 4.11%); pigmentation changes were similar and week-12 TEWL did not differ significantly. The trial had no vehicle arm, was short, primarily included Fitzpatrick I–III skin, and was sponsored by the ingredient supplier ([peer-reviewed full text](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5s2952rq), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ski2.469), Grade B).
- **Supplier context:** Actera lists an off-white oil-phase powder, a supplier-use range of 0.16–0.5%, and heat processing up to 85°C ([Actera product page](https://acteraingredients.com/ingredient/adapinoid/)). These are supplier facts, not proof of stability in an ODM base.
- **Mechanism and regulatory problem:** The supplier and paper describe enzymatic hydrolysis to adapalene. Adapalene is a US OTC acne-drug active; a cosmetic whose design and messaging depend on delivery of adapalene presents classification and claim risk even if the ester is listed elsewhere as a skin conditioner. Before Formula work, obtain a written US regulatory opinion covering intended use, mechanism evidence, claims, labeling, safety, and whether drug requirements are implicated.
- **IP/supply:** US patent 12,133,907 covers lipophilic adapalene esters including Oleyl Adapalenate and examples in MCT/squalane ([patent record](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12133907B2/en)). Require authorized supply/license scope, territory, field of use, indemnity, trademark rules, patent expiry/continuity, analytical standard, impurity/degradant method, and a second-source assessment.
- **Formula gates:** identity/purity and free-adapalene limit at release and shelf life; enzymatic-conversion claim review; photostability/oxidation; final-pack assay; irritation/sensitization and repeat-use tolerance; target-population clinical comparison; and no acne or prescription-equivalence language.
- **Decision:** **Conditional 2026 prototype, not a lean-launch default.** The human comparison is genuinely interesting; it does not resolve US product classification, freedom to operate, long-term safety, or finished-Formula performance.

### Bakuchiol

- **Identity:** `Bakuchiol`, a purified meroterpene commonly sourced from *Psoralea corylifolia*/*Cullen corylifolium*. It is not a retinoid and should not be called “plant retinol.” Require chromatographic assay, isomer/impurity profile, residual solvents, pesticide/heavy-metal limits, photosensitizing furocoumarin controls, botanical traceability, and adulteration testing.
- **Evidence:** In a 12-week randomized double-blind study of 44 participants, 0.5% bakuchiol cream twice daily and 0.5% retinol cream once daily both reduced wrinkle surface area and hyperpigmentation, with more scaling and stinging reported in the retinol group ([Dhaliwal et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29947134/), Grade B). There was no vehicle arm, the dosing schedules differed, and one small study does not establish broad equivalence.
- **Formulation:** Lipophilic; requires an oil/emulsion or solubilized chassis. Obtain supplier limits for heat, light, oxidation, pH, solubilizers, and incompatibilities; verify assay and odor/color in pack. Botanically derived batches can vary.
- **Vegan/supply:** Plant provenance is compatible with vegan positioning if extraction aids and carriers are non-animal. The source crop carries biodiversity, adulteration, and responsible-sourcing questions.
- **Decision:** **Launchable alternative hero** for a non-retinoid AM/PM lane, but less novel in-market and not automatically compatible with every peptide/ceramide base.

### Signal and expression-line peptides

“Peptide complex” is not a material identity. The brief should specify the peptide INCI, sequence where non-confidential, active peptide concentration, supplier solution concentration, carrier, preservative, counter-ion, purity, assay, manufacturing route, and trademark/license. Only then can a study, stability report, or vegan declaration transfer.

| Peptide/material | Human evidence maturity | Technical and claim limits | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4` (Pal-KTTKS) | A 12-week randomized double-blind vehicle-controlled split-face study in 93 Caucasian women tested 3 ppm Pal-KTTKS and reported improvement in wrinkles/fine lines ([Robinson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18492182/), Grade A for that exact tested material/vehicle). | Oil-tail plus peptide behavior depends on delivery and active ppm. Do not convert a trade-solution percentage to active peptide without supplier math. “Boosts collagen” should become a tested appearance claim. | Strong candidate for an exact, dossier-backed peptide story. |
| `Palmitoyl Tripeptide-5` | SYN-COLL and analogous materials have supplier clinical packages, but evidence is often on the proprietary solution or finished Formula rather than independent active isolation. dsm-firmenich identifies the trade material and a 2.5% supplier test context ([SYN-COLL supplier page](https://www.dsm-firmenich.com/es-mx/businesses/perfumery-beauty/beauty-care/products/syn-coll.html), Grade E/C until dossier review). | Confirm complete INCI, active peptide, preservative, palmitoyl source, TGF-β/collagen claim language, and freedom to use trademark data. Haramoon's reference to SYN-COLL does not prove the base contains the studied version or amount. | Conditional launchable; request exact dossier and Formula evidence. |

Many stronger-looking charts are supplier studies. | Lubrizol's standard Argireline solution is `Water, Acetyl Hexapeptide-8, Caprylyl Glycol` and the supplier recommends 2–10% solution—not 2–10% pure peptide ([supplier record](https://espanol.lubrizol.com/Personal-Care/Products/Product-Finder/Products-Data/Argireline-peptide-solution-C)). | `Dipeptide Diaminobutyroyl Benzylamide Diacetate` | Recent human evidence often appears inside multi-active Formulas. Avoid snake-venom or neuromuscular claims. | Optional Formula-level story, not evidence-dense hero alone. |
| `Copper Tripeptide-1` / GHK-Cu | Human facial evidence is weaker than market familiarity suggests. After CO2 laser, a small 13-completer trial found no objective wrinkle or erythema advantage versus control despite higher satisfaction ([Miller et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16847171/), Grade D). | Copper can interact with chelators, reducing agents and some antioxidants; color and metal-catalyzed oxidation require exact compatibility work. Copper salt/complex identity and free copper matter. | Watch/secondary only in a multi-active launch; do not build the board thesis on it. |

Peptides are generally made by chemical synthesis or biotechnology, but amino-acid feedstocks, palmitic acid, solvents, carriers, glycerin, lecithin, and culture media can have animal origins. Require a complete trade-material vegan declaration and residual-solvent/elemental-impurity specifications. Peptides can hydrolyze, oxidize, adsorb to surfaces, or lose potency outside a defined pH/temperature range; obtain stability-indicating assay and final-Formula recovery, not only visual stability.

### Antioxidant and visible-tone options

| Material | Evidence | Formula burden | 2026 decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Ascorbic Acid` | Small vehicle-controlled facial studies report improvement in photodamage, including a six-month double-blind study and a ten-person half-face study that used 10% ascorbic acid plus 7% tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate ([Humbert et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12823436/), [Fitzpatrick & Rostan](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11896774/), Grade A/B but Formula-specific). ### Postbiotics and ferments

- **Identity:** A preserved serum is rarely a live probiotic. The ISAPP scientific consensus defines a postbiotic as a preparation of inanimate microorganisms and/or their components that confers a health benefit; a purified metabolite or cell-free filtrate is not automatically one ([consensus statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8387231/)). Use the complete INCI—such as `Lactococcus Ferment Lysate`, `Bifida Ferment Lysate`, `Lactobacillus Ferment Lysate`, or `Saccharomyces Ferment Filtrate`—and record organism to strain level, deposit/accession where available, substrate, fermentation and kill/lysis/filtration process, active marker, solids, carrier and preservative. “Microbiome complex” is not identity.
- **Evidence:** A 20-person randomized vehicle-controlled forearm study found barrier effects for a Lactococcus lysate emulsion ([Hausmann et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30630183/), Grade A/B but material- and site-specific). A 30-day study of a four-lysate lotion and a short Cera-Glow ferment study provide Formula-level support but involve proprietary blends and supplier/company authors ([Cui et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37803101/), [Lee et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36718839/), Grade C). A 2024 pilot serum combined postbiotics with niacinamide and peptides, so attribution is not possible ([Rusic et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39768384/), Grade C).
- **Formulation:** Ferment materials can add nutrients, protein, salts, odor, color, turbidity, bioburden, and preservative demand. Require batch marker assay, microbial limits, endotoxin/risk rationale, residual substrate/allergen profile, preservation interaction, and color/odor drift. The selected preservative system must remain effective despite the ferment load.
- **Vegan:** Microorganisms themselves do not settle vegan status. Require culture-media and processing-aid declarations; animal peptones, dairy substrates, and animal-origin antifoams disqualify the material.
- **Claims:** Do not claim a live probiotic, “balances the microbiome,” changes bacterial species, or treats inflammatory disease without final-Formula methods and regulatory review. “Supports a healthy-looking moisture barrier” is more realistic.
- **Decision:** **Launchable support/conditional differentiator.** Select one identity-resolved material with a strong dossier; avoid a decorative stack of overlapping ferments.

### PDRN and plant-derived nucleic acids

PDRN is a descriptive biomedical term, not a guarantee that two cosmetic materials are compositionally or biologically equivalent.

- **Salmon evidence and vegan conflict:** A July 2026 randomized split-face comparison used 0.1% salmon-sperm-derived PDRN-850K (reported ≥96% purity, average about 1,200 base pairs, molecular weight ≤850 kDa) versus 0.1% retinol eye cream in 31 Chinese women with self-reported sensitive skin for 28 days. PDRN produced larger periocular imaging changes, but there was no vehicle-only arm; the time was short; ultrasound changes may partly reflect hydration; in-vivo Raman penetration was examined on one forearm and could not unequivocally distinguish exogenous from endogenous nucleic acid; and authors included corporate affiliations ([PLOS ONE full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0350905), Grade B). Salmon PDRN fails helix's vegan gate regardless of efficacy.
- **Plant evidence:** A 2025 rose-derived PDRN report is in vitro/ex vivo, not a human topical trial ([Cavagnino et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41614736/), Grade E). Supplier catalogs advertise plant materials with high DNA purity, but do not establish clinical equivalence to salmon PDRN; for example, MC-PhytoDNA markets plant Sodium DNA alternatives without a matched human comparison in the public brochure ([supplier catalog](https://pub-mediabox-storage.rxweb-prd.com/exhibitor/document/exh-5f922121-7025-4fa3-ae31-af9e6c22164d/d6b8ff40-8c7e-46c0-9342-ffd79e909dda.pdf), Grade E).
- **Identity required:** likely `Sodium DNA` or a supplier-specific blend, not simply “PDRN.” Require botanical species and tissue, cultivation/source, extraction/enzymatic process, sodium/salt state, fragment-length and molecular-weight distribution, nucleic-acid identity method, purity and active solids, residual protein, endotoxin/bioburden, solvent/pesticide/heavy-metal limits, nuclease activity, carrier/preservative, and shelf-life assay.
- **Mechanism and claim boundary:** Nucleic-acid fragments can have biological effects in preclinical models, but a cosmetic serum should not claim DNA repair, cellular regeneration, wound healing, tissue regeneration, angiogenesis, or drug-receptor activity. The Helix DNA Motif is brand language and should not be conflated with biological DNA intervention.
- **Formulation:** Water compatibility alone does not establish delivery or activity. Test nuclease/heat/pH/shear stability, polymer/salt interactions, preservation, adsorption to pack, molecular-weight distribution and active recovery through shelf life. Ordinary intact-skin penetration needs exact-Formula evidence.
- **Decision:** **Conditional vegan-biotech lane, not required for launch.** It graduates only with analytical identity, non-animal process, clean US claim opinion, dependable supply, and final-Formula human evidence. Otherwise remove it rather than inherit salmon-PDRN expectations.

## 2027 watchlist and exclusions from the lean launch

### NMN, NAD, NADH, and “cellular energy” actives

- **Identity:** `Nicotinamide Mononucleotide` (NMN), oxidized nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), reduced NADH, niacinamide, and nicotinamide riboside are different molecules. A ferment filtrate said to contain NMN is not equivalent to purified NMN. Require exact INCI acceptance, chemical form/salt, stereochemistry where relevant, chromatographic assay, purity, water content, degradants, carrier, and active amount.
- **Evidence:** A 2025 paper on NMN-containing yeast ferment examined an artificial membrane, fibroblasts, and raw-material stability; it was not a human topical trial ([Betsuno et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40317586/), Grade E). A 2026 NAD “skinbooster” melasma report was a 36-woman single-arm microneedling-assisted case series, not intact-skin evidence ([Yi et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41870519/), Grade D). Older topical myristyl nicotinate studies concern a distinct niacin ester and cannot substantiate NMN/NAD ([Jacobson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17518989/)).
- **Technical risk:** Nicotinamide cofactors can hydrolyze or oxidize; skin penetration and intracellular conversion after ordinary topical use are unresolved. Require pH/light/oxygen/temperature degradation kinetics, final-pack assay, skin delivery, and a vehicle-controlled human trial.
- **Claim limit:** No “raises cellular NAD,” mitochondrial rejuvenation, longevity, senolysis, DNA repair, or cellular-energy claim without an exceptionally strong regulatory and clinical record. These phrases move beyond an appearance claim.
- **Decision:** **2027 watchlist.** Niacinamide already supplies a more mature, lower-risk NAD-pathway story without speculative longevity positioning.

### Extracellular vesicles and exosomes

- **Identity:** “Exosome” should mean a characterized extracellular-vesicle fraction, not a ferment filtrate, conditioned medium, liposome, plant extract, or marketing metaphor. Require source organism/cell and tissue, donor and pathogen controls where relevant, isolation process, particle number and size distribution, morphology, marker panel, cargo/protein/nucleic-acid characterization, purity from non-vesicular contaminants, sterility/bioburden/endotoxin, preservative, freeze-thaw behavior, and potency/release assay.
- **Evidence:** A 2026 scoping review screened 2,394 records and found only 18 topical studies; just eight studied topical use alone, ten combined delivery procedures, and commercial products often omitted isolation, processing, and shelf-life details ([Knauer et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411735/)). A 2025 review highlighted limited clinical evidence and infection, inflammation, and malignancy concerns ([Mahmoud et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40256340/)). A human platelet-exosome study was single-arm and nonrandomized ([Proffer et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35689936/), Grade D). A 2026 radiofrequency-microneedling/EV study was an unblinded adjunct procedure, not a daily cosmetic serum ([Yen et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42635327/), Grade D).
- **Regulatory/safety:** FDA states that products intended to treat disease using exosomes are drugs/biologics and that there are no FDA-approved exosome products ([FDA safety notification](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/public-safety-alert-due-marketing-unapproved-stem-cell-and-exosome-products)). A cosmetic label does not cure a composition, intended-use, sterility, donor, or safety problem.
- **Vegan:** Human- or animal-cell vesicles fail helix's vegan ethos; culture media may also contain animal materials. Plant-derived vesicles can be vegan in principle but must not borrow human-exosome evidence. A 2025 potato-derived exosome report is product-specific and does not validate the category ([Lee et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40283895/)).
- **Decision:** **No Q1 2027 launch.** Reconsider only after standardized identity/potency, scalable GMP manufacture, intact-skin randomized evidence, long-term safety, vegan provenance, and a written US classification/claims opinion.

### Sponge or silica spicules

- **Identity:** Require biological/mineral source, exact INCI, chemistry, morphology, length/diameter and distribution, surface treatment, dissolution/retention behavior, sharp-particle count, contaminants/heavy metals, sterilization/microbial control, and batch release. “Natural microneedles” is not identity.
- **Evidence:** Marine sponge spicules can enhance macromolecule delivery in experimental models, but the foundational work is predominantly preclinical ([Zhang et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28763230/), Grade E). A 2025 secretome/nanospicule facial study enrolled only 21 people, was single-arm, used Wharton's-jelly mesenchymal-stromal-cell secretome, and disclosed corporate interests ([Lee et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41155317/), Grade D). A 2026 dermatology editorial specifically flagged hidden risks and granuloma concern in cosmetic silica-spicule use ([Moon et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41371418/)).
- **System risk:** A penetration enhancer acts on the entire Formula, including preservatives, fragrance contaminants, degradants, allergens, metals, or microbial products. It conflicts with a no-fragrance, low-irritation, post-shave-friendly serum and magnifies retinoid risk.
- **Regulatory:** FDA explains that microneedling products whose needles penetrate living skin and affect tissue structure/function may be devices, and cleared devices have not been cleared for delivery of cosmetics, drugs, or biologics ([FDA microneedling consumer page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices/microneedling-devices), [FDA regulatory guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/regulatory-considerations-microneedling-products)). Classification turns on actual design and intended use, not the word “cosmetic.”
- **Decision:** **Exclude from the lean launch.** A supplier claim such as “more than 20% absorption without barrier damage” must be rejected unless the exact spicule, Formula, human protocol, comparator, tissue endpoint, safety follow-up, and regulatory rationale are disclosed.

### Emerging peptide and delivery systems

Croda announced Matrixyl Neolide in 2026 as a patented encapsulated peptide system with supplier claims of controlled release up to 15 days and rapid firming ([Croda announcement](https://www.croda.com/en-gb/media-hub/news/general/croda-beauty-matrixyl-neolide)). The public announcement does not provide enough exact INCI, release-method, independent human, or finished-Formula detail to transfer the claims. Treat it and analogous “smart,” “adaptive,” or long-release carriers as **2027 watchlist** materials until the full dossier supports identity, loading, delivery, repeat-use safety, shelf stability, patent/license, and claim rights.

Likewise, recombinant collagen fragments, growth-factor mimetics, plant callus/stem-cell extracts, autophagy/senolytic actives, and AI-designed peptides are discovery signals—not a single interchangeable class. They enter the commercial screen only after exact INCI/material identity, human intact-skin evidence, vegan process, supplier quality, and cosmetic claim feasibility are established.

### Watchlist graduation scorecard

An emerging material graduates only if **all** applicable cells are green; a high efficacy score cannot compensate for failed identity, safety, or legality.

| Gate | Green | Yellow | Red |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Accepted exact INCI/full blend; validated assay and reference standard; controlled impurities | Partial characterization or proprietary method under NDA | Marketing category only; no reproducible identity |
| Human evidence | Randomized blinded vehicle-controlled intact-skin study on exact material/Formula and relevant endpoints | Comparator/uncontrolled or non-target population | In vitro, animal, oral, injection, or procedure-only |
| Safety | Cosmetic safety assessment plus repeat-use tolerance; contaminants and route risks controlled | Short supplier patch/tolerance only | Sterility/endotoxin/donor/particle or systemic uncertainty |
| US claims/classification | Written cosmetic route and claims matrix | Counsel requests narrower claims or further data | Drug/biologic/device intended use or unresolved classification |
| Vegan provenance | Active, carrier, media, processing aids and facility controls documented non-animal | One upstream declaration missing | Animal/human source or undisclosed culture media |
| Formula/pack stability | Stability-indicating assay in exact Formula and saleable pack through justified program | Raw-material or short pilot only | No assay, unknown degradants, no pack data |
| Supply/IP | Authorized territory/use, change control, continuity and acceptable MOQ/lead/cost | Sole source with mitigations | Unclear license/FTO, uncontrolled site, or no commercial continuity |
| Claim ownership | helix can lawfully use the exact data/wording and final Formula reproduces it | Supplier trademark/data restrictions | Borrowed ingredient-class claim or inaccessible underlying report |

## Formula architecture and incompatibility map

This is a development-risk map, not a categorical “never combine” list. Compatibility must be tested in the nominated base because pH, water activity, solvent, chelation, oxygen, emulsifier, ionic strength, preservative, process, and package can change the answer.

| Combination / architecture | Primary risk | Practical decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retinal/retinol + strong acids or aggressive penetration enhancers | Chemical degradation plus cumulative irritation | Keep the retinoid lane mildly acidic-to-neutral as supported by the exact system; avoid AHA/BHA, low-pH L-ascorbic acid, and spicules unless the complete Formula has unusually strong data. |
| Retinoid + antioxidants | Can improve oxidative protection, but antioxidant, solvent and retinoid can also react or compete for Formula architecture | Use a supplier-validated protected system; assay both actives and degradants. Do not assume “antioxidant” means stabilizer. |
| L-ascorbic acid + niacinamide | Not an absolute consumer-level incompatibility, but a stable low-pH ascorbic Formula can increase niacinamide conversion/flush risk under heat and create sensory conflict | If used together, require nicotinic-acid and ascorbic-degradation assays through stability. For lean launch, choose a vitamin-C derivative or separate architecture. |
| L-ascorbic acid + peptides/ceramides | Low pH, ionic strength and solvent system may destabilize peptides, lamellae or sensory profile | Avoid the overloaded one-bottle premise unless the exact peptide/lipid suppliers support the pH and final Formula data pass. |
| Copper peptide + ascorbate/reducing agents/chelators | Metal complexation, color change and redox instability | Get supplier-specific compatibility; assay free/complexed copper and relevant actives. Simpler answer is not to combine. |
| Ceramide premix + clear water gel | Recrystallization, haze, poor deposition and insufficient oil/co-lipids | Use a lamellar/emulsion base designed for the exact premix or remove the ceramide headline. |
| Multiple HA grades + tremella + beta-glucan + gums | Tack, stringing, pilling, poor spread, pump tailing and salt-sensitive viscosity | Optimize total polymer solids and molecular-weight distribution; use trained sensory plus sunscreen/moisturizer layering tests. Ingredient count is not a goal. |
| Peptides + high heat/extreme pH/proteolytic ferment | Hydrolysis, oxidation or loss of assay | Add at validated cool-down conditions; monitor active recovery and screen ferment enzyme activity. |
| Plant DNA/PDRN + nuclease/heat/shear/high bioburden | Fragmentation, identity drift and loss of claimed molecular-weight distribution | Use stability-indicating nucleic-acid methods; validate process order, preservation and pack adsorption. |
| Ferments/extracts + preservation system | Added nutrient load, bioburden, color/odor and preservative binding | Re-run preservation efficacy on every material or level change; do not rely on a supplier microbial limit alone. |
| High electrolyte active load + polymer thickener | Viscosity collapse, clumps, syneresis or dose drift | Map ionic tolerance experimentally and qualify rheology after accelerated/real-time stability. |
| Oils/lipophilic heroes + “weightless” sensory target | Grease, slow break, occlusion and excessive shine | Use an emulsion/emollient architecture and sensory benchmark; controlled glow is an endpoint, not proof of more oil. |
| Dropper + oxidation-sensitive Formula | Repeated oxygen ingress, variable dose, neck contamination and light exposure | Prefer an opaque/UV-controlled airless route or prove the dropper by active assay, dose, in-use simulation and microbiology. |

### Chassis choices

| Chassis | Best use | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aqueous polymer serum | Niacinamide/NAG, ectoin, HA, selected peptides/ferments/plant DNA | Cannot honestly carry poorly soluble lipophilic retinoids, bakuchiol, THD derivatives or meaningful barrier lipids without solubilization/emulsification; polymer crowding causes tack/pilling. |
| Lightweight oil-in-water emulsion serum | Ceramides/co-lipids, retinal/retinol, bakuchiol, lipophilic antioxidants plus aqueous support | More process and pack complexity; emulsion stability, oxidation and sensory must be tuned. Often the best “all-in-one treatment” architecture. |
| Lamellar/liquid-crystal emulsion | Barrier-lipid delivery and elegant cushion | Supplier architecture is difficult to recreate by dropping a ceramide premix into another base; microscopy/thermal/rheological evidence may be needed. |
| Anhydrous or water-light serum | Some retinoids and lipophilic antioxidants; reduced microbial risk | Poor fit for niacinamide, HA, ectoin and peptides; may feel oily or silicone-heavy and cannot deliver the desired broad hydration alone. |
| Encapsulated/liposomal dispersion | Protect or meter unstable lipophilic active; potentially improve feel | Must disclose complete carrier, active loading, free active, size/PDI, encapsulation efficiency, release and shelf stability. “Liposome” does not prove dermal delivery or efficacy. |
| Cyclodextrin/host system | Solubilization or stabilization of a defined guest molecule | Guest loading, free fraction, dissociation, irritation and polymer/preservative interactions are Formula-specific. |

## Raw-material facts versus finished-Formula claims

| Evidence supplied | It can support | It cannot support on its own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| INCI listing or regulatory database entry | Name/use-function context | Safety, efficacy, concentration, vegan origin, or FDA approval |
| SDS/TDS/CoA | Hazard handling, specification and lot release for the raw material | Consumer tolerance, finished-Formula stability, preservation, or claim efficacy |
| In-vitro pathway data | Mechanistic plausibility and biomarker selection | Visible consumer benefit or skin penetration |
| Ex-vivo skin penetration | Delivery under the tested donor skin, dose and vehicle | Clinical effect, long-term safety, or delivery from another base |
| Supplier raw-material clinical | Performance of the tested supplier material, amount, vehicle, protocol and population | Performance of a different trade grade or modified Formula |
| ODM base before/after study | Directional performance of that exact base | Active attribution or performance after helix changes the base |
| Finished helix Formula randomized vehicle-controlled study | The tested endpoint, population, use pattern and pack when methodologically sound | Untested drug, biological, permanence, or universal claims |
| Consumer-perception questionnaire | Truthful qualified perception wording in similar target users | Instrumental or biological claims |

Helix should maintain a claim-evidence matrix at Formula version level. Each public claim should point to the test product code, manufacturing lot, pack, protocol, population, endpoint, timepoint, statistical result, limitations, and approved wording. Changing an active, carrier, preservative, pigment, fragrance status, process, pack, use instruction, or claim can invalidate the bridge.

## US cosmetic and California decision boundary

FDA determines cosmetic-versus-drug status from **intended use**, which can be established by the package, website, social media, advertising, consumer perception, and sometimes an ingredient's recognized therapeutic use. A product intended to treat/prevent disease or affect body structure/function is a drug even if it also improves appearance ([FDA cosmetic/drug classification](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap), [FDA cosmetic-claims guidance](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling/cosmetics-labeling-claims)). Supplier mechanism language becomes helix risk if repeated publicly.

| Prefer, after substantiation | Remove or send to counsel |
| --- | --- |
| Improves the appearance of fine lines | Stimulates collagen/elastin production |
| Visibly smooths uneven-looking texture | Remodels skin, regenerates cells, reverses aging |
| Helps skin feel firmer and look more resilient | Repairs DNA, activates longevity pathways |
| Supports the moisture barrier and reduces visible dryness | Heals, treats inflammation/eczema/rosacea |
| Improves the look of uneven tone and dullness | Inhibits melanin/tyrosinase; treats melasma |
| Helps reduce the look of visible environmental-stress signs | Prevents UV damage/photoaging or replaces sunscreen |
| Lightweight hydration for blemish-prone skin | Treats/prevents acne, kills acne bacteria |

Oleyl Adapalenate deserves extra review because FDA identifies adapalene 0.1% as an approved OTC anti-acne drug active ([FDA prescription-to-nonprescription switch list](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/over-counter-otc-nonprescription-drugs/prescription-nonprescription-switch-list)). Exosome and spicule routes have the additional biologic/device issues described above. FDA also advises that nanoscale materials can differ in absorption, distribution, bioavailability, and biological activity; it calls for finished-product characterization of identity, particle distribution, morphology, surface chemistry, aggregation, stability, dermal penetration, and impurities ([FDA nanomaterial safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-safety-nanomaterials-cosmetic-products)).

Under MoCRA, the responsible person named on the label must hold adequate safety substantiation; serious-adverse-event, contact-label, facility-registration, and product-listing duties cannot simply be outsourced. FDA does not prescribe one universal cosmetic test battery, so helix must justify a risk-based program ([FDA MoCRA overview](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/modernization-cosmetics-regulation-act-2022-mocra), [21 U.S.C. § 364d safety substantiation](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title21-section364d), [FDA product testing](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-science-research/product-testing-cosmetics)). Verify the Korean manufacturing/filling site's applicable FDA registration and US agent, but never call registration “FDA approval” ([FDA registration/listing](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/registration-listing-cosmetic-product-facilities-and-products)).

The retail declaration must decompose every premix into its constituents under US naming and order rules; a hero list or trade name is not Complete INCI ([21 CFR 701.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-701/section-701.3), [FDA ingredient-name guidance](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling/cosmetic-ingredient-names)). FDA has no federal definition for “fragrance-free” or “hypoallergenic,” and “cruelty-free” can misleadingly refer only to the finished product. Define these standards contractually across actives, carriers and processing aids ([FDA cosmetic allergens](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/allergens-cosmetics), [FDA cruelty-free claims](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-claims/cruelty-freenot-tested-animals)).

For Q1 2027, screen the **2027 California Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act list now**, including every premix carrier and trace profile; the statutory CAS-specific list expands January 1, 2027 ([California HSC § 108980](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=108980.)). Separately screen California Safe Cosmetics reporting—including fragrance allergens that may be reportable even when used for another function—and conduct an exposure-based Proposition 65 assessment rather than accepting a generic supplier letter ([CDPH compliance FAQ](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CCDPHP/DEODC/OHB/CSCP/Pages/Compliance-FAQ.aspx), [CDPH reporting guidance](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CCDPHP/DEODC/OHB/CSCP/Pages/ReportingGuidance.aspx), [OEHHA business guidance](https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65/businesses-and-proposition-65)). California's 2026 draft priority-product work on formaldehyde-releasing preservatives and butylparaben in leave-on products creates 2027 reformulation risk; avoiding them where feasible is sensible, but “preservative-free” is not ([DTSC leave-on preservative project](https://dtsc.ca.gov/scp/leave-on-products-containing-preservatives/)).

FTC expects objective express and implied claims to have an adequate basis before advertising. Its guidance specifically warns that a “clinically tested ingredient” claim may imply that the marketed complete product delivers the benefit ([FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)). No ODM or ingredient clinical deck should become final helix copy until the exact Formula's evidence bridge is documented.

### Risk-based finished-Formula test program

The safety assessor and qualified laboratories must set the actual program and acceptance criteria. The following is a decision framework, not a claim that FDA mandates every test.

| Workstream | Minimum question | Triggered additions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Safety assessment | Do ingredient toxicology, impurity/exposure levels, intended population/site/frequency and pack support reasonable certainty of safety? | Novel biological/nano material; penetration enhancer; eye-area use; sensitizing botanical; pregnancy-related retinoid labeling; impaired-skin exposure |
| Microbiology | Are limits controlled and does the exact preservative system protect the final Formula through expected use? | High ferment/botanical/polymer load; dropper; water activity change; pack or preservative change. Use an appropriate preservation-efficacy method such as [ISO 11930](https://www.iso.org/standard/75058.html) and microbial-limit framework such as [ISO 17516](https://www.iso.org/standard/59938.html) where justified. |
| Stability | Do appearance, odor, pH, rheology, assay, degradants, microbiology and dose remain within pre-set specification for claimed shelf/use life? | Retinoid/antioxidant/DNA/peptide assay; particle size and encapsulation; oxygen/light challenge; shipping excursion; freeze-thaw/centrifuge only when scientifically useful |
| Package compatibility | Does the exact Formula in every saleable component avoid migration/sorption, swelling, cracking, coating attack, leakage, weight loss, clogging and dose drift? | Retinoid/antioxidant oxygen barrier; oil/elastomer interaction; dropper versus airless; recycled material; decoration/contact-material change |
| Human tolerance | Is repeated use acceptable in the target population and intended routine? | Retinoid acclimation; recent shaving/stubble; combination/oily-dehydrated skin; concomitant sunscreen/moisturizer; separate prescription retinoid where concept intends compatibility; eye proximity |
| Cosmetic efficacy | Does final Formula outperform the matched vehicle or a pre-defined benchmark on its priority visible endpoints? | Numerical/time claims, comparative claims, “clinically proven,” firmness/line/tone endpoints, non-comedogenic or microbiome claims |
| Consumer/sensory | Does it deliver fragrance-free, lightweight hydration, controlled glow, firm-not-tight feel, no residue and low pilling? | Layering with common sunscreen, moisturizer, facial hair, humid/dry climate and different application doses |

For an executive-grade hero claim, use a pre-registered or at least pre-specified randomized, blinded, controlled design where feasible. Recruit enough men aged 18–35 to support the actual claim, include meaningful Fitzpatrick diversity, standardize shave timing and concomitant products, predefine primary endpoints and analysis, report attrition/adverse events, and include a matched vehicle when the question is active contribution. Useful measures can include corneometry, TEWL, cutometry, standardized imaging/profilometry, expert grading and validated consumer perception—but only where each method maps to the intended wording. Eight to twelve weeks is a practical visible-benefit window for many treatment claims; hydration and immediate sensory can be separate shorter endpoints.

## Source interpretation note

This report prioritizes FDA, US law/regulators, ISO scope pages, peer-reviewed human studies, ingredient-association identity records, patents for IP facts, and original supplier technical material for supplier facts. Supplier webpages and press releases are cited only to establish what a supplier says about its own material; they are not independent proof. PubMed records were checked for study design, population, comparator, endpoints and conflicts when available. Absence of a study in this search is not proof that none exists; ODMs and suppliers should disclose unpublished full reports under NDA for critical review.
