# Rhode responsive design audit — 10 September 2026

Iteration 1. Independent design consultant audit of the current Rhode storefront, using first-party pages in Codex's in-app browser. The homepage currently features the Alexandra Leclerc campaign. This is an observation record, not approval to copy Rhode's brand, product claims, legal text, photographs, or commerce behavior.

## Evidence and method

Primary visual passes: 1440 × 1000 desktop and 390 × 844 mobile. Read-only DOM geometry and computed typography supplemented screenshots. Screenshots are in `outputs/mobile-audit/rhode/`; `pdp-mobile-metrics.json` records mobile section and image geometry. Geometry is rounded where appropriate and describes this viewport/content, not a universal fixed-height requirement. Media and review widgets initialize asynchronously; early screenshots can capture a transient state, so settled dimensions take precedence over an immediately post-navigation image.

The serum PDP was scrolled from its gallery to the footer on both sizes, with every major module visually inspected. Home and About were scrolled through their entire content sequence on mobile; desktop compositions were inspected and full-page captures saved. Shop, FAQ, contact, login, menu, search, and empty cart were also inspected. A separate research subagent verified official route/content structure through first-party web extraction, independently of the visual audit.

Sources: [Home](https://www.rhodeskin.com/), [Shop](https://www.rhodeskin.com/collections/shop), [Peptide Glazing Fluid](https://www.rhodeskin.com/products/peptide-glazing-fluid), [About](https://www.rhodeskin.com/pages/about-us), [FAQ](https://www.rhodeskin.com/pages/faq), [Contact](https://www.rhodeskin.com/pages/contact-us), [Account entry](https://www.rhodeskin.com/account).

## Design judgment

Rhode's mobile design is a re-composition of modules, not a desktop layout reduced to one column. It chooses a dominant image, keeps its related content in the same card, changes internal order, relocates controls to where a thumb can find them, and hides redundant desktop imagery. The strongest transferable idea is **one coherent image-and-content unit per screenful**, with a consistent narrow frame. The weakest idea to copy would be Rhode's very small secondary controls and indiscriminate use of horizontal carousels.

### Sticky purchase behavior

After the main purchase region leaves view, a compact bottom bar appears. Desktop includes product thumbnail, name, both size buttons, and purchase CTA. Mobile removes the thumbnail/name and uses two adjacent controls: size dropdown and purchase CTA. At390 ×844 it measures390 ×72px, x0/y772, with181 ×44px selector and181 ×43px CTA. It stops showing when the footer is reached. It does not add redundant full product copy. The gallery initially has no sticky bar covering its bottom. Screenshot evidence: `pdp-mobile-profile.png`, `pdp-mobile-application.png`, `pdp-desktop-full.png`.

The mobile product disclosures expose `aria-expanded`; the Benefits interaction expanded correctly. No cart mutation or checkout was performed.

## Homepage

Desktop uses a large landscape campaign hero, a three-card product rail, full-width campaign messaging, split feature modules, multi-image social rail, and a split mission/values module. Mobile uses an independently authored hero copy variant and mobile crop; it retains the product rail but shows one card with a narrow next card. Campaign copy is centered over a portrait image. The feature carousel becomes one vertically composed image/content card. The lip feature becomes image over copy. The social module uses a square image rail, thin progress line, previous/next buttons and a CTA below. Mission is image first, then one selected paragraph/CTA, then three selector rows. [Source](https://www.rhodeskin.com/).

Settled mobile module heights observed: product rail 468 px; campaign banner 556; feature carousel 679; image/copy feature 585; social module 501; mission module 871. The homepage hero initially appeared much taller while video/layout initialized; subsequent DOM measurements reported 488 px. That initialization shift is a flaw to avoid rather than a dependable design target.

The rail shows many tiny pagination dots because it contains most of the catalog. This is poor navigation at scale: dozens of dots are neither clear nor tappable. Prefer a bounded featured selection, a fraction indicator, and accessible next/previous buttons.

## Collection page

[Shop](https://www.rhodeskin.com/collections/shop) compresses its wide hero from about 450 px desktop to 223 px mobile. Category pills become a single horizontal rail; the selected Shop All item was scrolled into view at the right end. Sort is left, count and column toggles right. Desktop supports two and three columns; mobile supports one and two. The observed settled desktop cards were 431.7 × 523.2 px in three columns with 40 px gutters between cards. Mobile two-column cards were 174.8 × 289 px, with about 8.45 px gap.

Mobile cards hide descriptive copy and the separate price; product name and price-bearing CTA remain. Product imagery is still dominant. Editorial campaign tiles occupy product-sized grid cells, preserving rhythm rather than becoming giant interstitial sections. The mobile product title is compact uppercase; shorthand merchandising labels remain on the image.

Do **not** copy the 28.6 px-high compact card CTA, 24 × 26.5 px column toggles, or 17.4 px-high sort trigger as tap-target standards. Their observed rendered areas fall below the safer 44 px target for Helix.

## Navigation, search, cart

- Mobile menu occupies the inset full-height card. Category tabs scroll horizontally near the top, product links form a single vertical list with small thumbnails, then Shop CTA and secondary destinations/account/region follow below. This is a merchandising menu, not a tiny accordion containing every desktop column. Body scrolling is locked. **Escape did not close this menu in the tested state**; clicking the close toggle did. The toggle is a `div` with button role and `aria-haspopup=dialog`; the menu did not expose a clearly named modal in the inspected state. Do not copy that implementation.
- Mobile search is an inset full-height drawer with centered heading, upper-right close, full-width input, suggestions, divider and thumbnail result rows. Opening focuses Close instead of the input; Escape restores focus to Search. Main content remained represented in extracted accessibility output, so full screen-reader isolation is not established. Search was inspected with the query `serum`; no customer data was entered. The initially captured result list still showed suggestions during asynchronous updating, so no search-quality claim is made from that transient state.
- Mobile empty cart is an inset full-height drawer: empty message and free-shipping meter at top; generous empty middle; recommendation, subtotal, explanatory line, full-width checkout CTA at bottom. An empty cart still presents a checkout button, a questionable cue. No purchase or cart change was attempted. Cart drawer takes viewport width390 with inner panel16px inset. Desktop Escape closed the drawer, but immediate inspection still found focus on Close, so reliable cart focus restoration is not established.

Source: shared UI on [Home](https://www.rhodeskin.com/).

## Editorial, help, forms and footer

[About](https://www.rhodeskin.com/pages/about-us) preserves strong mobile portrait images, but converts split features to image-over-copy and stacks the two expert bios. Ingredient education uses a roughly200px square card rail with a partial next card. Mobile observed modules include founder note558px, product philosophy539px, editorial image458px, values782px, ingredient rail437px, advisor area1195px. The large founder hero remains tall; this is an editorial choice, not a universal height for utility pages.

[FAQ](https://www.rhodeskin.com/pages/faq) turns the desktop left category sidebar into horizontal pills above a single-column disclosure list. Questions use full-row clickable areas and right-edge circular pluses. Category selection scrolls to the corresponding section; it does not remove all other sections. Expanded shipping tables overflow within their own horizontal region with previous/next controls rather than widening the page. The FAQ hero is about458px mobile. Avoid demanding this large photographic preamble on Helix's practical help routes.

[Contact](https://www.rhodeskin.com/pages/contact-us) has one mobile card with service copy, required-field note, full-width form fields, large textarea, attachment area, submit and provider privacy notice. Fields are about58px high and use placeholder-based visual labeling. No input or submission performed. [Login](https://www.rhodeskin.com/account) redirects to the public login page and uses one tall card with a centered narrow form, email/password, sign-in and recovery/registration links. The extensive empty space in the login card is not a usability requirement.

Mobile footer sequence is: large wordmark image → newsletter copy and side-by-side email/subscribe row → privacy text → divider → **three visible link columns** → support details and link → privacy opt-outs/cookie preferences → payment brand → divider → region → copyright. These are not collapsed accordions. At390 the three columns wrap longer links; meaningful separation is kept between rows. Preserve Helix's real destinations and public facts instead of copying Rhode support hours, legal claims, payment brands or region options.

## Limits and exclusions

This is viewport emulation in the in-app browser, not physical iOS/Android device testing. Touch gestures, virtual keyboard behavior, VoiceOver/TalkBack, reduced-motion preference, real checkout, authenticated account/order pages and populated cart were not validated. The gallery's horizontal arrangement, next-image peek and progress track are observed; successful finger-swipe behavior is not established. Only public unauthenticated surfaces were inspected. No signups, support messages, transactions, or remote mutations were made.
