# Secondary-packaging supplier and system scan

- **Decision served:** choose an evidence-backed development architecture and RFQ panel for cartons, labels, direct decoration, first-opening evidence, inserts, coding, accessibility, and retail presentation across the six provisional Helix Product Variants.
- **Market and destination:** U.S.-first, California-aware; Southern California receipt and DTC fulfillment.
- **Evidence date:** 31 August 2026.
- **Scope boundary:** public sources only. No supplier was contacted. No private quotation, final primary-package dimensions, production dieline, final artwork, filled transit result, or commercial approval is represented here.

> This report is a sourcing screen, not an award. Public price examples are directional leads, not comparable landed quotations. Exact cartons, labels, seals, inserts, decoration, coding, and claims remain configuration-specific and must pass the project's hard gates before weighted scoring or purchase.

## 1. Executive decision

Helix should not buy one identical secondary-packaging system for all six variants. The lowest-risk, lowest-regret development architecture is a **two-track system**:

1. **Lean/direct track:** direct-decorated primary container, durable unit-level lot code, and the smallest validated first-opening feature, with no unit carton when the exact immediate container can lawfully carry the information and survives the distribution/retail route.
2. **Right-sized carton track:** a restrained, white paperboard folding carton with no window, no plastic lamination, no foil, no magnetic or rigid-box construction, and no insert unless filled-product tests prove one is necessary.

Run both tracks for the cleanser. Begin the glass serum and cream in right-sized cartons, then challenge each carton and each insert with an elimination test. This is not a conclusion that glass always needs a carton; it is a reversible development sequence that protects the fragile, information-constrained formats while primary dimensions and filled-product behavior are still provisional.

## 2. Evidence rules and decision boundary

The screen covers nine carton converters/integrators, nine label converters or enterprise controls, six named direct-decoration providers, four expanded-content/information-insert systems, and multiple band/liner/coding suppliers. Some companies span categories; the report does not inflate credibility by counting the same commercial entity twice. Inclusion means the public record is credible enough for a bounded comparison or RFQ—not that Helix has verified capacity, factory, quality, compliance, price, or fit.

### 2.1 Claim labels used throughout

- **[V] Verified public observation:** a current selector, price, address, catalogue option, government rule, certificate, or named first-party case is directly visible in the cited source.
- **[SA] Supplier or brand assertion:** the supplier or brand states a performance, origin, certification, environmental, quality, or capability claim that Helix has not independently validated for the exact item.
- **[SI] Supported inference:** a bounded Helix interpretation drawn from cited facts and project prerequisites.
- **[Lead] Lead only:** credible enough to quote or sample; not commercially or technically qualified.
- **[U] Unknown:** public evidence did not resolve the point.

Supplier websites are first-party evidence of what the supplier publishes, not independent proof that the proposed Helix production item will have that attribute. A family-level FSC, ISO, G7, recycled-content, or origin claim does not transfer automatically to a purchase order.

### 2.2 Hard gates before price or weighted score

Each proposed configuration must pass the existing [quality, compatibility, and supplier gates](./packaging-quality-compatibility-supplier-gates.md). At minimum:

- exact legal entity, converter/factory, address, country of origin, and subcontracting path;
- exact drawing, board/facestock grade, caliper or grammage, component mass, tolerance, revision, and controlled-change terms;
- full material stack for board, ink, coating, varnish, laminate, foil, label, adhesive, liner, seal, and insert;
- California toxics-in-packaging certificate for the exact purchased component;
- transaction-linked FSC claim where FSC will be represented, plus exact recycled-content basis and chain of custody;
- production-intent proof/sample, color target, defect standard, AQL or equivalent inspection plan, lot traceability, and complaint/change process;
- filled-product fit, rub/scuff, label adhesion, seal, code, transit, leakage, glass-containment, and accessibility results;
- comparable P3 fulfillment-ready landed cost per accepted saleable unit, with P4 delivery economics kept separate.

Missing evidence is an unknown, not a zero and not a pass. Suppliers that fail a hard gate are not rescued by a lower web price or a stronger sustainability story.

### 2.3 Provisional dimensions and capacities

No final primary-package drawing exists in this report. Therefore:

- no carton inside dimension, board grain, lock geometry, insert cutout, label dieline, or code location is frozen;
- a nominal bottle or jar capacity is not its maximum decorated dimension, center of gravity, filled mass, or safe carton clearance;
- a carton stated to “hold one pound” is not evidence of glass-drop or DTC performance;
- point caliper and gsm are not interchangeable specifications across board grades;
- the 15 g and 50 g cream declarations cannot be converted into jar milliliters without the final Formula density, headspace, closure displacement, fill temperature, and process-capability study.

## 3. Required information, label area, and hierarchy

The FDA's current Cosmetics Labeling Guide makes the outer package part of the label system when one exists, but it does not erase every immediate-container obligation. The final retail configuration must map required information across the immediate container and any outer carton. [FDA Cosmetics Labeling Guide](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-regulations/cosmetics-labeling-guide)

For Fair Packaging and Labeling Act commodities, the net quantity needs both U.S. customary and SI units. NIST's current guidance likewise says a metric-only package does not satisfy the federal dual-unit requirement. [15 U.S.C. § 1453](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title15-section1453) · [NIST packaging and labeling](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/packaging-and-labeling)

### 3.1 Provisional information hierarchy

This is a space-allocation brief, not final legal copy:

| Surface | Reserve first | Secondary use | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Principal display panel | Helix, Product identity, variant/size role, legally reviewed dual-unit net quantity | Minimal Product descriptor | That a website or QR code can replace required on-pack information |
| Side/rear panel | ingredients, directions, warnings where applicable, Responsible Person contact path, required qualifications | substantiated claims and concise use guidance | That sparse design permits unreadably small type |
| Secondary side/bottom | UPC/retail barcode, lot/code clear zone, internal SKU | disposal instructions only after package-specific substantiation | That carton-only lot identity is enough after carton disposal |
| Immediate container | identity, quantity and other required immediate-container elements as the final configuration requires; durable lot traceability | short directions or QR code | That an outer carton makes the primary legally or operationally blank |

Ingredient text is generally at least 1/16 inch, with a narrow 1/32-inch provision when available label surface is below 12 square inches; exact applicability must be measured on physical artwork. Minis are not automatically exempt merely because they are travel size. [FDA Cosmetics Labeling Guide](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-regulations/cosmetics-labeling-guide)

For early geometry budgeting, FDA's guide describes a rectangular package PDP as one entire side, a cylindrical PDP as 40% of height multiplied by circumference, and an “other shape” PDP as 40% of total surface excluding tops, bottoms, shoulders, and necks. Net quantity normally occupies the bottom 30% of the PDP, parallel to the base; the bottom-position rule is relaxed at 5 square inches or less, but prominence and readability are not. Federal minimum net-quantity type heights begin at 1/16 inch for a PDP at or below 5 square inches, 1/8 inch above 5 through 25 square inches, and 3/16 inch above 25 through 100 square inches. These are regulatory floors, not Helix readability targets. [FDA Cosmetics Labeling Guide](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-regulations/cosmetics-labeling-guide)

### 3.2 Measured label-area deliverable

For each of the six final primary drawings, create a label-area worksheet before any award:

1. measured principal display panel using the applicable package geometry;
2. total available label surface and exclusions for curvature, seams, tube crimp, closure overlap, shoulders, base push-up, and frosting/coating transition;
3. all required text at physical scale, not screen zoom;
4. dual-unit declaration block and clear space;
5. lot-code location that remains with the Product;
6. retail barcode quiet zone and scan orientation;
7. decoration/label tolerance, including wrap seam and label skew;
8. accessible opening tab, grip, and contrast clearances.

The 10 mL serum and 15 g cream Mini are the highest-risk formats. If the immediate container cannot carry its required information legibly, the answer is not smaller type; it is a larger primary, a lawful outer information layer, or a different architecture.

### 3.3 GTIN and barcode architecture

Each sellable Mini and Full has different declared net content and therefore needs a distinct GTIN. The six proposed sale units require at least six GTIN-12s; a later declared-content change triggers a new GTIN and must be propagated to relevant higher packaging levels. [GS1 cosmetics barcodes](https://www.gs1us.org/upcs-barcodes-prefixes/barcodes-for-cosmetic-products) · [GS1 declared net content rule](https://www.gs1.org/1/gtinrules/en/rule/266/declared-net-content)

- Put the active point-of-sale barcode on the carton when the carton is the sale unit, or on the primary when cartonless.
- Do not expose multiple active consumer barcodes.
- Reserve a flat, matte or otherwise scan-proven, high-contrast zone before selecting varnish, foil, frosting, or metallic decoration.
- GS1 US gives a nominal UPC-A size of about 1.644 × 1.153 inches and a minimum-magnification width of about 1.174 inches with bar height about 0.72 inches; quiet zones still apply. Verify the final printed symbol rather than trusting digital artwork. [GS1 US UPC-A guidance](https://www.help.gs1us.org/work-with-my-upc-a-barcode)
- Prefer lower-right rear placement when the geometry permits. Where a curved surface exceeds the supplier/GS1 scanning envelope, evaluate ladder orientation rather than wrapping bars around the curve.
- Create separate case/higher-level identifiers; do not use exposed repeats of consumer UPCs as the shipping-case identification method. [GS1 US packaging levels](https://www.help.gs1us.org/packaging-level) · [case barcode guidance](https://www.help.gs1us.org/create-a-case-and-generate-a-barcode)

### 4.2 No-carton decision model

Do not compare a carton price with zero. Compare complete accepted-unit systems:

**Cartonless advantage per accepted unit**

= avoided carton recurring cost  
+ avoided carton tooling/proof amortization  
+ avoided insertion, inspection, storage, inbound freight, and obsolescence  
− added direct decoration or label cost  
− added first-opening feature/application  
− added immediate-container information solution  
− added master-case protection and inbound scuff, leak, breakage, rejection, and Product-loss cost.

Run the comparison at 1,000, 2,000, and 3,000 finished units per Product Variant and at the project's 10/90, 25/75, and 50/50 Mini/Full demand mixes. Put costs through accepted fulfillment-ready inventory in the project's P3 metric. Keep parcel protection, customer delivery, outbound damage, reshipment, return, and service effects in a separate P4 delta. Do not blend the ledgers. See the [landed-cost evidence model](./packaging-landed-cost-evidence-model.md).

Keep a second protection break-even outside P3:

**required avoided-damage rate**

= incremental parcel-delivered cost of the carton configuration per shipped order  
÷ fully loaded cost of one damaged order.

The damaged-order denominator includes lost Product/packaging, pick/pack, delivery, replacement/refund, support time, disposal/return, and customer-remediation cost under the project's approved P4 boundary. Use observed route data or a labeled sensitivity, never an invented “cartons prevent X%” assumption.

### 4.3 When no carton is likely to win

**[SI]** The cartonless path is strongest when:

- the primary is a low-breakage tube rather than glass;
- the exact primary has enough lawful, readable information area;
- direct decoration or a single label survives wet hands, oil, abrasion, flexing, and aging;
- first-opening evidence is built into the closure or supplied by a very low-mass feature;
- retail does not require a hang tab, theft-deterrence format, shelf-block shape, or flat barcode panel;
- parcel/master-case tests pass without adding more material than the unit carton removed;
- saved labor and obsolete printed inventory exceed the cost of more complex primary decoration.

### 4.4 When a carton is likely to win

**[SI]** A carton is more defensible when:

- glass scuff, pipette/closure impact, or jar movement causes unacceptable damage;
- a Mini cannot carry required text and dual-unit quantity legibly;
- the retailer needs a stable shelf block, barcode panel, seal, hanger, or presentation standard;
- common primary components can stay undecorated while cartons localize copy or variants;
- a carton materially reduces reships, Product loss, or customer injury exposure;
- the carton replaces a less favorable plastic insert, full-body sleeve, or oversized parcel dunnage.

### 4.5 Retail-ready boundary

There is no single universal retail-ready cosmetics specification in the public evidence reviewed. Exact barcode symbology, label position, shelf dimensions, security, hanger, master-case, and vendor-compliance requirements are buyer- and channel-specific. Treat DTC, specialty retail, and mass retail as separate configurations until a named buyer supplies its current requirements. A generic “retail ready” supplier claim is not a gate pass.

### 4.6 Right-sizing and California slack fill

California treats nonfunctional slack fill in an opaque package as misleading. Listed functional reasons can include protection, machinery needs, unavoidable settling, required label area, pilfer deterrence or handling, and dispensing functions. A carton chosen only to reuse a “universal” dieline across Mini and Full could therefore create both cost/cube waste and a legal-review problem. [California Business and Professions Code § 12606](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=12606.)

For every proposed carton, preserve the primary and carton dimensions, calculated void, protection/label rationale, photos, engineering notes, and filled-pack test result. Do not assume a DTC fact pattern will remain valid if the same sale unit later enters retail.

### 6.2 Carton specification direction

The starting brief is a **white, non-windowed, one- or two-color editorial folding carton**:

- begin with uncoated or low-coated SBS/FBB in the supplier's proven 14–18 pt range for tubes and 18–24 pt range for glass;
- compare straight/reverse tuck for light units and snap-lock/auto-bottom for heavy jars;
- require board stiffness, grain, moisture, compression, fold/crack, and rub data rather than choosing on caliper alone;
- use aqueous or low-burden protective coating only when rub/scuff tests require it;
- exclude plastic windows, full plastic lamination, foil, metallization, magnets, foam, and molded plastic trays from the baseline;
- compare one-color black, two-color, and CMYK quotes; do not add embellishment until the quiet Helix system is proven without it;
- leave a code zone that the filler can mark without coating/ink interference;
- require carton mass, flat-pack case count, pallet pattern, and overrun/underrun policy for the landed-cost and SB 54 ledgers.

### 6.3 Insert decision

The insert hierarchy is:

1. no insert;
2. carton geometry alone, including lock bottom and controlled clearances;
3. small paperboard collar, platform, base pad, or neck retainer;
4. corrugated or molded fiber only if paperboard fails;
5. plastic thermoform or foam only as a documented exception after lower-burden systems fail.

For serum, test pipette/collar impact and bottle movement. For cream, test jar base, closure, center of gravity, and glass containment. The final parcel and master case—not the attractive unit carton alone—must pass with filled production-equivalent units.

### 6.4 Protective insert versus information insert

“Insert” has two different cost and compliance meanings:

- a **protective retainer** positions the primary and belongs in the filled transit test;
- an **information leaflet/card** adds copy or education but does not protect the Product and creates another component, insertion step, reconciliation risk, and SB 54 ledger row.

Do not add a loose marketing card to every unit by default. A QR code and accessible web page may supplement Product education, while mandatory physical information stays on the lawful label system. A loose leaflet, belly band, or sleeve must not be the sole carrier of lot identity or required information if it can separate from the sale unit.

### 7.2 Product-contact boundary and application tests

A label on the outside of a bottle, jar, tube, or carton is part of the package BOM but is not the intended direct Formula-contact layer. No reviewed label supplier source authorizes a proposed facestock, ink, laminate, or adhesive for direct contact with a Helix cosmetic Formula. A food-contact adhesive declaration does not establish cosmetic Formula contact, migration performance, or compatibility.

Required label trials:

- **Resilient cleanser tube:** squeezable film, repeated flex/evacuation, wet hands, cleanser smear, cap contact, mandrel lift, freezer/heat cycling, and aging.
- **Crinkle-retaining tube:** test label bridge, cracking, wrinkling, ink fracture, edge lift, and whether a label defeats the intended crinkle behavior. Direct printing is the preferred aesthetic if it passes.
- **Frosted/amber serum glass:** exact frosting/coating surface energy, curvature, clear/white ink opacity, edge lift, oil/serum smear, condensation, abrasion, and pipette drips.
- **Frosted cream jar:** low vertical panel, base radius, lid contact, wet/oily fingers, bathroom humidity, and repeated wipe/abrasion.
- **All machine-applied labels:** exact core ID, roll OD, unwind, gap, splice allowance, liner, labels per roll, sensor registration, application speed, and reject handling.

For most web-to-print label candidates, the exact producing factory, machine, quality-system scope, and country of origin for the Helix order remain publicly unresolved. Their speed and transparent prices are useful only if the RFQ converts that anonymity into a controlled factory/material record.

### Coding rule

- Put a readable lot identifier on the immediate container or a durable label that remains with it.
- A carton may repeat the code for receiving/retail use, but carton-only coding is insufficient after disposal.
- Static artwork and UPC may be preprinted. Variable batch, lot, and date data should normally be applied and verified at filling/packout.
- Do not buy a coder for launch if the qualified domestic filler already owns suitable, maintained, validated equipment.
- Require a substrate/coding trial for frosted glass, dark amber glass, white tube, closure, label, and carton.
- Define contrast, character height, position tolerance, rub/water/oil resistance, light/aging, scan grade if encoded, reconciliation, rework, and record retention.
- Do not add an expiration or period-after-opening representation until final Formula/package stability supports it.

## 11. Accessibility and inclusive use

Accessibility is both a physical-opening problem and an information problem. No public source reviewed established a universal tactile/Braille mandate for these ordinary U.S. cosmetics. That absence is not a legal opinion; counsel must review final channels and claims. Helix should nonetheless set measurable inclusive-use requirements.

### 11.1 Minimum development requirements

- required text at legal minimum or larger, with measured physical contrast and no low-contrast silver-on-frosted copy for essential information;
- clear Mini/Full differentiation that does not rely only on color;
- wet-hand grip and opening torque testing for black and white jar closures;
- a band tear strip or liner tab large enough to locate and grip;
- no sharp torn band, foil, carton, or label edge;
- no seal that requires a tool unless intentionally specified and justified;
- one-handed tube opening/closing where the cap concept permits;
- clear open/close direction and first-use instruction;
- consumer test participants with low vision and reduced grip/dexterity represented in the sample;
- code/UPC placement that does not obscure instructions or require destructive label removal.

MCC publishes a Braille-embossed label system, duraSCULPT Braille, as an optional inclusion feature; Selig and TekniPlex describe easy-open tabbed liners. These are supplier capabilities, not evidence that Helix needs those exact systems or that they meet a final accessibility standard. [MCC CARE collection](https://www.mcclabel.com/en/the-care-collection) · [Selig induction sealing](https://www.seliggroup.com/induction-cap-sealing-in-tamper-evident-pharmaceutical-packaging/) · [TekniPlex EdgePull](https://tekni-plex.com/en/consumer-products/products/edgepull)

Relevant voluntary design references include ISO 17480 for ease of opening, ISO 22015 for manipulation, ISO 19809 for accessible packaging information/marking, and ISO 24508 for tactile symbols. Their relevance must be mapped to the exact package and user need; listing them does not establish conformity. [ISO 17480](https://www.iso.org/standard/59881.html) · [ISO 22015](https://www.iso.org/standard/72368.html) · [ISO 19809](https://www.iso.org/standard/66239.html) · [ISO 24508](https://www.iso.org/standard/69767.html)

### 11.2 Accessibility gate

Record opening torque/peel force, successful first attempt, completion time, instruction comprehension, label readability, and damage/injury observations for each final configuration. A “tamper evident” feature that many intended users cannot open is not acceptable merely because it is inexpensive.

## 12. Sustainability, California, and claims

### 12.1 Component-level scope

California's Toxics in Packaging law reaches primary, secondary, and tertiary packages and components, including coatings, inks, labels, pigments, adhesives, stabilizers, and additives. Suppliers must provide a signed Certificate of Compliance, and the purchaser must retain it while the component remains in use. [DTSC Toxics in Packaging](https://dtsc.ca.gov/toxics-in-products/toxics-in-packaging/) · [DTSC purchaser fact sheet](https://dtsc.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2016/01/TIPPurchasers_FINAL-1.pdf)

California SB 54 requires a component-level covered-material analysis; labels, seals, cartons, inserts, shippers, tape, and protective material are not ignored simply because the primary is glass. Permanent regulations took effect in 2026, and category/producer implementation is still evolving. [CalRecycle SB 54 program](https://calrecycle.ca.gov/packaging/packaging-epr/) · [final regulations](https://www2.calrecycle.ca.gov/Docs/Web/138757) · [Identifying Covered Materials](https://www2.calrecycle.ca.gov/Docs/Web/138814)

### 12.2 The small-paper Mini issue

CalRecycle's current Covered Material Category guidance places paper/fiber items with at least two sides at or below two inches into small-paper categories. The 2026 list currently does not designate those categories recyclable unless the specified at-least-90% size-sortation retention exception is demonstrated. This is consequential for likely 10–15 mL/g Mini cartons and must be determined from the **exact finished dimensions and separable components**, not from a rendering. The category result still does not authorize or prohibit a consumer-facing claim by itself. [2026 Covered Material Category list](https://www2.calrecycle.ca.gov/Docs/Publications/137815)

**[SI]** Several serum/cream Mini cartons may trigger that geometry. Do not enlarge a carton merely to escape a category: that may add avoidable material and deceptive empty space. Measure each dieline, apply the current CalRecycle method, compare cartonless feasibility, and omit recyclability claims until the exact item, current law, and counsel support them.

### 12.3 FSC and recycled-content evidence

FSC chain of custody concerns traceable forest-based sourcing and sales claims; it does not prove recyclability, recycled content, low carbon, or overall environmental superiority. For a finished-product claim, the proposed item must be in the supplier's scope and transaction documents must carry the applicable claim and certificate information. A certificate copy or website logo alone is insufficient. [FSC Chain of Custody standard](https://open.fsc.org/handle/resource/302) · [FSC promotional-use requirements](https://us.fsc.org/promotional-use)

For each carton, insert, or paper label, request:

- supplier and exact manufacturing-site FSC certificate/scope;
- board/paper mill, grade, basis weight/caliper, and claimed input category;
- exact post-consumer and pre-consumer percentages with denominator;
- purchase-order and invoice claim;
- trademark/label approval path if the logo or claim will be used;
- coating, ink, adhesive, liner, foil, and laminate identities;
- component mass and yield;
- controlled substitution and change-notice terms.

### 12.4 Environmental claim rule

Default to no recyclability, compostability, biodegradable, carbon, refillable, “eco,” or generalized environmental benefit on Product, carton, label, website, or retail copy. California's SB 343 implementation remains legally unsettled after a 2026 preliminary injunction; that is not substantiation for a claim. The producer remains responsible for the exact package assessment. [CalRecycle Accurate Recycling Labels](https://calrecycle.ca.gov/wcs/recyclinglabels/)

Use the project's [15-point evidence-adjusted sustainability score](./packaging-sustainability-scoring.md) only after safety, legality, quality, compatibility, and supplier identity pass. Avoidable material, actual recovered-system evidence, and Product-loss prevention are distinct; do not award the same fiber or PCR claim multiple times.

### 12.5 Proposition 65 reserve

Proposition 65 is exposure-based; a listed substance's presence does not by itself establish that a warning is required. Do not print a precautionary warning without an exposure-based determination. If final review finds a warning necessary, reserve physical space and provide the required pre-purchase online treatment; the carton does not replace the online duty. Recheck the current safe-harbor text and rule status at artwork lock. [OEHHA plain-language guide](https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65/general-info/proposition-65-plain-language) · [OEHHA safe-harbor warning rules](https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65/crnr/proposition-65-clear-and-reasonable-warnings-safe-harbor-methods-and-content)

## 14. Brand and design-rights guardrails

The [reference-packaging design-rights screen](./reference-packaging-design-rights-screen.md) requires independent Helix expression. Secondary packaging should:

- use Helix's editorial, modern, sparse, ingredient-literate language;
- reserve Marcellus for controlled display use and Manrope for functional copy;
- avoid Rhode's monochrome vertical wordmark hierarchy, Aesop's dense black apothecary rules, and Haramoon's technical-grid/blue-accent system;
- use ordinary stock structural geometry rather than source-identifying trade dress;
- keep required copy factual and readable;
- treat direct decoration, labels, and cartons as one coherent system, not three unrelated visual treatments.

The visual baseline is a quiet white or natural-white board, black functional typography, disciplined spacing, and structure-driven tactility. Premium finishes must solve a measured performance problem or survive a separate cost/sustainability challenge.

## 15. Common RFQ specification

Issue one controlled RFQ revision to every bidder. Quote all six Product Variants separately at 1,000, 2,000, and 3,000 accepted pieces, plus the 10/90, 25/75, and 50/50 Mini/Full mixes where gang-run economics apply.

### 15.1 Commercial and cost fields

### 15.2 Technical fields

## 16. Qualification and test plan

### Stage 1 — paper screen

Reject or hold candidates that will not disclose factory, exact material stack, California certificate path, controlled changes, or comparable landed-cost inputs. Do not score them.

### Stage 2 — structure and artwork-free fit

- receive existing-die samples;
- measure primary and secondary clearances;
- check insertion/extraction, closure interference, panel bow, carton squareness, glue, and code zones;
- compare no insert, paperboard restraint, and stock carton;
- weigh every component and record flat/assembled cube.

### Stage 3 — decoration, label, seal, and code

- drawdown and physical proof under controlled lighting;
- tape/cross-hatch or supplier-appropriate adhesion, rub/scuff, wet, oil/Formula smear, bathroom humidity, thermal cycle, flex, and aging;
- barcode/code readability and permanence;
- band perforation and frangible-label evidence;
- liner seal window, leak, peel force, tab grip, residue, and reclosure where applicable.

### Stage 4 — filled distribution and use

Test each exact Formula, primary, secondary, insert, lot code, master case, DTC parcel, and route. Define before testing:

- zero leakage;
- no glass escape;
- closure and pipette remain functional;
- required information and code remain readable;
- no unacceptable carton crush, scuff, label lift, decoration loss, or sharp edge;
- acceptable Product loss/residual and consumer opening;
- no insert unless the no-insert configuration fails a predefined requirement.

### Stage 5 — cost and sustainability

Build P3 with accepted yield, inbound freight/duty, tooling amortization, working capital, inbound damage/rejection, obsolete inventory, and fill/pack labor. Build the customer-delivery protection and damage/reship delta separately at P4. Run the 15-point sustainability score with evidence confidence. Only then apply the project's 45/20/15/10/10 weighted award model.

### Stage 6 — pilot and release

- lock drawings/BOMs and golden samples;
- complete final regulatory/artwork review;
- run line qualification and pilot lot;
- reconcile first production against samples, certificates, weights, decoration, codes, and factory;
- release lots only through the authorized quality process.

## 19. Conclusion

The cheapest credible launch architecture is not “buy the lowest web-priced carton.” It is the lowest **qualified complete system** that gets an accepted, readable, intact, easy-to-open Product through filling, retail or DTC, customer use, and recall traceability.

Public evidence supports a strong first competition:

- domestic digital cartons from YBS, Sttark, Packlane, and UPrinting;
- PackMojo as the offshore landed-cost challenger and PakFactory as the integrated carton/insert lead;
- EcoEnclose as the unprinted stock floor and Oliver/JohnsByrne as the quality ceiling;
- Lightning, Wizard, Avery, SheetLabels, and Sttark for pressure-sensitive labels;
- local direct decoration versus integrated primary/filler decoration;
- low-cost bands versus better-integrated liners/closure features;
- filler-owned immediate-container coding.

For now, use cartons as **testable protection and information devices**, not as automatic luxury signals. Begin with no insert, remove any carton that cannot prove its value, and do not enlarge a Mini or make an environmental claim to solve a category or marketing problem. Final award waits for common quotes, exact drawings, production-intent samples, hard-gate evidence, and accepted-unit landed cost.
