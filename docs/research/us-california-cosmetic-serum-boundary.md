# U.S. and California cosmetic boundary for the helix treatment serum

**Research ticket:** [Establish the U.S. and California cosmetic boundary](https://github.com/brandon-y-lee/helix/issues/290)

**Decision date:** 4 September 2026

**Decision status:** current public-source regulatory and claims baseline for a Q1 2027 launch; not a legal opinion, FDA clearance, California approval, final safety assessment, or authorization to sell

**Assumed product and route:** one fragrance-free, leave-on, 30 mL facial treatment serum, made by a Korean ODM, imported and sold direct to U.S. consumers including California, positioned only as a cosmetic and intended to replace Peptide Bounce

**Supplier context:** Ecoment and Haramoon catalog concepts are treated as candidate starting formulas. Supplier decks, ingredient stories, efficacy summaries, and convention conversations are inputs to diligence—not instructions, regulatory conclusions, or substantiation for the modified helix formula.

> This report translates public authorities into formula, claim, evidence, and operating gates. It does not decide whether a particular formula is safe, whether a particular exposure needs a Proposition 65 warning, or whether a study substantiates final copy. U.S./California regulatory counsel and a qualified cosmetic safety assessor should approve the final formula, label, claim matrix, and launch file. Refresh every linked authority before formula lock and again before artwork release because several federal rules remain unfinished.

## Executive decision

The serum can follow a fast cosmetic-only route if helix treats five matters as hard launch gates:

1. **helix should be prepared to act as the Responsible Person.** If helix is the manufacturer, packer, or distributor named on the label, it owns the federal safety-substantiation, adverse-event, contact, and ordinarily product-listing duties. A Korean ODM's certificate or contract does not transfer those statutory duties.
2. **Choose a formula that is California-compliant on 1 January 2027, not merely compliant when sampled in 2026.** The final quantitative formula, every raw-material subingredient, intentional function, impurity profile, and packaging migration risk must be screened against California's existing PFAS and ingredient bans and the additional 2027 prohibitions. Inventory manufactured earlier but offered for sale in California in Q1 2027 still meets the sale prohibition.
3. **Keep the intended use at the appearance/sensory level.** “Visibly smoother,” “the appearance of fine lines,” “more even-looking tone,” “glow,” “hydration,” and “skin feels firmer” can support a cosmetic position. Claims to prevent or reverse photoaging, repair UV damage, stimulate collagen, regenerate cells, reduce inflammation, alter melanin, microneedle skin, or enhance delivery through living skin create drug/device risk and are outside this launch path.
4. **Substantiate the exact finished helix formula before publishing objective claims.** A catalog-base study, branded-ingredient brochure, in-vitro mechanism, or study on an unmodified formula does not automatically prove the claim for helix's formula. “Clinically proven,” percentages, time-to-result, sensitive-skin, and compatibility claims require especially close fit between the tested product, population, protocol, endpoint, statistic, and final words.
5. **Use narrowly defined ethical and environmental representations.** “Fragrance-free,” “vegan,” “cruelty-free,” “clean,” “non-toxic,” and “sustainable” are not FDA preapproved quality grades. Each carries express and implied messages. For a lean launch, use a strict fragrance-free formulation standard, consider “vegan formula” only after source-chain evidence, defer “cruelty-free” until the California animal-testing file and claim scope are auditable, and omit broad clean/environmental claims.

The fastest defensible concept is therefore a conventional topical serum with no spicule/microneedling mechanism, no sunscreen or acne use, no drug-active ingredient, no intentionally added fragrance or PFAS, a conservative California-ready exclusion screen, appearance-only claims, and a finished-product safety and claim dossier.

## 1. Authority and status legend

The distinction matters: a legal duty, a nonbinding FDA document, an enforcement signal, and a retailer program do not have the same force.

| Status | Meaning | Treatment for this project |
|---|---|---|
| **Binding law** | Enacted statute or effective regulation when its factual conditions are met | Hard gate; final applicability and ambiguous facts go to counsel |
| **Final agency guidance** | Current agency interpretation that does not itself bind FDA or the public | Strong operating baseline; statute/regulation controls if inconsistent |
| **Draft guidance / pending rule** | Agency thinking or planned regulation not yet effective | Do not represent as law; preserve data and label space, and refresh before release |
| **Enforcement risk** | Import alert, warning-letter pattern, inspection authority, recall authority, or private-enforcement exposure | Treat as a practical launch risk even when it is not an additional product standard |
| **Retailer / certification overlay** | Voluntary commercial contract, badge, or private standard | Apply only if helix chooses that channel or claim; never call it legal compliance |
| **helix recommendation** | Conservative internal policy selected for speed, credibility, and lower rework | Put into the ODM specification and approval workflow; it can be stricter than law |

Federal cosmetics law is the national floor. California may still prohibit or limit ingredients and enforce state ingredient-reporting and exposure-warning laws; MoCRA expressly preserves important parts of that authority in [21 U.S.C. § 364j](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A21+section%3A364j+edition%3Aprelim%29). Compliance with one layer never establishes compliance with another.

## 3. Federal cosmetic-only boundary

### 3.1 Classification turns on intended use, not the bottle or the word “serum”

The FD&C Act defines a cosmetic by its intended use to cleanse, beautify, promote attractiveness, or alter appearance; a drug includes an article intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent disease or affect body structure or function ([21 U.S.C. § 321(g), (i)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title21-section321)). FDA's current explanation makes clear that intended use can be established by package copy, websites, advertising, social content, consumer perception, and ingredient context—not only the INCI panel ([FDA, “Is It a Cosmetic, a Drug, or Both?”](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap)).

This means that the same moisturizing base may remain a cosmetic with appearance claims but become an unapproved drug through claims about inflammation, cellular regeneration, collagen synthesis, melanin, UV protection, acne, eczema, or other treatment. FDA does not preapprove ordinary cosmetic claims or labels, and market examples are not safe harbors ([FDA cosmetic labeling claims](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling/cosmetics-labeling-claims)). “Cosmeceutical” has no meaning under the FD&C Act and should not be used as a regulatory category ([FDA on “cosmeceutical”](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-claims/cosmeceutical)).

### 3.2 Product-route exclusions for the Q1 2027 brief

The following are outside the authorized cosmetic-only route and require reformulation, removal of the use/claim, or a separate counsel-led drug/device analysis:

- sunscreen, SPF, broad-spectrum, sunburn, UV protection, or prevention/reversal of UV damage;
- acne treatment/prevention, eczema/dermatitis/rosacea treatment, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial treatment, wound healing, scar treatment, or treatment of irritation as a condition;
- increasing collagen/elastin synthesis, regenerating cells/tissue, repairing DNA, raising NAD in skin, changing cellular metabolism, or otherwise changing living structure/function;
- inhibiting/reducing melanin, bleaching/whitening skin, or treating hyperpigmentation rather than improving the **appearance** of uneven tone;
- prescription-drug ingredients or an OTC drug active/use not covered by an applicable monograph and full drug compliance;
- “natural microneedling,” puncturing living skin, transdermal delivery, increased penetration into living layers, wound response, or collagen induction; and
- directions to combine with a prescription treatment in a way that implies enhanced treatment or drug delivery.

FDA's wrinkle guidance distinguishes moisturization that makes lines look less noticeable from claims to remove wrinkles or increase collagen production ([FDA wrinkle and anti-aging products](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/wrinkle-treatments-and-other-anti-aging-products)). FDA also treats penetration into living skin, collagen production, and delivery of cosmetics through microneedling as device/drug-adjacent territory; FDA states that microneedling devices are not cleared to deliver cosmetics into skin ([FDA microneedling devices](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices/microneedling-devices); [FDA microneedling guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/regulatory-considerations-microneedling-products)). For this launch, a spicule formula promoted as microneedling or enhanced delivery is a **no-go**, not a copy-edit exercise. A surface exfoliation theory would need product-specific physical evidence and classification review before reconsideration.

### 3.3 Appearance and sensory territory that can remain cosmetic

Subject to truthful presentation and adequate substantiation, the claim architecture should stay within:

- immediate and lasting hydration;
- skin looks smoother, more luminous, refreshed, or rested;
- visible improvement in the appearance of fine lines caused by dryness;
- more even-looking tone and reduced appearance of dullness or dark spots;
- skin feels soft, supple, or firmer;
- visibly refined texture;
- helps support the skin's moisture barrier or helps reduce moisture loss; and
- lightweight feel and a non-greasy glow/sheen.

“Firm-feeling skin” is materially safer than “rebuilds collagen to firm skin.” “Helps support the moisture barrier” is materially safer than “repairs a damaged barrier.” “Visibly brightens” is materially safer than “inhibits melanin.” The surrounding mechanism, imagery, ingredient callouts, testimonials, FAQs, influencer briefs, search metadata, and before/after presentation must preserve the same boundary.

## 4. MoCRA and other federal obligations

### 4.1 Binding obligation matrix

| Area | Binding rule for the assumed serum | Release gate |
|---|---|---|
| **Safety / adulteration** | The RP must ensure and maintain records of “adequate substantiation of safety”: evidence or information qualified experts consider sufficient to support a reasonable certainty that the cosmetic is safe, considering intended and customary use. A cosmetic lacking adequate substantiation is adulterated ([21 U.S.C. § 364d](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A21+section%3A364d+edition%3Aprelim%29); [21 U.S.C. § 361](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A21+section%3A361+edition%3Aprelim%29)). | Signed finished-product assessment plus accessible source records before sale. Recommend no intentionally added color for this lean serum. |

### 4.2 Registration/listing details for two possible Korean ODMs

FDA's current [registration and listing page](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/registration-listing-cosmetic-product-facilities-and-products) identifies Cosmetics Direct and Forms FDA 5066/5067 and explains that FDA uses an FEI for facility registration. The December 2024 [final registration/listing guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-registration-and-listing-cosmetic-product-facilities-and-products) is nonbinding but is the current operating guide.

- Each physical Ecoment or Haramoon site actually manufacturing or processing the helix serum needs its own applicability and registration check. A company-level certificate is insufficient.
- A facility registration or product listing is **not FDA approval, certification, endorsement, or safety review**. Do not show a registration number or “FDA registered” as a consumer benefit.
- One product listing can identify all facilities that manufacture/process the listed cosmetic. If two ODMs produce the same product, include the actual covered facilities.
- MoCRA's flexible grouping is limited to products with identical formulations or differences only in color, fragrance/flavor, or quantity. Two materially different catalog bases are not made one formula by using the same brand name. Treat different active systems or concentrations as separate controlled formulas and obtain FDA filing advice.
- A reformulation triggers a safety, claim, label/listing, California, stability, preservation, and change-control review. The fact that the product replaces Peptide Bounce does not permit carrying forward Peptide Bounce's listing or evidence without a documented match.

### 4.3 Small-business exemption: narrow and fact-dependent

[21 U.S.C. § 364h](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title21-section364h) exempts a qualifying business whose average U.S. gross annual cosmetic sales for the previous three years are below the inflation-adjusted statutory threshold (originally $1 million) from the future GMP rule and facility-registration/product-listing sections, unless it manufactures/processes specified higher-risk product types. The exemption does **not** remove the safety-substantiation, adverse-event, label-contact, adulteration, misbranding, import, or California obligations.

Do not assume a startup-brand exemption also covers a larger Korean ODM. Determine each legal person/facility separately, preserve sales calculations and product-type analysis, and obtain counsel's written conclusion. The internal recommendation remains to build listing-ready data and request a registered covered ODM site even if helix itself may qualify.

### 4.4 GMP and fragrance-allergen rules are not yet final product standards

MoCRA directs FDA to establish cosmetic GMP and federal fragrance-allergen labeling regulations. As of the decision date, FDA's current [MoCRA page](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/modernization-cosmetics-regulation-act-2022-mocra) still describes those regulations as required work and links FDA's older **draft** cosmetic GMP guidance; no effective MoCRA cosmetic GMP final rule or federal fragrance-allergen list was located. The [Unified Agenda entry for fragrance allergens, RIN 0910-AI90](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?RIN=0910-AI90&pubId=202504) remained at proposed-rule stage in the available agenda. Agenda dates are planning information, not law.

**Current legal floor:** the serum still may not be adulterated, contaminated, prepared/held under insanitary conditions, unsafe, or misbranded. **helix recommendation:** make ISO 22716-aligned controls, batch traceability, validated cleaning/water/preservation, deviation/CAPA, supplier qualification, change control, stability, complaint handling, and recall cooperation contractual now. Do not call an ISO certificate “FDA compliant” or assume it covers the exact site/product. Reserve label flexibility and conduct a federal-rule refresh 60 days before artwork lock and immediately before print authorization.

FDA's January 2026 [records-access draft guidance announcement](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-news-events/fda-issues-draft-guidance-industry-fda-records-access-authority-cosmetics-products) is useful preparation but remains draft and nonbinding. Its status should be stated whenever cited.

## 5. Federal label, INCI, import, and origin controls

### 5.1 Ingredient declaration is not the quantitative formula

FDA's [Cosmetic Labeling Guide](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-regulations/cosmetics-labeling-guide) and [ingredient-name explanation](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling/cosmetic-ingredient-names) provide the current operating detail:

- ingredients are generally declared by U.S. common/usual names in descending predominance;
- ingredients at 1% or less may be placed after those above 1% without descending order, and colors follow the applicable rule;
- “fragrance” or “flavor” can be used collectively under the federal declaration rule, but that does not remove MoCRA listing, California reporting, safety, or supplier-disclosure needs;
- an ingredient is not “incidental” merely because it is a carrier, processing aid, preservative within a blend, or omitted from a supplier's marketing INCI; the narrow 21 C.F.R. § 701.3(l) standard must actually be met;
- EU-style names alone can be wrong in the United States: for example, `Aqua`, `Parfum`, and `CI` nomenclature require U.S.-specific review; and
- percentages are not printed on the consumer ingredient statement, but helix needs the complete quantitative formula and subingredient breakdown under confidentiality for listing, California screening, safety, claims, and change control.

For a 30 mL liquid serum, the outer principal display panel ordinarily uses fluid measure plus metric; the final physical artwork must satisfy dual-unit, placement, type-size, business identity, and panel rules. The label also needs the monitored MoCRA contact path. There is no general federal cosmetic expiration-date mandate, but any stated shelf life/PAO and the period of safe marketed use must be supported.

### 5.2 Fragrance-free is a defined helix formula standard, not an FDA badge

FDA states there is no federal standard or definition governing “fragrance-free,” “unscented,” or “hypoallergenic” representations ([FDA allergens in cosmetics](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/allergens-cosmetics)). FDA also notes that “unscented” products may contain fragrance used to mask odor. For helix, define **fragrance-free** contractually as:

- no `Fragrance`, `Parfum`, aroma composition, masking fragrance, or flavor;
- no essential oil, fragrant hydrosol, aromatic isolate, or botanical added to impart or counteract odor;
- no raw-material blend whose supplier withholds a fragrance component;
- complete disclosure and threshold calculation for naturally present fragrance allergens and reportable constituents; and
- no fragrance claim until the final INCI, quantitative formula, supplier declarations, certificate of analysis/specification, California reportable-ingredient screen, and finished-product sensory review agree.

A raw material may have an inherent odor without being a fragrance ingredient, but “fragrance-free” still must not mislead. For a fast launch, exclude essential oils and intentionally aromatic botanicals even when a supplier assigns them another cosmetic function. Prefer “fragrance-free” to “unscented” only after the stricter standard passes.

### 5.3 Korean import and country-of-origin boundary

Imported cosmetics must meet the same FDA requirements as domestic products. FDA may examine or sample an entry and refuse admission when a product appears adulterated or misbranded ([FDA importing cosmetics](https://www.fda.gov/industry/importing-fda-regulated-products/importing-cosmetics); [FDA import basics](https://www.fda.gov/industry/import-program-food-and-drug-administration-fda/import-basics)). The entry file should reconcile exact manufacturer/site, importer/consignee, product description, formula/category, label, country of origin, and any facility/listing identifiers relied upon.

FDA's current [Import Alert 66-41](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cms_ia/importalert_190.html) is an enforcement signal—not a new classification statute—and targets products presented as cosmetics but bearing unapproved drug claims. Border copy, website screenshots, supplier inserts, and shipment documents should not say anti-inflammatory, acne, healing, UV protection, cell regeneration, collagen stimulation, whitening/melanin inhibition, or natural microneedling.

Country-of-origin marking is a separate Customs obligation. [19 U.S.C. § 1304](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A19+section%3A1304+edition%3Aprelim%29) and [19 C.F.R. §§ 134.11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-134/section-134.11), [134.41](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-134/section-134.41) generally require conspicuous, legible, sufficiently permanent marking for the ultimate purchaser. Have the broker/counsel approve “Made in Korea,” exact placement, container treatment, and any exception; FDA registration does not answer Customs marking.

## 6. California launch boundary

California adds four distinct questions. Keep separate files and conclusions for each:

| California layer | What it does | What it does **not** mean |
|---|---|---|
| **Ingredient sale prohibition** | Bars intentionally added PFAS and listed ingredients; sets a 2027 musk-ketone cap | Passing the list does not establish overall safety or “clean” status |
| **Safe Cosmetics reporting** | Requires the label-name company to report products containing specified chemicals/constituents | A report is not a warning, ban, approval, or finding that the finished product is unsafe |
| **Proposition 65** | Requires a clear and reasonable warning for knowing/intended listed-chemical exposure unless an exemption or exposure defense applies | Presence on an INCI or supplier list does not by itself answer exposure or warning need |
| **Animal-testing sale restriction** | Bars sale/import-for-profit of certain cosmetics developed/manufactured using post-2020 animal tests by the label manufacturer or suppliers, subject to exceptions | Legal sale does not automatically substantiate an unqualified global “cruelty-free” claim |

CDPH's current [Cosmetic FAQ](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CEH/DFDCS/CDPH%20Document%20Library/FDB/ExportandCosmetic/CosmeticFAQ.pdf) describes registration with the department as a cosmetic manufacturer as voluntary for an ordinary cosmetic. That **official guidance** does not waive California's binding adulteration/misbranding rules, the ingredient prohibitions below, Safe Cosmetics reporting, Proposition 65, animal-testing restriction, or the separate registration obligations triggered by an organic claim.

### 6.1 Binding ingredient prohibitions through Q1 2027

California [Health and Safety Code § 108981.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?article=&chapter=14.&division=104.&lawCode=HSC&part=3.&title=) has prohibited any intentionally added PFAS since 1 January 2025. PFAS is defined broadly as a fluorinated organic chemical with at least one fully fluorinated carbon atom, and “intentionally added” includes functional additions and intentional breakdown products. A supplier statement limited to 13 named PFAS is not enough.

The same chapter's [§ 108980](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=108980.) prohibits the following intentionally added ingredients now:

- dibutyl phthalate (84-74-2) and diethylhexyl phthalate (117-81-7);
- formaldehyde (50-00-0), paraformaldehyde (30525-89-4), methylene glycol (463-57-0), and quaternium-15 (51229-78-8);
- mercury (7439-97-6);
- isobutylparaben (4247-02-3) and isopropylparaben (4191-73-5);
- m-phenylenediamine and salts (108-45-2) and o-phenylenediamine and salts (95-54-5); and
- the 13 PFAS/salts separately enumerated in § 108980, in addition to the broader class ban.

Beginning **1 January 2027**, § 108980 also prohibits intentionally added:

| Group | Prohibited ingredient(s) and CAS number(s) |
|---|---|
| Fragrance / musk | lily aldehyde (lilial), 80-54-6; musk ambrette, 83-66-9; musk tibetene, 145-39-1; musk moskene, 116-66-5; musk xylene, 81-15-2 |
| Solvents / monomers / process chemicals | acetaldehyde, 75-07-0; cyclohexylamine, 108-91-8; styrene, 100-42-5; vinyl acetate, 108-05-4; 2-chloroacetamide, 79-07-2; trisodium nitrilotriacetate, 5064-31-3 |

### 6.2 California Safe Cosmetics reporting

Under [HSC §§ 111791–111793.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?article=3.5.&chapter=7.&division=104.&lawCode=HSC&part=5.&title=), the “manufacturer” is the entity whose name appears on the label. For helix, that likely makes the helix label entity the reporting company even though the serum is made in Korea.

There are two related reporting regimes:

1. **CSCA (§ 111792).** A label-name manufacturer with at least $1 million in worldwide annual aggregate cosmetic sales (based on its most recent tax-year filing) reports each product sold in California that contains a chemical identified by the statute as causing cancer or reproductive toxicity. The statute sets no concentration threshold. Updates are required when reportable ingredients are removed or list status changes.
2. **CFFIRKA (§ 111792.6).** The label-name manufacturer reports designated-list fragrance/flavor ingredients and specified fragrance allergens. The statute contains no parallel $1 million manufacturer exemption. A leave-on fragrance allergen is reportable at or above **0.001% (10 ppm)**; designated fragrance/flavor ingredients are not given that threshold.

CDPH's current [reporting guidance](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CCDPHP/DEODC/OHB/CSCP/Pages/ReportingGuidance.aspx) adds important implementation detail: a fragrance allergen is reportable regardless of its intended function; naturally occurring chemicals that are constituents of an intentionally added ingredient can be reportable; and a product newly introduced after 31 July 2026 containing any of the newly listed 56 fragrance allergens follows the 2026 reporting timeline. The [Cosmetics Companies page](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CCDPHP/DEODC/OHB/CSCP/Pages/Cosmetics-Companies.aspx) states that reporting is continuous for new products and reformulations and links the current Reportable Ingredient List, which CDPH updated in May 2026.

**Launch control:** download and archive the then-current CDPH list on the actual screen date; require CAS, function, source, concentration/range, and allergen constituents for every raw material; have the safety assessor/regulatory owner complete a reportability worksheet; submit any required product record before California sale as the internal deadline. If nothing is reportable, keep the signed no-report rationale and source data. Never promote “not reported,” “California compliant,” or absence from the public database as a safety approval.

### 6.3 Proposition 65 is an exposure analysis, not an ingredient-name lookup

Proposition 65 generally requires a business knowingly and intentionally exposing a person in California to a listed carcinogen or reproductive toxicant to provide a clear and reasonable warning unless an exemption or exposure defense applies. Businesses with fewer than 10 employees are exempt, but employee count can change and retailers/partners may impose contractual requirements ([OEHHA business guidance](https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65/businesses-and-proposition-65); [California Attorney General FAQ](https://www.oag.ca.gov/prop65/faq)).

Presence alone does not decide the outcome. A qualified assessor needs the current Proposition 65 list, exact concentration and impurity data, use amount/frequency, dermal exposure/absorption assumptions, relevant no-significant-risk level or maximum-allowable-dose level, and the burden-of-proof posture. Private enforcement makes unsupported “no warning required” conclusions commercially risky.

**Recommended decision rule:** formulate away from listed chemicals where reasonably possible, then commission and preserve a product-specific exposure assessment. If a warning is actually required, counsel must approve the on-product, online, and catalog treatment. OEHHA's amended short-form regulations took effect 1 January 2025 and give existing users a three-year transition; the new short form identifies at least one chemical ([OEHHA final warning-rule page](https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65/crnr/proposition-65-clear-and-reasonable-warnings-safe-harbor-methods-and-content)). A new Q1 2027 product should use the current format if counsel selects a safe-harbor warning, not rely on an expiring legacy shortcut.

### 6.4 California's animal-testing sale restriction

[California Civil Code § 1834.9.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1834.9.5.) makes it unlawful for the label-name manufacturer to import for profit, sell, or offer in California a cosmetic developed or manufactured using an animal test conducted or contracted by that manufacturer or any direct/indirect ingredient supplier on or after 1 January 2020, subject to narrow governmental, foreign, noncosmetic, and drug exceptions and the statute's pre-2020 product/ingredient provisions. Violations carry a $5,000 fine plus $1,000 per day, and local prosecutors can review testing data relied upon.

Require both ODM candidates to provide a signed, product-specific chain-of-custody declaration covering:

- final product and every raw material, subingredient, supplier, and known upstream supplier;
- any post-1 January 2020 live-vertebrate cosmetic/ingredient test, who conducted/requested it, date, purpose, jurisdiction, and who relied on the data;
- pre-2020 sale/testing basis when relied upon;
- any claimed statutory exception with underlying regulator request and nonanimal-alternative analysis;
- no formula/supplier/site change without advance disclosure; and
- inspection-ready source records, not merely a generic “not tested on animals” logo.

FDA does not require animal testing for a cosmetic and notes that “cruelty-free” and “not tested on animals” have no federal legal definitions and can misleadingly refer only to the finished product while ingredients were tested by others ([FDA cruelty-free claim page](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-claims/cruelty-freenot-tested-animals); [FDA animal-testing Q&A](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/resources-consumers-cosmetics/cosmetics-safety-qa-animal-testing)). Therefore, passing California's sale restriction does not alone prove a broader marketing claim.

## 7. Claims and representation policy

### 7.1 The substantiation rule applies before publication

The FTC's [Advertising Substantiation Policy Statement](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation) requires a reasonable basis for objective express and implied claims before dissemination. The current [Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) explains that health/safety claims generally need competent and reliable scientific evidence, the research must fit the product and claim, and an ingredient study may not substantiate the marketed finished product. “Clinically proven,” “tests prove,” and quantified claims expressly promise a level and type of evidence that helix must actually possess.

California independently prohibits untrue or misleading advertising, including internet advertising, when the speaker knew or with reasonable care should have known it was misleading ([Business and Professions Code §§ 17200](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17200.), [17500](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17500.)). The rule reaches PDPs, cartons, product pages, paid ads, email/SMS, founder statements, retail training, influencer briefs, reposted testimonials, FAQs, and visuals.

### 7.3 Evidence ladder for final copy

| Claim type | Minimum recommended evidence before publication | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Ingredient identity/concentration | Approved master formula, raw-material specification, CoA, and stable assay where concentration is stated | Marketing concentration counts a supplier blend rather than the named active |
| Sensory/texture (“lightweight,” “non-greasy,” “glow”) | Controlled finished-product sensory or consumer-use data using the production-equivalent package | Founder/supplier opinion stated as universal fact |
| Hydration / TEWL / visible fine lines / tone | Finished-product protocol with appropriate instrument or validated grading, controls/baseline, population, timepoints, statistics, and full report | Branded-ingredient in-vitro data or altered-base results presented as finished-product clinical proof |
| “Clinically tested” | Human study of the exact finished formula relevant to the words used | Patch testing is presented as efficacy testing |
| “Clinically proven,” percentage, “in 4 weeks” | High-quality finished-product study specifically proving the claim, with pre-specified endpoint and transparent denominator/statistics | Selecting only a favorable secondary endpoint, or quoting participants instead of responders |
| Sensitive-skin / non-irritating / dermatologist-tested | Finished-product tolerance protocol in the claimed population, adverse-reaction accounting, assessor credentials, and precise scope | Small HRIPT generalized to every user, condition, and combination |
| Before/after imagery | Standardized lighting, angle, exposure, expression, application, timing, no retouching; representative result and documented consent | Exceptional result or changed lighting implies typical efficacy |

No claim goes live until the claims register identifies exact wording, medium, implied message, owner, evidence file/version, tested formula/batch/package, population, endpoint, qualification, approval, and expiry/review trigger.

## 8. Fragrance-free, vegan, cruelty-free, clean, and environmental positions

### 8.1 Recommended launch policy

| Representation | Legal/status boundary | Q1 2027 recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Fragrance-free** | No federal cosmetic definition; must be truthful and not misleading. California can require reporting of allergen constituents even when their purpose is not fragrance. | **Use only after the strict helix formula standard in § 5.2 passes.** This aligns with the user's sensory brief and reduces reporting/sensitization complexity, though it does not mean allergen-free. |
| **Vegan / vegan formula** | No FDA cosmetic certification or preapproval. | **Cruelty-free / not tested on animals** | No federal definition; California has a binding but narrower sale restriction. | **Clean** | No FDA definition or approval; context can imply safer, non-toxic, or environmentally superior. Retailers define it differently. | **Omit as a free-standing consumer claim.** Publish a precise formulation standard or specific “made without” facts only when relevant and substantiated. |
| **Natural** | FDA has not defined “natural” for cosmetics; natural origin does not mean safe. | **Omit.** The active-forward formula and necessary preservation make it low-value and high-ambiguity. |

| **Organic / made with organic** | FDA does not define organic for cosmetics; USDA rules can apply to eligible agricultural claims. California permits cosmetic organic claims only under its thresholds/controls. | **Do not pursue for this launch.** California says a cosmetic needs at least 70% organic content excluding water/salt for “organic”/“made with organic,” with organic-handler registration implications. |

### 8.2 Vegan evidence specification

The ODM's “vegan” checkbox is not enough. Require source and process declarations for the finished product and each ingredient, carrier, solvent, processing aid, culture medium, encapsulant, coating, and subingredient. At minimum resolve:

- PDRN/Sodium DNA (fish/salmon, plant, microbial, or other source);
- collagen, elastin, gelatin, milk proteins, silk, honey/propolis/beeswax, lanolin, carmine, shellac, cholesterol, and keratin;
- glycerin, stearates/fatty acids, squalene/squalane, ceramides, hyaluronic acid, amino acids, and peptides whose source can vary;
- fermentation media, peptones, enzyme processing aids, and animal-derived carriers not visible in the marketing INCI; and
- shared-equipment and change-control facts if the selected claim or certifier addresses cross-contact.

Define the claim scope explicitly. “Vegan formula” ordinarily addresses product composition, not animal testing or environmental impact. It must not be used as a substitute for “cruelty-free.” If packaging adhesives/inks are outside scope, do not say “100% vegan product and packaging.”

### 8.3 Clean and “free-of” discipline

FTC's current [Green Guides summary](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/environmental-claims-summary-green-guides) explains that unqualified general environmental-benefit claims are difficult to substantiate and that a “free-of” claim can deceive when the substance was not associated with the product or a replacement poses the same or similar risk. The Guides are interpretations, not an independent clean-beauty statute, but FTC can enforce deceptive advertising under Section 5.

Therefore:

- never say “chemical-free,” “toxin-free,” “free of all harmful ingredients,” “zero contaminants,” or “California approved”;
- do not turn the statutory prohibited list into promotional fear copy;
- use “made without added [specific ingredient/class]” only when consumers could reasonably expect it, the formula and supplier/impurity evidence support it, and the substitute does not defeat the message;
- qualify analytical absence claims with the scope/method where material; and
- keep packaging recyclability, recycled content, refillability, degradability, and lifecycle claims in the separate [U.S./California packaging constraints](./us-california-cosmetic-packaging-constraints.md) workstream.

California's [organic-program guidance](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CEH/DFDCS/Pages/FDBPrograms/FoodSafetyProgram/OrganicFoodProcessors.aspx) states that cosmetic/personal-care products sold as “organic” or “made with organic” need at least 70% organic content excluding water and salt and that businesses handling products sold as organic have registration obligations. Avoid the words in brand copy and ingredient callouts unless a dedicated certification/registration analysis approves them.

## 9. Enforcement-risk register is not a second set of product standards

| Risk signal | Why it matters operationally | Control |

## 11. Safety and testing boundary

FDA says neither the statute nor its regulations prescribe a universal test battery for every cosmetic, but the RP must have adequate, scientifically robust substantiation and the product must be safe under labeled and customary conditions ([FDA MoCRA page](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/modernization-cosmetics-regulation-act-2022-mocra); [FDA product testing](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-science-research/product-testing-cosmetics)). The following is therefore a **recommended evidence architecture**, not a claim that each named method is independently mandated by law:

1. **Formula identity:** exact quantitative formula to 100%, INCI/common-name mapping, functions, raw-material trade names, complete subingredients, origin, specifications, CoAs, SDS/TDS, impurity/contaminant profile, prohibited/restricted-ingredient screen, and change history.
2. **Ingredient and exposure safety:** toxicology review at actual use concentration, foreseeable cumulative exposure where relevant, target population, face/eye proximity, frequency/amount, impurities, interactions, phototoxicity/photosensitization where relevant, and margin/rationale documented by a qualified assessor.
3. **Microbiological quality and preservation:** production-equivalent finished-product limits, preservative-efficacy/challenge evidence appropriate to the water activity/package/use, objectionable-organism controls, validated water/environment program, and batch release limits.
4. **Stability and package compatibility:** real-time and justified accelerated/temperature-cycle/light/freeze-thaw observations; active/preservative assay where relevant; pH, viscosity, appearance/odor, microbiology; pump/dropper dose and seal; adsorption, leaching, corrosion, evaporation, and transport behavior in the final decorated package.
5. **Human tolerance:** protocol selected by the assessor for the exact leave-on formula, with adverse reactions and exclusions reported; add use-condition/sensitive-population work only for claims actually intended. A patch/HRIPT result is not efficacy proof and does not establish no irritation for all users.
6. **Claim efficacy:** separate finished-product studies mapped to each public claim, as set out in § 7.3. Instrumental hydration or TEWL data do not automatically prove wrinkle, sensitive-skin, or long-term barrier claims.
7. **Production bridge:** first commercial/validation lots shown equivalent to the tested formula/process/package, with specifications, release results, retains, lot traceability, deviations, and change-control triggers.

Any meaningful change to active level, preservation, pH, solvent/humectant system, botanical, supplier, manufacturing process, package contact material, or directions reopens the assessor's bridge decision. “Slightly altered base” is not a scientific category.

### Dated refresh triggers

Refresh the analysis immediately if any of the following occurs before or after launch:

- FDA publishes proposed/final cosmetic GMP or fragrance-allergen labeling rules;
- FDA changes registration/listing guidance, portal data, or renewal handling;
- California amends HSC §§ 108980–108982, its CSCP Reportable Ingredient List, fragrance-allergen timelines, or Proposition 65 list/safe-harbor levels;
- formula, supplier, subingredient, active level, process/site, package contact material, directions, claim, business label entity, or ODM changes;
- a retailer/badge is selected;
- a serious or unexpected adverse-event pattern appears; or
- helix's sales/employee facts change an exemption analysis.

## 14. Board decisions enabled by this boundary

The board can now direct both ODMs on equal terms:

1. **Cosmetic-only claim envelope:** approve the appearance/sensory territory in § 3.3 and prohibit the mechanisms in § 3.2/§ 7.2.
2. **Formula eligibility:** reject any base that cannot disclose a 100% quantitative/subingredient formula, pass the 1 January 2027 California screen, support a strict fragrance-free standard, or provide an auditable animal-testing/source chain.
3. **Spicule decision:** exclude Ecoment concepts whose differentiator is natural microneedling or increased living-skin delivery from this lean serum brief.
4. **Haramoon adaptation:** evaluate the Anti-Photoaging Pepti Serum as a base only after replacing “photoaging,” UV repair, collagen synthesis, inflammation, regeneration, whitening/melanin, and inherited clinical language with the cosmetic claim/evidence framework above.
5. **Ethical claims:** make vegan conditional on source evidence; treat California animal-testing compliance as a mandatory legal file and cruelty-free as a separate optional marketing standard.
6. **Clean positioning:** express helix's standard as a controlled ingredient policy and transparent specific facts, not a vague “non-toxic clean serum” promise.
7. **Fast-launch burden:** authorize helix to own RP/claims/filing/postmarket systems and require the ODM to supply site/formula/quality/evidence data contractually before deposit or formula lock.

With those decisions, neither supplier wins because it offers the most dramatic ingredient story. It wins by delivering the strongest compliant formula, sensory fit, finished-product evidence, change control, and launch-ready records.
