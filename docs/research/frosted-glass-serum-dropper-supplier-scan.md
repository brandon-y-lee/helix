# Frosted-glass serum dropper-system supplier scan

**Research date:** 2026-08-31

**Decision status:** Research-qualified sourcing map; no production approval and no universal-cheapest conclusion

**Scope:** Primary packaging for the Helix serum Mini and Full variants

**Public-source boundary:** Supplier websites, catalogs, technical sheets, sustainability reports, and published catalog prices only; no supplier outreach or private material

## Decision in one page

Helix should not award this package from public catalog evidence. No screened supplier publicly proves, in one repeatable stock configuration, all of the target attributes—10 mL and 30 mL, coordinated frosted-clear and genuinely light-protective amber options, black bulb, silver metal-look collar, glass pipette, optional wiper, launch quantities of roughly 1,000–3,000 units per SKU, exact product-contact materials, filled-product compatibility, delivered Southern California cost, and production-site quality evidence. Consequently, no supplier can yet be called the cheapest **high-quality** source.

The best next move is a controlled two-wave RFQ and sample program, not a single-source commitment:

1. **Virospack, Lumson, APC Packaging, Quadpack, SGD Pharma, and Baralan** form the first technical/commercial wave. They collectively provide the strongest public evidence for a complete dropper system, the target size family, launch-relevant stock or customization, and an identifiable operating company. Their evidence is complementary rather than interchangeable.
2. **Stocksmetic/Berlin Packaging** is a conditional commercial benchmark, not a qualified manufacturing source. Its public European catalog names a silver/black dropper for the Pure 30 mL bottle and establishes a useful price envelope near Helix's launch quantity, but the upstream factories, exact seal/pipette/contact-material specification, optional-wiper compatibility, assembly charge, origin, and U.S. landed cost must be resolved before comparison.
3. **Stoelzle, PGP Glass, Bormioli Luigi, DWK Life Sciences, Gerresheimer, Toly, Samhwa, and ICONS America/ICS** form a scale, specialist, or integrator wave. Each has useful evidence, but none publicly closes the whole target system at launch MOQ.
4. **Queens Packaging, Lansen, and LumLun** remain cost/scale leads rather than finalists. Their published MOQs or factory-identity gaps currently conflict with the 1,000–3,000-unit launch objective.

## 1. What this report does—and does not decide

This report converts the desired Haramoon-adjacent visual language into a sourceable, testable packaging system while preserving design distance:

- frosted clear/translucent or dark amber glass;
- a black squeeze bulb;
- a silver metal-look collar;
- a glass pipette;
- a gasket or other disclosed sealing interface;
- an optional wiper/reducer where formula rheology and evacuation testing support it;
- Mini and Full sizes that read as one family; and
- stock-first economics near 1,000–3,000 primary containers per SKU.

It does **not** approve a supplier, certify a material, establish filled-product compatibility, prove a UV/visible-light barrier, clear third-party design rights, promise a supplier's capacity, or establish a delivered price. All candidates remain below the “Commercial configuration approved” status defined in the [packaging quality and compatibility gates](./packaging-quality-compatibility-supplier-gates.md).

The serum formula is intentionally held constant as an unknown. The comparison assumes an ODM can receive Helix-nominated packaging or transfer bulk to an authorized domestic filler. Actual outside-component acceptance, line compatibility, component delivery terms, and lot-disposition responsibility remain gates under the [ODM/domestic-filler route comparison](./packaging-to-odm-domestic-filler-route-comparison.md).

### 1.1 Evidence states

Every consequential statement in this report uses one of five states:

| Code | Meaning | Procurement treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **V — verified public fact** | Directly observed on a current first-party product page, technical sheet, catalog, corporate filing/report, or displayed public price. It verifies what the source publishes, not independent conformance. | May enter the sourcing record with URL and access date; obtain a controlled document or sample before approval. |
| **S — supplier assertion** | Marketing, quality, performance, sustainability, precision, or capacity claim published by the supplier without configuration-specific Helix validation. | Treat as a question or test hypothesis, never as release evidence. |
| **I — supported inference** | A bounded conclusion from two or more verified facts or from published geometry/material data. | Useful for screening; not sufficient for an award or label claim. |
| **L — lead** | Plausible catalog or commercial route with incomplete legal entity, factory, configuration, or evidence. | Keep outside the finalist score until the named gap closes. |
| **U — unknown** | Not found in the reviewed public evidence or not established for the exact configuration. | Missing is not zero, not compliant, and not qualified. |

Prices and catalog availability were observed on 2026-08-31 and can change. Supplier-hosted certification and sustainability statements are still supplier assertions unless the exact certificate, scope, site, standard, and validity period are obtained.

### 1.2 “Supplier” and “manufacturer” are not interchangeable

A visible brand or distributor is not automatically the component manufacturer. The award record must identify:

1. contracting legal entity and seller of record;
2. bottle-forming site;
3. dropper/closure, bulb, pipette, and wiper manufacturing sites;
4. decorator/coater/metallizer;
5. final assembler and inspection site;
6. any broker, importer of record, and U.S. warehouse; and
7. who controls drawings, molds, tooling, approved substitutions, and change notification.

Virospack, Lumson, SGD Pharma, Stoelzle, PGP Glass, Bormioli Luigi, DWK, and Gerresheimer provide relatively strong public evidence of direct manufacturing. APC, Quadpack, Toly, Samhwa, Baralan, and ICONS provide useful integrated offerings, but exact item/site responsibility still must be mapped. Stocksmetic and LABESP provide commercial evidence without naming every upstream factory and therefore cannot pass the factory-identity gate from public evidence alone.

| Light protection | Formula-led | Formula-led | Formula-led | Formula-led | Frost is appearance, not evidence of wavelength protection. |

US fluid-ounce conversions use 29.5735 mL per US fl oz and are rounded to two decimals for planning. Final declared net contents must follow the applicable U.S./SI presentation and label-area analysis in the [U.S. and California constraints report](./us-california-cosmetic-packaging-constraints.md).

### 2.2 The package is an assembly, not a bottle SKU

Every quote, drawing, sample, test report, purchase order, and approval must identify the exact bill of materials:

```text
bottle + neck finish + inner cap/closure + metal or metal-look overshell
+ bulb + bulb-to-cap retention + gasket/liner/top seal
+ optional wiper/reducer + pipette + pipette tip/finish
+ coating/frost + decoration + assembly method + packout
```

Substituting any one item can change leak resistance, torque, dose, chemical compatibility, residual product, appearance, recycling route, and line performance. “Compatible with 20/400,” “DIN18,” or “18/415” is a screening fact, not a matched-system release.

## 4. Candidate-system dossiers

### 4.1 Virospack: deepest disclosed component integration

**Why it is in wave one.** Virospack publishes a rare breadth of in-house operations: transparent and amber neutral glass, rubber injection, plastic injection, glass-pipette production, decoration, automated assembly, laboratory work, technical data, and analysis certificates. It states annual capacities of roughly 98 million droppers and 20 million bottles; those are **S** capacity claims, not reserved Helix capacity. ## 5. Technical cost-benefit analysis

### 5.1 Glass construction, color, and light protection

“Frosted amber” can describe at least four materially different constructions:

1. amber-colored glass plus acid etch or mechanical frost;
2. amber-colored glass plus translucent coating;
3. clear glass plus amber color coating plus frost effect; or
4. clear glass with a single decorative brown/frost coating.

These constructions differ in spectrum transmission, scratch resistance, formula compatibility, recycling acceptance, decoration yield, and cost. A product photo cannot distinguish them.

The supplier must identify base-glass color and chemistry, colorant/coating layers, process, coating supplier, cure, coating weight or thickness, restricted-substance statement, and whether the coating contacts any product-wetted area. Helix must test the finished decorated pack's wavelength transmission against formula sensitivity. Frost is not a UV/visible-light barrier claim. A dark-looking coating can transmit damaging wavelengths, and some genuine amber glass may still be insufficient for a particular active.

**Cost-benefit implication:** stock clear/frosted glass can reduce cash, lead, and decoration MOQ, but may require an opaque carton or stability mitigation. True amber glass may improve light control and eliminate a decorative color coat, but can constrain premium visual consistency or stock family options. The winner is the least-cost **stable** system, not the darkest photograph.

### 5.2 Molded versus tubular glass

- **Molded glass** supports heavier, premium shapes and robust stock families but generally adds mass and dimensional variation. Heavy walls increase inbound and parcel cost and can increase breakage energy even while feeling premium.
- **Tubular glass** can support smaller capacities, more controlled wall geometry, and lower mass, but may look clinical and may not match a molded Full bottle.

Virospack's likely 10 mL tubular / 30 mL molded split illustrates the Mini-family tradeoff. Before accepting it, Helix should compare filled side-by-side proportions, frost/color match, base thickness, shoulder form, label/print area, and secondary-pack cube.

Published bottle weights demonstrate why mass must be quoted rather than assumed: SGD's examples range from roughly 26 g at 10 mL to 44 g at 30 mL; PGP's 30 mL ROY is 43 g; Stoelzle's 30 mL Elixir is 53 g; Bormioli Luigi describes a 30 mL EcoSkin lightweighting path from 42 g to 29 g. These are different designs and not direct performance equivalents, but the 24 g spread between 29 g and 53 g becomes 24 kg per 1,000 bottles before secondary or tertiary packaging. [SGD Ensiemo](https://www.sgd-pharma.com/glass-dropper-bottles-ensiemo-sgd-pharma) · [PGP brochure](https://www.pgpfirst.com/wp-content/uploads/Final_PGP-Cosmetics-Perfumery-Brochure_27-1-2026.pdf) · [Stoelzle Elixir](https://www.stoelzle.com/beauty/customization/elixir/) · [Bormioli sustainability report](https://www.bormioliluigi.com/file/en/2021_Bilancio_Sostenibilita.pdf)

### 5.3 Neck finish and seal integrity

A supplier's 18/400, 18/415, 20/400, or DIN18 label is not a compatibility certificate. For each exact bottle/closure combination, obtain:

- controlled bottle and closure drawings with thread profile, neck land, E/T/H dimensions, tolerances, and revision;
- sealing mode: top seal, plug seal, gasket/liner, wiper contact, or combination;
- gasket/liner material, grade, thickness, hardness, compression target, and supplier;
- closure resin/grade, metal overshell construction, and anti-rotation method;
- application and removal torque windows at assembly and after aging;
- bulb retention and pipette retention/pull-force specifications;
- pipette material, outer/inner diameter, length, tip geometry, and bottom clearance;
- wiper/reducer geometry, retention force, orifice, and insertion method;
- overflow capacity and filled headspace including pipette displacement; and
- leak-test method, orientation, temperature, pressure/vacuum, duration, sample size, and acceptance rule.

**Cost-benefit implication:** mixing apparently standard stock components can reduce initial unit price but transfer fit and leak risk to Helix. A slightly higher matched-system quote with one drawing owner and one change-control owner can have lower expected landed-good cost.

### 5.4 Bulb and product-contact materials

Public options include nitrile/NBR, butyl/IIR, TPE/TPR, silicone, natural rubber, PP/HDPE/LDPE, glass, and coatings. Names alone are insufficient because formulations, fillers, plasticizers, extractables, hardness, cure, pigments, and supplier grades differ.

The product-contact declaration must include every wetted or potentially wetted material: pipette, bulb interior if product can reach it, gasket, liner, wiper, closure plug, adhesive, ink/coating edge, and any lubricant or processing aid relevant to use. Ask for resin/elastomer trade name and grade, color masterbatch, food/cosmetic/pharma declarations where applicable, REACH/SVHC and Proposition 65 statements, heavy metals, latex status, PFAS disclosure policy, change control, and retained-sample traceability.

NBR can support a black luxury bulb and appears in Virospack, APC, Quadpack alternatives, Lumson, SGD, and Baralan literature. That recurrence is useful sourcing evidence, not universal compatibility. The exact serum must be aged in the production-equivalent black compound.

### 5.5 Dose, repeatability, and consumer use

A nominal aspiration volume is not the labeled dose. It can vary with squeeze force, bulb recovery, pipette wetting, formula viscosity/surface tension, headspace, bottle angle, wiper drag, fill level, and consumer technique.

Public anchors include Virospack's approximate 0.35/0.40/0.65 mL assemblies, Lumson's 0.60 mL DELUXE dropper and broader 0.13–0.75 mL range, and SGD's 0.7/1.0 mL bulb sizes. These values are not directly equivalent: some describe nominal aspiration, some bulb capacity, and none proves delivered Helix dose.

For scale only, ideal division implies the following maximum full aspirations before residual, overfill, and loss:

| Nominal aspiration | 10 mL | 15 mL | 30 mL | 50 mL |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.35 mL | 28.6 | 42.9 | 85.7 | 142.9 |
| 0.40 mL | 25.0 | 37.5 | 75.0 | 125.0 |
| 0.60 mL | 16.7 | 25.0 | 50.0 | 83.3 |
| 0.65 mL | 15.4 | 23.1 | 46.2 | 76.9 |

These are arithmetic illustrations, not claims about usable doses. A 50 mL fallback can lengthen post-opening use materially, so formula stability and contamination/use behavior must justify it.

The bench study should weigh delivered mass at the beginning, middle, and end of pack life; include multiple assemblies and lots; standardize squeeze/hold/release timing; test vertical and foreseeable angled use; record first-prime behavior, single-actuation mean, standard deviation/CV, drip/stringing, bulb recovery, tip wipe, and consumer force; and repeat after temperature cycling and aging. Product development must set the target dose and acceptance limits before supplier comparison.

### 5.6 Wiper/reducer and residual product

A wiper can reduce mess and control excess serum on the pipette, but it can also increase actuation force, scrape decoration or glass particles if poorly designed, trap product, raise residual, complicate assembly, and add material/cost. Its value depends on viscosity and pipette geometry.

Virospack and Quadpack publicly offer wipers on portions of their ranges; APC's reviewed pages do not establish one for the target systems; Stocksmetic prices a separate 3 mm reducer. Each candidate should quote with and without the part, followed by measured evacuation:

```text
evacuation yield (%) = dispensed product mass / initial usable product mass × 100
residual fraction (%) = non-dispensable product mass / initial usable product mass × 100
```

An older Virospack article uses the supplier's own phrase “restitution under 0.4%” for a Wide “last drop” design. The publication does not establish, in the evidence reviewed here, whether that term means residual fraction, loss, another denominator, or a translated metric. It is therefore **S + U** and is not entered as either evacuation yield or residual until Virospack supplies its definition, method, raw data, configuration, and exact-formula replication. [2016 article](https://virospack.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/ARTICULO-COSMMA-MAGAZINE-mayo-2016.pdf)

### 5.7 Decoration and tooling

The target should begin with stock round geometry and supplier-standard necks. Quote decoration as separate adders:

- bare clear/amber glass;
- mechanical/acid frost if available;
- spray frost or translucent color coat;
- one-color and two-color screen print;
- label-ready untreated or pretreated surface;
- hot stamp or metallization only where required; and
- silver closure as actual aluminum overshell versus metallized/painted plastic.

Require decoration setup, plate/screen/tooling, color-match, line-trial, scrap, overrun, under-run, rework, and reorder terms. A low component quote with a 10,000-unit decoration minimum does not satisfy the 1,000–3,000 launch objective.

### 6.2 Comparable landed-good cost

For this primary-packaging report, the first comparable denominator is usable empty assemblies received at the named fill site—not ordered pieces:

```text
P1 landed-empty cost per received usable assembly
= (bottles + droppers + wipers + decoration + assembly
   + samples + tooling allocation + inspection + inbound protective packout
   + origin freight + export/import fees + duty/tariff + brokerage
   + domestic freight + insurance + receiving
   + expected inbound damage/reject/replacement)
  / usable empty assemblies received at the named ODM or domestic fill site
```

Use supplier-specific quantities when case packs differ, but normalize consumption to exactly 1,000, 2,000, and 3,000 received usable assemblies per SKU. Record paid excess inventory rather than hiding it in a nominal unit price. Separate one-time and recurring costs. P2 then adds filling, assembly/changeover, validated process loss, release, and final packout. P3 adds finished-goods freight, transport packaging, customs where applicable, and receipt at the named Southern California 3PL/warehouse. The cross-supplier winner metric remains qualified P3; this report cannot calculate it from primary-pack public evidence alone.

At quote stage, rank only after common hard gates pass. Use this decision model:

| Category | Weight / treatment | Required evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Legal/factory identity, safety/compliance, exact-formula compatibility, fill-line acceptance, quality dossier, and rights/provenance | **Hard gates; pass/fail** | Controlled documents, production-equivalent samples, test results, signed representations, and named sites. |
| P3 fulfillment-ready landed cost | **45 points** | Comparable E3 written quotes on identical Incoterm, destination, quantity, validity, BOM, fill/conversion, finished-goods freight, transport packaging, and Southern California receipt. |
| Functional quality and dose/use performance above the minimum gate | 20 points | Exact-configuration results: leak, torque, dose, residual, breakage, decoration, aging, and line yield. |
| Supplier reliability and lead time | **10 points** | Written allocation/lead plan, continuity and change-control terms, dual-source/site disclosure, and logistics evidence. |
| Aesthetic/family fit and design-rights distance | 10 points | Production-equivalent decorated samples and legal/design review. |
| Evidence-adjusted sustainability | **15 points** | Exact component mass/BOM, yield, recycled-content chain of custody, process/site energy evidence, freight, recovery route, and score per the [sustainability rubric](./packaging-sustainability-scoring.md). |

Cost remains the largest weighted objective after quality and compliance gates, while sustainability remains a material 15% objective. Missing evidence receives the rubric's uncertainty penalty; glass, PCR, “recyclable,” local warehouse, or lightweighting language never receives automatic credit.

### 6.3 Required quote normalization

Every finalist quote must state:

- legal seller, currency, quote date/validity, Incoterm, named ship-from and deliver-to points;
- manufacturer/site for every component and decoration/assembly step;
- exact part number, drawing revision, BOM/material/grade/color, dimensions, mass, neck, brim/overflow, pipette and wiper;
- 1k, 2k, and 3k **per SKU** tiers, plus whether colors/sizes can aggregate;
- case pack, pallet pattern, minimum order, minimum spend, paid overrun/underrun, sample cost;
- stock reservation, production lead, decoration lead, assembly lead, transit, and reorder lead;
- tooling, plates/screens, color matching, preproduction sample, testing, and amortization;
- assembly, cleaning, inspection/AQL, protective packout, labels, and documentation;
- freight quote with packed dimensions, weight/cube, mode, surcharges, validity, and insurance;
- HTS rationale, country of origin by component, expected entry date, duties/additional duties, and broker status;
- payment events and timing, not just percentages; and
- reject/replacement, breakage, warranty, substitution, discontinuation, and change-notification terms.

No cost cell may be blank in the comparison. Use zero only when the written quote expressly includes or waives it; otherwise use U and stop the comparison.

## 7. Quality, factory, logistics, and sustainability

### 7.1 Public quality evidence is only the beginning

Virospack publishes ISO 9001/14001/45001, EMAS, EcoVadis, and CDP statements; Lumson publishes ISO history and inspection practices; PGP identifies plant-level standards including ISO 15378; Samhwa publishes certification images; Baralan and other global providers publish company quality systems. These are useful screening signals. They do not prove that the exact bottle, bulb, pipette, wiper, coating, assembly line, or subcontractor is within a valid certificate's scope. [Virospack](https://virospack.com/es/inicio/) · [Lumson](https://lumson.com/en/company/) · [PGP infrastructure](https://www.pgpfirst.com/infrastructure/) · [Samhwa certifications](https://www.samhwakorea.com/eng/company/certifications/index.jsp) · [Baralan company profile](https://www.baralan.com/wp-content/uploads/BARALAN_CompanyProfile_EN.pdf)

The supplier dossier must include controlled certificate copies and scope, organization chart, site list, process map, quality manual summary, calibration, incoming/in-process/final controls, AQL/defect taxonomy, Cpk or dimensional capability where relevant, mold/cavity traceability, lot coding, retained samples, nonconformance/CAPA, change control, business continuity, complaint/recall process, and sub-supplier controls.

### 7.2 Release ladder

Use the shared quality report's stages, names, and order without collapsing them:

0. **Identity screen:** verify legal entity, actual sites, process map, subcontractors, ownership, and relevant adverse facts; decide whether to admit the supplier to document review.
1. **Document prequalification:** review site-scoped QMS evidence, complete component dossier, drawings/specifications, traceability, change control, defect/CAPA process, capacity, and continuity.
2. **Empty-package qualification:** test production-equivalent empty assemblies for dimensions, function, leakage, dose mechanics, evacuation, decoration, glass, secondary packaging, and risk-selected transport conditions.
3. **Filler and pilot qualification:** qualify the nominated line, settings, coding, controls, rejects, reconciliation, yield, line trial, and production-equivalent packout.
4. **Formula-specific validation:** validate the exact Formula, every saleable size, full BOM/decoration, compatibility/stability/microbiology program, line trial, and finished-pack transit; the decision approves or rejects the exact commercial configuration.
5. **Lot release and surveillance:** disposition each actual lot using the approved sample plan, inspection/test record, traceability, deviations, retains, and supplier performance evidence.

The public scan creates research leads before formal Stage 0 disposition. No candidate has passed Stage 0 from this report alone.

### 7.3 Logistics and breakage

The delivered-good comparison must use actual packed mass and cube. Ask each supplier for palletized case dimensions, gross/net weight, glass orientation, dividers, layer pads, pallet height, stack limit, desiccant/temperature controls if relevant, and historical transit-damage method/data for the proposed lane.

Glass-specific sample work should include cosmetic-defect maps; critical crack/chip criteria; thermal shock where relevant; vertical compression/top-load; filled leak under side/inverted storage; pipette impact and pull; bulb/collar separation; closure back-off; decorated abrasion; and final DTC/inbound transport validation. The secondary and tertiary package cannot be finalized independently of the selected bottle's mass, diameter, shoulder, and dropper height.

APC's U.S. warehouse may reduce launch replenishment lead and import coordination, but it does not prove lower P3 cost or eliminate upstream China risk. Virospack/Lumson/Baralan European production can improve integrated-system accountability but may increase freight/working capital at launch. Asian factory-direct routes may lower ex-works cost at scale but often carry higher MOQ, longer transit, larger safety stock, and more coordination. These are **I** hypotheses to price, not geographic rules.

### 7.4 Sustainability evidence

Apply the project's 15-point evidence-adjusted rubric to the exact quoted configuration. At minimum collect:

- component-level mass for bottle, collar, closure, bulb, gasket, wiper, pipette, coating/ink, and protective packout;
- base glass type/color and cullet/PCR percentage with chain of custody and accounting method;
- resin/elastomer grade and PCR/renewable content, if any;
- metal type and mass, and whether the overshell can be separated in ordinary recovery;
- manufacturing and decoration site energy/emissions data allocated to the actual process where available;
- scrap/reject/yield and whether cullet/process scrap is closed-loop;
- origin, route, shipment mode, case/pallet cube, and damage/replacement rate;
- recovery guidance for the complete consumer assembly in target U.S. markets; and
- evidence date, scope, assurance, certificate number, and exclusions.

Glass is not automatically the sustainable winner. A heavy multi-material dropper with an aluminum shell, rubber bulb, PP closure, glass pipette, coating, and wiper may be difficult to separate and can create more freight and breakage than a lighter alternative. Conversely, durable inert glass, high recovered-content feedstock, effective recovery, lightweighting, good yield, and low-damage regional supply can score well. Company-level EcoVadis, ISO 14001, “PCR available,” or “100% recyclable glass” statements do not establish the exact SKU score.

The most concrete public lightweighting evidence in this scan is bottle-level: Stoelzle calls its 30 mL Elixir 32% lighter than a standard comparator, and Bormioli Luigi describes a 42 g-to-29 g 30 mL EcoSkin path. Both are supplier claims/design data and require like-for-like performance testing and exact quoted mass. [Stoelzle Elixir](https://www.stoelzle.com/beauty/customization/elixir/) · [Bormioli report](https://www.bormioliluigi.com/file/en/2021_Bilancio_Sostenibilita.pdf)

## 8. Design-rights and visual-distance screen

The reference image contributes a broad rounded-square amber/frosted slab, low shoulders, an oversized silver collar, black bulb, and dense technical graphic treatment. Individual common features—amber glass, frosted finish, dropper, silver collar, black bulb—can exist in the category, but a close overall combination of proportions, silhouette, finish, closure scale, and graphics can create avoidable risk. Follow the [reference-packaging design-rights screen](./reference-packaging-design-rights-screen.md).

### 8.1 Lower-distance direction

- stock round Boston, cylindrical, or softly shouldered bottle rather than a rounded-square slab;
- standard-sized collar proportion rather than an oversized block;
- Helix-specific spacing, typography, hierarchy, and ingredient language;
- distinct bottle-to-closure proportion across both Mini and Full;
- no imitation of Haramoon's dense boxed technical grid, line breaks, or placement; and
- documented independent design chronology and source provenance.

### 8.2 Candidate implications

| Configuration direction | Provisional distance burden | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Virospack Slim/Wide round, Lumson DELUXE round, APC Boston round, Baralan Olivia round, SGD round | **I:** lower, subject to finished design | Category-standard round geometry creates clearer silhouette distance. |
| Quadpack Charismo 10/30 square | **I:** higher | Exact paired square geometry can reproduce more of the reference's overall massing even with different graphics. |
| Toly or other custom case-inspired package | **I:** medium to high until provenance reviewed | Aesthetic capability is strong, but custom references can carry third-party/tooling provenance questions. |
| Bespoke rounded-square heavy glass with oversized silver collar | **I:** highest and unnecessary for launch | Adds tooling/MOQ/cost while moving closer to the reference. |

Before award, obtain warranties covering tool ownership/right to sell, non-infringing supplier design, authorized decoration files, no unauthorized overruns, confidentiality, return/destruction of Helix tools/artwork, and disclosure of customer-exclusive components. Stock-catalog presence is not design-rights clearance.

### 9.1 Quote configurations

Ask the first wave to quote the following as separate, factory-matched assemblies:

| Code | Bottle | Finish/color | Dropper | Quantity tiers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M10-C | 10 mL Mini | frosted clear/translucent | black bulb, silver metal-look collar, glass pipette; wiper priced optional | 1k / 2k / 3k |
| M10-A | 10 mL Mini | true amber glass plus frost option, or fully disclosed amber coating | same | 1k / 2k / 3k |
| M15-C | 15 mL Mini fallback | frosted clear/translucent | same | 1k / 2k / 3k |
| M15-A | 15 mL Mini fallback | amber | same | 1k / 2k / 3k |
| F30-C | 30 mL Full | frosted clear/translucent | same; quote at target delivered-dose choices | 1k / 2k / 3k |
| F30-A | 30 mL Full | amber | same | 1k / 2k / 3k |
| F50-C/A | 50 mL fallback | both finishes | same | budgetary only until dose/stability gate authorizes |

For each code, require a no-decoration stock price and separate one-color, two-color, label-ready, and custom-coating prices. If the black bulb or silver shell forces a custom MOQ, quote the nearest stock alternative and state the aesthetic delta rather than silently substituting.

### 9.2 Supplier questionnaire

The RFQ response should answer, with documents where requested:

1. Who is the legal seller and each actual factory/assembler/decorator?
2. Is each component stock, made to order from an existing tool, or new tooling?
3. What customer exclusivity, mold ownership, territory, and resale rights attach to it?
4. What are the exact drawing revisions and BOM/material/grade/color codes?
5. Is amber integral glass or coating? Is frost etched, mechanical, sprayed, lacquered, or another process?
6. What wavelengths/transmission data exist for the finished pack?
7. What seal system, gasket/liner, torque window, leak method, and closure-retention data apply?
8. What bulb material/grade/hardness, pipette geometry, aspiration/delivery data, and wiper options apply?
9. What decoration adhesion, abrasion, chemical-resistance, and color-tolerance methods apply?
10. What are MOQ, minimum spend, case pack, color aggregation, paid overrun, tooling, samples, and reorder terms?
11. What production, decoration, assembly, QC, and transit leads apply at 1k/2k/3k?
12. What exact-site certificates, AQLs, defect limits, capability data, lot traceability, change control, and CAPA terms apply?
13. What component masses, recycled-content evidence, energy/site data, yield, origin, and recovery guidance apply?
14. What fill-line equipment, chuck/capper, torque, insertion, cleaning, and feeding constraints apply?
15. What freight pack, pallet, Incoterm, HTS/origin, duty, insurance, breakage, and replacement terms apply?

### 9.3 Sample and qualification sequence

**Stage 0 — identity screen.** Verify the contracting entity, ownership, actual factories/assemblers/decorators, process map, named subcontractors, and relevant adverse facts. Reject or hold suppliers that will not disclose the chain; document review has not yet occurred at this stage.

**Stage 1 — document prequalification.** Review controlled drawings/BOM, specifications, site-scoped quality evidence, traceability, change control, defect/CAPA process, capacity/continuity evidence, and tooling/design provenance. Only document-prequalified configurations proceed to sampling.

**Stage 2 — empty-package qualification.** Inspect production-equivalent samples from at least two lots where practicable. Measure bottle/neck/pipette/wiper critical dimensions, overflow capacity, component mass, visual defects, color/frost match, collar alignment, torque, pull forces, empty/simulant leak and dose mechanics, evacuation, decoration abrasion, glass performance, and risk-selected empty-pack transport. Use actual case-pack transit samples, not hand-carried showroom samples alone.

**Stage 3 — filler and pilot qualification.** Fill, cap, code, reconcile, and assemble a production-representative pilot on the nominated line using the intended settings, controls, and packout. Assess feeding, pipette insertion, wiper seating, torque, glass damage, throughput, rejects, yield, leakage, priming, dose repeatability, residual, cleanability, and line-specific changeover.

**Stage 4 — Formula-specific validation and commercial-configuration decision.** Use the exact Formula, every saleable size, production-equivalent BOM/final decoration, and the qualified line/packout. Evaluate appearance/odor, stability-supporting parameters, weight loss, elastomer/coating/ink/adhesive change, torque drift, leakage, microbiology and extractables/leachables risk as determined by quality, dose, residual, and finished-pack inbound/DTC transport over the intended shelf/use period. Freeze the approved specification, golden samples, quality agreement, change control, methods, AQL/defects, incoming plan, route, and lot-release authority only after this evidence passes.

**Stage 5 — lot release and surveillance.** Inspect and disposition the actual first production lot under the approved plan, with heightened inspection where specified. Preserve traceability, retains, deviations, corrective action, and supplier-scorecard surveillance; any later reduction in inspection requires evidence.

No supplier sample “passes” for all formulas, colors, sizes, lots, sites, or routes. Approval is exact-configuration specific.

## 10. Shortlist, stop conditions, and recommendation

### 10.1 First-wave shortlist

| Candidate | Use in RFQ | What could make it the best value | Immediate stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Virospack | Integrated 10/15/30/50 technical proposal | One accountable BOM, internal manufacturing/lab, matched family, acceptable custom-MOQ exception | Cannot quote black/silver and two colorways near launch quantity; Mini/Full visual mismatch; factory/site or contact materials withheld. |
| APC | U.S.-stock 15/30 control and cross-compatible target proposal | Case-stock access, fast U.S. replenishment, low tooling/cash | Treats separate SHAM/ZHBR parts as interchangeable without matched drawing/testing; cannot disclose exact factory/certs. |
| Lumson | DELUXE 10/15/30 beauty/dose proposal | Exact Mini/Full family, disclosed nominal dose/material architecture, integrated Italian droppers | No black/silver or amber/frost route; MOQ/decoration economics outside launch range. |
| Quadpack | Skin-Up 15/30/50 and round 10 alternative | Premium complete system, optional wiper, broad material choices | Only 10 mL option is high-risk square; upstream factories or item quality evidence withheld. |
| SGD Pharma | 10/15/30 clear/amber functional control | Most complete amber/clear functional BOM and strong dimensional evidence | Cannot achieve acceptable cosmetic finish/silver closure near MOQ without losing system responsibility. |
| Baralan | Olivia 15/30/50 Boston-round family | Coherent stock geometry/colors and beauty supply experience | Closure/pipette/wiper system remains undefined or supplier will not own matched assembly. |

Stocksmetic should run beside wave one only as the transparent E1 price control. It becomes a finalist only if Berlin/Stocksmetic discloses actual factories, certifies the exact matched system, supports U.S. delivery, and provides a comparable E3 P3 quote.

### 10.2 Second-wave use

- **Stoelzle, PGP, Bormioli Luigi, Gerresheimer:** complete-bottle cost, weight, origin, and decoration controls, quoted through an accountable dropper-system integrator.
- **DWK:** functional amber assembly and glass-type reference where beauty aesthetics can be relaxed.
- **Toly, Samhwa, ICONS/ICS:** premium integrator alternatives after actual factories and item-level BOMs are disclosed.
- **Queens, Lansen, LumLun:** scale cost checks only if a documented stock exception meets 1k–3k and full factory/quality gates pass.

### 10.3 Stop conditions

Do not advance a configuration when any of the following persists after one documented clarification round:

- legal seller or actual production/decorating/assembly sites undisclosed;
- no controlled drawing or product-contact BOM;
- no change-notification commitment or substitutions permitted without approval;
- no exact black-bulb/silver-collar construction disclosure;
- amber/frost process or finished-pack light performance misrepresented or left ambiguous;
- MOQ is materially above 3,000 per SKU without an economically bounded stock/shared-component path;
- component “compatibility” rests only on a nominal neck code;
- exact formula, fill line, leak/dose/residual, or transport test cannot be supported;
- P3 quote leaves material costs unknown or uses noncomparable terms;
- design/tool provenance, customer exclusivity, or no-overrun protection is refused; or
- sustainability statements cannot be tied to the exact component/site while being used for scoring.

### 10.4 Recommendation

Proceed with the six-supplier first wave and the Stocksmetic price control. Hold the visual target constant, but let suppliers disclose whether it is stock, configured from existing tooling, or custom. Keep **10 mL** as the consumer-led Mini target and **15 mL** as an explicit stock fallback; do not let an easy 15 mL catalog silently replace the 10 mL decision. Keep **30 mL** as Full; defer 50 mL until dose and stability evidence exists.

Select the lowest **P3 landed-good cost** only among exact configurations that pass factory identity, rights, quality, formula compatibility, filler, dose, residual, light, and transport gates. Apply the 15-point sustainability score to the same configuration and evidence set. On today's public evidence, APC is the best launch-stock hypothesis, Virospack the best integrated technical hypothesis, Lumson the best disclosed 10/15/30 beauty-dose hypothesis, SGD the best amber/clear functional reference, Quadpack the strongest broad premium integrator hypothesis, and Stocksmetic the most transparent public price control. None is yet the winner.
