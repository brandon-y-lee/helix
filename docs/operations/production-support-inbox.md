## Read-only readiness packet

Run only against an operator-prepared, private **nonsecret** JSON packet:

```sh
pnpm exec tsx scripts/support-production-readiness.ts --candidate <exact-40-hex-commit> --packet <private-packet.json>
```

This command reads one owned nonsymlink file, maximum 64 KiB, with no group/other permissions. It does not load environment files, invoke Supabase CLI/keychain/authentication, query providers, replay callbacks or change settings. Invalid input errors and the report never echo packet values. Do not put message content, addresses, credentials, tokens, signed URLs or private attachment details in the packet.

The version 1 packet contains `candidateCommit`, a fresh UTC `observedAt`, nonsecret configuration snapshots and the exact claim names in `scripts/support-production/readiness.ts`. Configuration includes explicit project URL/ref and stored identity, dedicated bucket/store binding, a positive 1–16 photo lease limit, credential-presence booleans only, the manual/no-auto-ack policy, and **separate runtime and stored controls**. Preflight expects all controls disabled. Every live observation must be obtained separately in the approved operator context, not guessed from these fields.

Exit 1 means blocked. Exit 2 means the supplied packet is complete enough for operator review, **not a passing live gate**. Even an all-true packet always reports `actualProductionVerified:false` and `activationAuthorized:false`. Persistence, provider handoff, actual Inbox receipt, manual reply, maximum media, attachment hashes, threading, archive restoration, development/provider isolation and human responsibilities each have independent claims. None is inferred from another. A changed candidate, stale snapshot, missing claim, mismatched target/bucket or zero photo capacity blocks the packet. Actual evidence and applicable approvals remain outside this evaluator.

## Human monitoring and prospective safety records

The owner explicitly retains the approved weekday-noon-Pacific reply countdown and confirms primary monitoring through his daily support-email workflow. No backup or preservation-custodian assignment is implied. Record those responsibilities and an escalation/reconciliation cadence before live admission; do not invent coverage. The countdown is not a provider-delivery measurement.

The monitor checks native Inbox/spam and payload-free backlog/uncertainty/capacity signals. Assign recovery ownership for saved-but-not-handed-off work, missing RFC identities/callbacks, rejected/unknown provider outcomes, photo failures and quota/capacity stops. The native primary message does not advertise declared photo count, and rejected/reconciliation photo work does not automatically create another Inbox message. Inbox checking alone is therefore insufficient: qualify payload-free failure/backlog alerts and their actual delivery to the responsible monitor. Provider acceptance does not prove a person can see the Inquiry. Do not delete preserved records or rekey uncertain outboxes to clear a backlog.

For a possible cosmetic safety complaint, preserve earliest receipt/channel, original correspondence, product/lot if available, applicable label copy/version, assigned human and assessment status. Mark missing facts unknown. The responsible person is determined from the product label, not assumed from the website's company name. FDA requires serious US cosmetic adverse-event reports with label copy within 15 business days of responsible-person receipt, with new material medical information received within one year of the initial report followed up within 15 business days of receipt. A human assesses applicability and reporting; no automatic regulatory submission or current incident is assumed. [FDA industry reporting instructions](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-compliance-enforcement/how-report-cosmetic-product-related-complaint).

Records relating to received US cosmetic adverse-event reports are generally maintained for six years; the three-year provision has specific small-business conditions not established here. Preserve pending human review rather than applying an ordinary support closure timer. [21 U.S.C. 364a](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title21/html/USCODE-2024-title21-chap9-subchapVI-sec364a.htm).

Promptly preserve complete relevant messages, later correspondence, labels and actual original/clean attachment bytes at the approved access-restricted destination outside routine development systems. Verify files open, message/attachment counts and hashes match, and a restoration actually works. Keep a payload-free verification ledger; health/image content does not go to Helix AI prompts, public issues, analytics or dev fixtures. Do not enable native Inbox AI features for this manual workflow. Normal provider processing remains governed by its disclosed subprocessors; do not promise absolute absence of provider-side processing.

Resend documents 30-day general email/log retention on Free/Pro/Scale. That is not proof of a native Inbox active/archive exception, indefinite retention, legal hold or complete attachment export. Archive/Trash labels, CSV lists, screenshots and links alone do not establish preservation. Qualify actual retention/export routes and keep independent copies as required; no current noncompliance is inferred. [Resend retention and processing disclosures](https://resend.com/security/gdpr).
