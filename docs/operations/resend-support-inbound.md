# Support receiving and private photos

Ticket #435 / Spec #430 extends [private Support Intake](./resend-support-intake.md) with received email, bounded photo processing, and Operator review. This is source implementation, not hosted activation: the new database migration is unapplied remotely; no receiving address, webhook subscription, Storage bucket, worker schedule, or provider credential has been provisioned by this Ticket. Admission remains off until the R1-10 manifest qualifies the exact deployment and services.

## Identities and configuration

The website origin, email sender, and receiving mailbox are independent. `https://helixskin.vercel.app` is a website origin and cannot serve as Helix's email domain. Resend offers an account-assigned `<id>.resend.app` receiving domain without Helix owning a production domain. R1-10 must discover and qualify the actual account assignment and exact mailbox; neither this example nor the installed MCP connection establishes runtime configuration. A future owned receiving domain needs its own verified receiving/DNS setup. [Resend receiving](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/receiving/introduction).

Use a separately managed receiving credential, qualify its account and privilege in R1-10, and keep the sending credential scoped where supported. ## Receiving, processing, and review

`POST /api/webhooks/resend` verifies the original bounded body and records a durable receiving disposition before success; admitted messages also have durable work queued before acknowledgement. Event identity and received-email identity deduplicate retries. Callback success does not mean content retrieval, acceptance into an Inquiry, or a sent reply. Preserve the signed callback path during outages and domain changes.

The full recipient set, up to 100 addresses, is routed against stored exact addresses. A message reaching multiple known conversation aliases holds every affected Inquiry and is dismiss-only; choosing the first matching alias is forbidden. Oversized callbacks return a non-success response instead of truncating their recipients. Admission permits at most 20 unresolved jobs per known Inquiry and 100 globally. Over-cap signed events receive a durable terminal `rate_limited` receipt and safe audit, without fetching bodies or photos; later capacity or callback replay does not automatically recover that provider email. The service-only `inspect_support_ingress()` returns `pendingJobs`, `rateLimitedEvents`, `ignoredEvents`, and `rfcConflicts` counts without private content. Pending includes failed/quarantined jobs, so Operator review/dismissal is needed alongside worker draining.

`GET` or `POST /api/internal/support-ingest` requires `Authorization: Bearer <HELIX_SUPPORT_INGEST_SECRET>`. The Node route allows 60 seconds; each invocation has a 45-second budget, at most two email jobs and two photo jobs, and one cleanup when time remains. Persisted leases resume interrupted work on a later invocation; there is no post-response background processing. R1-10 must configure and qualify cadence, hosting limits, and backlog/cleanup monitoring. `ok: true` is not proof of an empty backlog; `receivingConfigured` only reports key presence/shape, and `messages` includes jobs finalized into quarantine.

The worker fetches received bodies with `html_format=cid`, bounded to 512 KiB, and keeps at most 10,000 characters of plain text. HTML is converted without loading images, links, or tracking content; truncated or malformed text needs review. Photo-only mail gets an explicit placeholder, not invented customer prose. Provider API authorization failures need configuration repair; temporary failures use bounded durable retry, and failed retrieval remains inspectable rather than silently accepted.

Automatic correlation requires an exact stored recipient route, its opaque conversation token, the fixed participant, and known RFC message history. Resend email IDs, Helix intent IDs, and RFC Message-IDs are separate identities. The worker can reconcile accepted outbound Message-IDs if a reply arrives before a delivery callback. Sender addresses, header claims, and message bodies never authorize Order access or change a reply recipient.

Helix also requires receiver-computed SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all to pass for automatic acceptance. Missing, failed, gray, or unknown results are quarantined, including some legitimate senders without a strict DMARC policy. This is Helix's conservative policy; forged `Authentication-Results` headers cannot satisfy it. Follow the available review actions rather than weakening authentication checks.

Operators review pending/quarantined items in `/admin/support` with `support.read`; accept/dismiss/retry additionally requires `support.reply`, same-origin requests, and the current Inquiry revision. Acceptance is allowed only by the database's safe-review rules. Automated mail and loop evidence cannot be promoted through a more permissive forwarding reason. Pending context holds approval; accepted inbound content and photo state changes advance the revision and invalidate stale approval. After processing or review, refresh the conversation and save/reapprove against the current content. Do not reset leases, histories, or approval revisions to bypass a hold.

Outgoing support attachments remain explicitly `[]`. Incoming photos are Operator-only context and are excluded from AI input in R1; they are not automatically forwarded to customers. Closing an Inquiry remains separate from email delivery.

## Photos, capacity, and private access

R1-10 must provision one private bucket with **exactly 10 MiB** (`10485760`) per-object limit and the exact MIME allowlist `image/jpeg`, `image/png`, `image/webp`. The runtime checks these settings before storage operations. Public access, extra MIME types, a changed size limit, or the wrong project fail closed. This Ticket does not create a bucket or relax Storage policies.

Web intake binds a maximum of five declared slots to a random submission capability; only its SHA-256 digest is stored. A submission or Inquiry UUID alone grants nothing. Upload admission checks that capability and the exact slot, type, and declared size. Guest status/completion access expires 24 hours after batch admission; new token minting has the stricter ten-minute window described below. The batch retains immutable original source/email digests; retries cannot rebind them by changing network or email. Bytes go directly to a unique private quarantine path using a non-upsert signed upload, avoiding the Vercel function request-body limit. The server later reads actual bytes and atomically charges the message budget before decoding.

Each original is at most **10 MiB** and actual admitted originals total at most **20 MiB** per message. Five independently signed 10 MiB slots can temporarily stage **50 MiB** if declared sizes are dishonest. Existing intake limits still apply: five submissions/hour and twenty/day per source, three/hour and ten/day per email digest. A serialized admission check additionally caps outstanding photo batches at **five per original source, five per original email, and 100 globally**. Canceled/consumed slots count through the latest token ceiling plus grace and until raw cleanup acknowledgement. Those active admissions represent up to **250 MiB per source or email, and 5,000 MiB globally** of raw staging; this is not a strict physical Storage ceiling when delayed orphan writes require another sweep. Immutable slots and charged-byte tombstones never become reusable message capacity.

Supabase upload tokens last two hours. Before each signing attempt the database persists a 30-second mint deadline and a monotonic expiry ceiling of now plus two hours/two minutes. A slot permits at most five mint attempts within ten minutes of batch admission, always at the same non-upsert path. Initial cleanup waits until the latest ceiling plus five minutes and the last processing lease plus five minutes; unissued slots expire after ten minutes plus the cleanup grace. Early object deletion could let a still-valid token recreate its path. [Signed uploads](https://supabase.com/docs/reference/javascript/file-buckets-createsigneduploadurl).

The grace interval is not treated as proof that every in-flight Storage write has finished. After successful raw deletion, the retained tombstone schedules the same exact path for another deletion attempt after 24 hours; failures retry after five minutes. The first `raw_deleted_at` remains immutable, ready clean photos are preserved, and per-message slots never become reusable. Already released source/global admission capacity is not charged again by a repeat sweep. This repeat eligibility continues until provider finality/retention is separately qualified. The worker handles at most one cleanup per invocation, so actual revisit cadence depends on scheduling and backlog; R1-10 must test late writes and monitor this repair path as tombstones accumulate.

Provider attachment URLs are fetched afresh through the authenticated attachment API, restricted to the exact documented Resend CDN resource, and never receive the API bearer credential. The downloader follows no redirects, limits actual bytes, and refreshes a CDN `403` URL once within the same worker deadline. Declared MIME and size are hints; actual bytes must still pass processing. [Resend attachments](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/receiving/attachments).

Sharp **0.35.5** is a direct pinned dependency. A child process receives bytes with a fixed minimal environment, fully decodes permitted containers, rejects animation/malformed content, and is killed and awaited at its deadline. Input is limited to 25 million pixels and 10,000 pixels per dimension. One decoder runs per application process, not fleet-wide; its JavaScript heap setting is not a native-memory guarantee or OS sandbox. Raw pixel re-encoding strips metadata and produces immutable WebP of at most **4 MiB** and 2,560 pixels per dimension. Rejected photos preserve accepted message text.

`POST /api/admin/support/{inquiryId}/photos/{photoId}` issues a 60-second access capability bound to the Operator and exact photo. Its `GET` rechecks current `support.read`, Inquiry/photo association, capability validity, and expiry after the bounded read. Responses are private/no-store, same-origin, no-referrer, and `nosniff`. Use this route and the private viewer; never use public Storage URLs or the Next Image optimizer. The 4 MiB output cap fits beneath Vercel's 4.5 MB function response limit. [Vercel limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations).

## Disable, recover, and change domains

- Disable new web photos with `HELIX_SUPPORT_PHOTOS_ENABLED=false`. Keep Storage/project credentials, bucket configuration, access signing secret, and worker available: accepted uploads can still complete/report status, existing jobs can drain and clean up, and authorized Operators can read clean photos.
- Disable new receiving admission using `configure_support_receiving(false, exact_updatedAt)`. Existing durable-event duplicates remain acknowledgeable and queued jobs can drain; new provider deliveries retry while admission is off. Removing the receiving address instead would also break acknowledgement of duplicates.
- Stop new web Inquiries through the existing intake controls. Keep the receiving identity and callback configured for previously published conversation routes. The email dispatch switch controls outbound sends independently; it does not stop receiving or photo processing.
- Repair worker credentials, Storage drift, or provider availability before using bounded retry/review. Keep original event, photo slot, intent, and lease identities. Missing receiving credentials leave email work unclaimed and skip Resend-photo fetches while web uploads and cleanup can drain. Photo expiry still applies; removing credentials does not freeze processing deadlines.
- For a domain migration, qualify receiving DNS/mailbox, signed callbacks, durable admission, and worker scheduling before sending acknowledgements with new opaque Reply-To addresses. Setting `HELIX_SUPPORT_RECEIVING_ADDRESS` supplies new routes independently of the database receiving switch. Version 2 receipts freeze the exact reply address and bounded history headers; version 1 receipts remain readable. Existing prepared requests and stored routes keep their original identity. Continue receiving on old published domains until their obligations are resolved; an environment change does not transfer mailboxes.
